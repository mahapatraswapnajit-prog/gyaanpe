Class 10 English Chapter 13: Poem: How to Tell Wild Animals

NCERT First Flight & Footprints Without Feet · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Carolyn Wells humorously describes how to identify wild animals like the lion, tiger, leopard, bear, hyena, crocodile and chameleon by the dangerous or funny things they do.

Key concepts

🦁The Poem

Carolyn Wells humorously tells how to identify wild animals — usually by the danger they pose!

Example: The Asian lion roars at you as you die; the Bengal tiger has black stripes on yellow.

In real life: Humour makes learning fun.

💡 Did you know? The poem is light-hearted and witty.

🐻Bear and Hyena

The bear hugs you very hard; the hyena comes and laughs at you.

Example: The leopard has spots and leaps on you.

In real life: Wildlife guides describe features.

💡 Did you know? Each animal is identified by its behaviour.

🐊Crocodile and Chameleon

The crocodile weeps as it eats; the chameleon has no ears and changes colour, so it is hard to see.

Example: 'Crocodile tears' means false sorrow.

In real life: Camouflage is used in nature and the army.

💡 Did you know? Chameleons blend into surroundings.

🎵Devices

Rhyme, humour, alliteration and deliberate misspellings to keep rhyme (e.g., 'ignore it' rhymes with 'roar it').

Example: Poetic licence lets poets bend grammar.

In real life: Funny poems are memorable.

💡 Did you know? Light verse aims to amuse.

💭Theme

Humour in danger; observing nature.

Example: The poem warns gently with jokes.

In real life: Respect wild animals' space.

💡 Did you know? Carolyn Wells was an American writer.
