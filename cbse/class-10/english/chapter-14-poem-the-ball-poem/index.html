Class 10 English Chapter 14: Poem: The Ball Poem

NCERT First Flight & Footprints Without Feet · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

In 'The Ball Poem', John Berryman watches a boy lose his ball into the harbour and reflects that the boy must learn the 'epistemology of loss' — how to accept loss and move on.

Key concepts

⚽The Poem

John Berryman shows a boy who loses his ball into the harbour water. The poet does not want to console him with a new ball.

Example: The boy stands rigid, trembling, staring at the water.

In real life: Childhood toys carry memories.

💡 Did you know? It is the boy's first experience of loss.

💡Epistemology of Loss

The boy must learn how to stand up after losing something — the 'epistemology of loss'.

Example: Money is external; it cannot buy back what is truly lost.

In real life: Coping with loss is part of growing up.

💡 Did you know? 'Epistemology' means the study of knowledge.

🧠Responsibility

The poet feels everyone must learn to bear loss on their own.

Example: Buying a new ball would not teach the lesson.

In real life: Resilience is a life skill.

💡 Did you know? Loss teaches maturity.

🎨Devices

Symbolism (ball = childhood, possessions), imagery of the harbour, free verse.

Example: The ball bouncing into the water symbolises passing childhood.

In real life: Symbols deepen meaning.

💡 Did you know? Free verse has no fixed rhyme.

💭Theme

Loss, growing up, responsibility.

Example: 'Balls will be lost always.'

In real life: Every loss teaches something.

💡 Did you know? Berryman was an American poet.
