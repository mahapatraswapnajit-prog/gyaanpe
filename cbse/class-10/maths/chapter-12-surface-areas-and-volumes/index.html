Class 10 Maths Chapter 12: Surface Areas and Volumes

NCERT Mathematics · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter applies surface area and volume formulas of cubes, cuboids, cylinders, cones, spheres and hemispheres to combinations of solids such as toys, capsules and tents.

Key concepts

🧊Basic Formulas

Cube 6a², a³; cuboid 2(lb + bh + hl), lbh; cylinder CSA 2πrh, V πr²h; cone CSA πrl, V (1/3)πr²h; sphere 4πr², (4/3)πr³; hemisphere CSA 2πr², TSA 3πr², V (2/3)πr³.

Example: Hemisphere r = 7: TSA = 462 cm².

In real life: Containers and tanks.

💡 Did you know? Slant height l = √(r² + h²).

🧩Combination of Solids

Surface area of a combined solid = sum of the visible surfaces only; volume = sum of volumes.

Example: Toy: cone on hemisphere → CSA(cone) + CSA(hemisphere).

In real life: Capsules, tents and toys.

💡 Did you know? Joined faces are not counted in surface area.

💊Capsule

A capsule = cylinder + two hemispheres.

Example: r = 2.5 mm, total length 14 mm → cylinder length 9 mm.

In real life: Medicine capsules.

💡 Did you know? Its surface area = 2πrh + 4πr².

🏕️Tent Problems

Tent = cylinder + cone on top. Canvas = CSA(cylinder) + CSA(cone).

Example: Base area is not covered with canvas.

In real life: Camping and circus tents.

💡 Did you know? Read carefully whether base is included.

📏Units

1 m³ = 1000 litres; 1 litre = 1000 cm³.

Example: A tank of 2 m³ holds 2000 L.

In real life: Water-tank capacity planning.

💡 Did you know? Convert all lengths to the same unit first.

  • Cone volume = (1/3)πr²h.
  • Hemisphere TSA = 3πr².
  • 1 m³ = 1000 litres.
