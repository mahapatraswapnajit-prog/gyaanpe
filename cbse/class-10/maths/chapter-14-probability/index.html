Class 10 Maths Chapter 14: Probability

NCERT Mathematics · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter defines theoretical probability, sure and impossible events and complementary events, and solves problems with coins, dice, cards and bags of balls.

Key concepts

🎲Theoretical Probability

P(E) = number of favourable outcomes ÷ total number of equally likely outcomes.

Example: P(even on a die) = 3/6 = 1/2.

In real life: Lotteries and games of chance.

💡 Did you know? 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1.

✅Sure and Impossible

P(sure event) = 1; P(impossible event) = 0.

Example: P(number < 7 on a die) = 1; P(8 on a die) = 0.

In real life: Weather forecasts give probabilities.

💡 Did you know? P(E) + P(not E) = 1.

🃏Playing Cards

A deck has 52 cards: 4 suits of 13 (spades, clubs black; hearts, diamonds red). Face cards: J, Q, K — 12 in all.

Example: P(king) = 4/52 = 1/13; P(red face card) = 6/52 = 3/26.

In real life: Frequent board questions.

💡 Did you know? There are 26 red and 26 black cards.

🎲Two Dice

Throwing two dice gives 36 outcomes.

Example: P(sum = 7) = 6/36 = 1/6; P(doublet) = 6/36 = 1/6.

In real life: Board games use two dice.

💡 Did you know? Sum 2 and sum 12 each occur once.

🪙Coins and Bags

Two coins: HH, HT, TH, TT. For bags of balls, count each colour.

Example: Bag 3 red, 5 black: P(red) = 3/8.

In real life: Lucky draws and quality checks.

💡 Did you know? P(at least one head in two tosses) = 3/4.
