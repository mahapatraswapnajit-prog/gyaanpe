Class 10 Maths Chapter 3: Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables

NCERT Mathematics · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter solves pairs of linear equations graphically and algebraically (substitution and elimination), tests consistency using coefficient ratios and applies them to word problems.

Key concepts

📊Graphical Meaning

Two linear equations represent two lines: intersecting (unique solution), parallel (no solution), or coincident (infinitely many).

Example: x + y = 5 and x − y = 1 meet at (3, 2).

In real life: Comparing two taxi tariffs graphically.

💡 Did you know? Each solution is a point common to both lines.

⚖️Consistency Test

For a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0 and a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0: a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂ → unique; a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂ → none; all three equal → infinitely many.

Example: 2x + 3y = 5 and 4x + 6y = 10 → coincident.

In real life: Board exams test these ratios often.

💡 Did you know? Consistent = at least one solution.

🔄Substitution Method

Express one variable from one equation and substitute in the other.

Example: x + y = 10, x − y = 2 → x = 2 + y → 2 + 2y = 10 → y = 4, x = 6.

In real life: Splitting a bill between two items.

💡 Did you know? Pick the equation where a variable is easy to isolate.

➖Elimination Method

Multiply equations to make one variable's coefficients equal, then add or subtract to eliminate it.

Example: 3x + 2y = 11, 2x + 3y = 4: multiply and subtract to get x = 5, y = −2.

In real life: Fastest method in many exam problems.

💡 Did you know? Choose multipliers to avoid fractions.

📝Word Problems

Translate the situation into two equations and solve.

Example: Sum of two numbers 50, difference 10 → 30 and 20.

In real life: Age, money, speed and fraction problems.

💡 Did you know? Always define variables clearly with units.

Key terms

TermMeaning
ConsistentHas at least one solution
InconsistentHas no solution
Coincident linesLines that overlap completely
SubstitutionReplace a variable by its expression
EliminationRemove a variable by adding/subtracting

Quick revision

  • a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂ → unique solution.
  • Parallel lines → no solution.
  • Coincident lines → infinitely many solutions.

Practice questions

Q1. Solve x + y = 10 and x − y = 2.

  1. x = 8, y = 2
  2. x = 6, y = 4
  3. x = 4, y = 6
  4. x = 5, y = 5
Show answerB) x = 6, y = 4

Q2. If a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂, the pair has

  1. Infinitely many solutions
  2. No solution
  3. Two solutions
  4. A unique solution
Show answerD) A unique solution

Q3. 2x + 3y = 5 and 4x + 6y = 10 have

  1. Infinitely many solutions
  2. No solution
  3. A unique solution
  4. Exactly two solutions
Show answerA) Infinitely many solutions

Q4. x + 2y = 4 and 2x + 4y = 12 have

  1. One solution at origin
  2. No solution
  3. A unique solution
  4. Infinitely many solutions
Show answerB) No solution

Q5. Parallel lines represent a pair that is

  1. Inconsistent
  2. Dependent
  3. Always coincident
  4. Consistent with one solution
Show answerA) Inconsistent
