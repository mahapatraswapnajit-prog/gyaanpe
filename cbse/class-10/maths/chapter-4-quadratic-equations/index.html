Class 10 Maths Chapter 4: Quadratic Equations

NCERT Mathematics · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter solves quadratic equations by factorisation and the quadratic formula, uses the discriminant to decide the nature of roots, and applies quadratics to word problems.

Key concepts

🟰Standard Form

A quadratic equation is ax² + bx + c = 0 with a ≠ 0. Its solutions are called roots.

Example: x² − 3x − 10 = 0 has roots 5 and −2.

In real life: Projectile height problems give quadratics.

💡 Did you know? A quadratic has at most two real roots.

🧩Factorisation

Split the middle term so that the product equals ac and the sum equals b.

Example: 6x² − x − 2 = 0 → (2x + 1)(3x − 2) = 0 → x = −1/2, 2/3.

In real life: Fast method when numbers are friendly.

💡 Did you know? Check roots by substituting back.

🧮Quadratic Formula

x = [−b ± √(b² − 4ac)] / 2a.

Example: x² − 4x + 1 = 0 → x = 2 ± √3.

In real life: Works for every quadratic.

💡 Did you know? This formula is often linked to Sridharacharya.

🔎Discriminant

D = b² − 4ac. D > 0 → two distinct real roots; D = 0 → two equal roots; D < 0 → no real roots.

Example: x² + 4x + 4: D = 0, equal roots −2, −2.

In real life: Finding k for equal roots is a common board question.

💡 Did you know? Equal roots mean the parabola just touches the x-axis.

📝Word Problems

Area, speed, age and number problems often lead to quadratics; reject roots that do not fit the situation.

Example: A rectangle 2 m longer than wide with area 48 m² → x(x + 2) = 48 → x = 6.

In real life: Planning a garden of given area.

💡 Did you know? Negative length or time is rejected.

Quick revision

  • D > 0 → two distinct real roots.
  • D = 0 → equal roots.
  • D < 0 → no real roots.

Practice questions

Q1. Roots of x² − 3x − 10 = 0 are

  1. 5 and −2
  2. 2 and 5
  3. −5 and 2
  4. 10 and −1
Show answerA) 5 and −2

Q2. The discriminant of ax² + bx + c = 0 is

  1. b² + 4ac
  2. b² − 4ac
  3. √(b² − 4ac)
  4. 4ac − b²
Show answerB) b² − 4ac

Q3. If D = 0, the roots are

  1. Not real
  2. Zero only
  3. Real and equal
  4. Real and distinct
Show answerC) Real and equal

Q4. Nature of roots of 2x² − 4x + 3 = 0:

  1. No real roots
  2. Two equal roots
  3. One root
  4. Two distinct real roots
Show answerA) No real roots

Q5. For x² + kx + 9 = 0 to have equal roots, k =

  1. ±9
  2. ±6
  3. ±3
  4. 9
Show answerB) ±6
