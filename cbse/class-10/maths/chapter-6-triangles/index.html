Class 10 Maths Chapter 6: Triangles

NCERT Mathematics · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter defines similarity, proves the Basic Proportionality Theorem and its converse, and uses AA, SSS and SAS criteria to find unknown lengths in similar triangles.

Key concepts

🔺Similar Figures

Figures are similar if they have the same shape: corresponding angles equal and corresponding sides proportional.

Example: All circles are similar; all squares are similar.

In real life: Maps and photographs are similar to real objects.

💡 Did you know? Congruent figures are a special case of similar figures (ratio 1).

📐Basic Proportionality Theorem

If a line parallel to one side of a triangle cuts the other two sides, it divides them in the same ratio (Thales' theorem). The converse is also true.

Example: In △ABC, DE ∥ BC → AD/DB = AE/EC.

In real life: Dividing a plank into equal parts.

💡 Did you know? Thales was a Greek mathematician.

✅Similarity Criteria

AAA (or AA), SSS (sides proportional) and SAS (one angle equal, including sides proportional).

Example: Two triangles with angles 50°, 60°, 70° are similar.

In real life: Finding heights using shadows.

💡 Did you know? AA is enough since the third angle follows.

📏Using Similarity

Corresponding sides of similar triangles are in the same ratio; this solves unknown lengths.

Example: Pole 6 m casts a 4 m shadow; tower casts 28 m shadow at the same time → tower 42 m.

In real life: Surveyors use this method.

💡 Did you know? Sun's rays are treated as parallel.

🧠Proof Skills

State given, to prove, construction and proof with reasons for each step.

Example: Prove BPT by comparing areas of triangles with the same height.

In real life: Logical writing earns full board marks.

💡 Did you know? Reasons like 'corresponding angles' must be written.

Key terms

TermMeaning
Similar trianglesSame shape, proportional sides
BPTLine parallel to a side divides others proportionally
AA criterionTwo equal angles imply similarity
SSS similarityAll sides proportional
SAS similarityOne angle equal, including sides proportional

Quick revision

  • DE ∥ BC → AD/DB = AE/EC.
  • AA criterion proves similarity.
  • All squares are similar.
