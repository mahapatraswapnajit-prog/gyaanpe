Class 10 Maths Chapter 7: Coordinate Geometry

NCERT Mathematics · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter uses the distance formula, section formula and midpoint formula to find lengths, dividing points and ratios, and to check the shapes formed by given points.

Key concepts

📏Distance Formula

PQ = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]. Distance from origin = √(x² + y²).

Example: Distance between (1, 2) and (4, 6) = 5.

In real life: GPS straight-line distances.

💡 Did you know? It comes from the Pythagoras theorem.

✂️Section Formula

Point dividing (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂) internally in ratio m : n is ((mx₂ + nx₁)/(m + n), (my₂ + ny₁)/(m + n)).

Example: Dividing (4, −3) and (8, 5) in 3 : 1 gives (7, 3).

In real life: Dividing a road between two towns.

💡 Did you know? For m = n, it gives the midpoint.

🎯Midpoint

Midpoint = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2).

Example: Midpoint of (2, 3) and (6, 7) = (4, 5).

In real life: Meeting halfway.

💡 Did you know? Diagonals of a parallelogram share a midpoint.

📐Checking Shapes

Use distances to show points form a square, rectangle, rhombus or right triangle; equal distances also test collinearity.

Example: Points (1, 1), (4, 1), (4, 4), (1, 4) form a square of side 3.

In real life: Map plotting and architecture.

💡 Did you know? Collinear points: AB + BC = AC.

🔍Finding Ratios

To find the ratio in which a line or axis divides a segment, take ratio k : 1 and set the needed coordinate.

Example: The x-axis divides a segment where the y-coordinate is 0.

In real life: A frequent 3-mark board question.

💡 Did you know? The ratio may come out as a fraction; express it as m : n.

Quick revision

  • Distance of (x, y) from origin = √(x² + y²).
  • Midpoint = average of coordinates.
  • x-axis points have y = 0.
