Class 10 Maths Chapter 9: Some Applications of Trigonometry

NCERT Mathematics · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter applies trigonometric ratios to heights and distances using angles of elevation and depression, including problems with two observation points.

Key concepts

👀Line of Sight

The line from the observer's eye to the object is the line of sight.

Example: Looking at the top of a tower.

In real life: Pilots and sailors use these angles.

💡 Did you know? Angles are measured from the horizontal.

⬆️Angle of Elevation

When the object is above the horizontal, the angle between line of sight and horizontal is the angle of elevation.

Example: From 30 m away, a tower top at 60° elevation → height = 30√3 m.

In real life: Finding heights of trees and buildings.

💡 Did you know? Height = distance × tan(angle).

⬇️Angle of Depression

When the object is below the horizontal, the angle is the angle of depression. It equals the angle of elevation from the object (alternate angles).

Example: From a 50 m cliff, a boat at 45° depression is 50 m away.

In real life: Lighthouse keepers spotting ships.

💡 Did you know? Alternate angles make the two equal.

🗼Solving Problems

Draw a neat figure, mark right angles, choose tan when height and distance are involved, sin/cos when slant length is involved.

Example: A 20 m ladder at 60° to the ground reaches 10√3 m up.

In real life: Fire ladders and ramps.

💡 Did you know? Use √3 ≈ 1.732 when needed.

📏Two-Angle Problems

When an observer moves, two right triangles share the height; set up two tan equations.

Example: Elevation changes from 30° to 60° after walking 20 m → height = 10√3 m.

In real life: Common 5-mark board question.

💡 Did you know? Subtract distances to link the triangles.

  • Angle of elevation = angle of depression (alternate angles).
  • Height = distance × tan θ.
  • Use tan for height–distance problems.

  1. 60 m
  2. 30√3 m
  3. 10√3 m
  4. 30 m

  2. 100 m
  3. 25 m
