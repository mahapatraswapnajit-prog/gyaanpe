Class 10 Science Chapter 1: Chemical Reactions and Equations

NCERT Science · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter covers writing and balancing chemical equations and the types of reactions — combination, decomposition, displacement, double displacement and redox — along with corrosion and rancidity.

Key concepts

⚗️Chemical Equations

A chemical equation shows reactants and products with symbols. A balanced equation has equal numbers of each atom on both sides (law of conservation of mass).

Example: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

In real life: Fuel burning, cooking and rusting are reactions.

💡 Did you know? State symbols: (s), (l), (g), (aq).

🔗Combination and Decomposition

Combination: two or more reactants form one product. Decomposition: one reactant breaks into simpler products (by heat, light or electricity).

Example: CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ (combination, exothermic). 2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl₂ in sunlight.

In real life: Quicklime with water is used for whitewashing.

💡 Did you know? Silver chloride turning grey in sunlight is used in black-and-white photography.

🔄Displacement Reactions

A more reactive element displaces a less reactive one from its compound. In double displacement, ions are exchanged, often forming a precipitate.

Example: Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu (blue solution turns green). Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄↓ + 2NaCl.

In real life: Iron nails get a brown copper coating in copper sulphate.

💡 Did you know? A precipitate is an insoluble solid formed in a solution.

🔥Oxidation and Reduction

Oxidation: gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen. Reduction: loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen. Both together are redox reactions.

Example: CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O: CuO is reduced, H₂ is oxidised.

In real life: Respiration is exothermic.

💡 Did you know? Exothermic reactions release heat; endothermic absorb heat.

🦠Corrosion and Rancidity

Corrosion is metals being eaten away by air and moisture (rusting of iron). Rancidity is oxidation of fats and oils, changing smell and taste.

Example: Chips packets are flushed with nitrogen to prevent rancidity.

In real life: Rust damages bridges, cars and ships.

💡 Did you know? Painting, galvanising and oiling prevent rusting.

Key terms

TermMeaning
Balanced equationEqual atoms on both sides
PrecipitateInsoluble solid formed in solution
ExothermicReleases heat
RedoxOxidation and reduction together
RancidityOxidation of fats and oils

Quick revision

  • Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu is displacement.
  • CaO + H₂O is exothermic combination.
  • Nitrogen flushing prevents rancidity.
