Class 10 Science Chapter 10: The Human Eye and the Colourful World

NCERT Science · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter explains the structure and working of the human eye, defects of vision and their correction, dispersion of light through a prism, atmospheric refraction and scattering of light.

Key concepts

👁️The Eye

Light enters through the cornea; the iris controls the pupil size; the lens focuses images on the retina; the optic nerve sends signals to the brain. Ciliary muscles change the lens's focal length (accommodation).

Example: Near point ≈ 25 cm; far point = infinity for a normal eye.

In real life: Eye donation can restore sight to others.

💡 Did you know? Rods sense dim light; cones sense colour.

👓Defects of Vision

Myopia (near-sightedness) — corrected by a concave lens. Hypermetropia (far-sightedness) — convex lens. Presbyopia (age-related) — bifocal lens. Cataract — cloudy lens, treated by surgery.

Example: Myopia: image forms in front of the retina.

In real life: Regular eye check-ups for students.

💡 Did you know? Bifocals have both concave and convex parts.

🌈Dispersion

White light splits into seven colours (VIBGYOR) through a prism because each colour bends differently. Violet bends most, red least.

Example: A rainbow forms by dispersion, refraction and internal reflection in raindrops.

In real life: Rainbows appear opposite to the Sun.

💡 Did you know? Newton recombined colours with a second inverted prism.

✨Atmospheric Refraction

Stars twinkle because their light is refracted by moving air layers. The Sun appears about 2 minutes earlier at sunrise and stays 2 minutes after sunset.

Example: Planets do not twinkle much because they are extended sources.

In real life: Astronomers place telescopes on mountains.

💡 Did you know? Hot air above fire makes objects look wavy.

🌅Scattering of Light

Small particles scatter blue light more, so the sky looks blue. At sunrise and sunset, light travels a longer path and blue scatters away, so the Sun looks reddish. Danger signals are red because red scatters least.

Example: The sky looks dark to astronauts in space.

In real life: Fog lights are often yellow.

💡 Did you know? Tyndall effect is scattering by colloidal particles.

Quick revision

  • Myopia is corrected by a concave lens.
  • Sky is blue due to scattering.
  • Stars twinkle due to atmospheric refraction.
