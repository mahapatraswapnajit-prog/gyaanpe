Class 10 Science Chapter 11: Electricity

NCERT Science · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter explains electric current, potential difference, Ohm's law, factors affecting resistance, series and parallel combinations, the heating effect of current and electric power and energy.

Key concepts

⚡Current and Potential Difference

Current I = Q/t (ampere). Potential difference V = W/Q (volt). Current flows from + to − (conventional).

Example: 2 C of charge flowing in 1 s → I = 2 A.

In real life: Phone chargers show output in volts and amperes.

💡 Did you know? An ammeter is connected in series; a voltmeter in parallel.

📏Ohm's Law and Resistance

V = IR at constant temperature. Resistance R = ρL/A — depends on length, area, material and temperature. Unit ohm (Ω).

Example: V = 12 V, R = 4 Ω → I = 3 A.

In real life: Nichrome is used in heaters for high resistivity.

💡 Did you know? Doubling length doubles resistance; doubling area halves it.

🔗Series and Parallel

Series: R = R₁ + R₂ + …, same current. Parallel: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + …, same voltage.

Example: Two 4 Ω resistors: series 8 Ω, parallel 2 Ω.

In real life: If one bulb fuses in parallel, others still work.

💡 Did you know? Household wiring is parallel so each device gets full voltage.

🔥Heating Effect

Heat H = I²Rt (Joule's law). Used in heaters, irons, fuses and bulbs.

Example: A fuse melts when current exceeds the safe limit.

In real life: Electric kettles use heating effect.

💡 Did you know? Bulbs are filled with inert gases to protect the tungsten filament.

💡Electric Power

P = VI = I²R = V²/R; unit watt. Energy commercial unit: 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.

Example: A 100 W bulb for 10 hours uses 1 kWh (1 unit).

In real life: Electricity bills are in units (kWh).

💡 Did you know? LED bulbs give the same light with far less power.
