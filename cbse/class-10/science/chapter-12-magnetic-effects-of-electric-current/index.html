Class 10 Science Chapter 12: Magnetic Effects of Electric Current

NCERT Science · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter explains magnetic fields and field lines, fields produced by currents in straight wires, loops and solenoids, the force on a current-carrying conductor (Fleming's left-hand rule), and domestic electric circuits and safety.

Key concepts

🧲Magnetic Field

A magnetic field is shown by field lines: from N to S outside the magnet; they never cross; closer lines mean a stronger field.

Example: Iron filings show field patterns.

In real life: Compasses detect magnetic fields.

💡 Did you know? Oersted found that current produces a magnetic field.

🔄Field due to Current

A straight wire has circular field lines (right-hand thumb rule). A circular loop and a solenoid make stronger fields; a solenoid's field inside is uniform, like a bar magnet.

Example: A solenoid with soft iron core is an electromagnet.

In real life: Electromagnets lift scrap iron in junkyards.

💡 Did you know? More turns and more current mean a stronger field.

👈Force on a Conductor

A current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field experiences a force. Direction is given by Fleming's left-hand rule (forefinger field, middle finger current, thumb force).

Example: Force is maximum when current is perpendicular to the field.

In real life: Fans, mixers and washing machines use motors.

💡 Did you know? Electric motors work on this principle.

🏠Domestic Circuits

Live wire (red/brown), neutral (black/blue) and earth (green). Supply in India: 220 V, 50 Hz. Fuses and MCBs protect against overloading and short circuits.

Example: Earthing protects users from shocks through metal bodies.

In real life: MCBs trip automatically during faults.

💡 Did you know? Overloading happens when too many devices run on one socket.

⚠️Safety

A short circuit happens when live and neutral wires touch. Never touch switches with wet hands; use earthed plugs for heavy appliances.

Example: A three-pin plug's longest pin is for earth.

In real life: Electrical safety saves lives.

💡 Did you know? Fuse wire has a low melting point.

Practice questions

Q1. Magnetic field lines outside a magnet go from

  1. South to North
  2. East to West
  3. Nowhere
  4. North to South
Show answerD) North to South

Q2. Magnetic field lines

  1. End at the middle
  2. Are straight always
  3. Always intersect
  4. Never intersect
Show answerD) Never intersect

Q3. Direction of field around a straight current-carrying wire is given by

  1. Right-hand thumb rule
  2. Fleming's left-hand rule
  3. Snell's law
  4. Ohm's law
Show answerA) Right-hand thumb rule

Q4. Force on a current-carrying conductor in a field is given by

  1. Newton's law
  2. Fleming's left-hand rule
  3. Lenz's law only
  4. Right-hand thumb rule

  1. Insulator
  2. Electromagnet
  3. Capacitor
