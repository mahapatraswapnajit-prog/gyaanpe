Class 10 Science Chapter 13: Our Environment

NCERT Science · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter explains ecosystems, food chains and webs, the 10% law of energy flow, biological magnification, ozone depletion and the management of biodegradable and non-biodegradable waste.

Key concepts

🌳Ecosystem

An ecosystem has biotic (living) and abiotic (non-living) components interacting together.

Example: A pond, a forest or a garden is an ecosystem.

In real life: Parks are small urban ecosystems.

💡 Did you know? Man-made ecosystems include crop fields and aquariums.

🔗Food Chains and Webs

Producers → herbivores → carnivores. Interconnected food chains form a food web. Decomposers recycle nutrients.

Example: Grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → eagle.

In real life: Food chains are usually short (3–4 levels).

💡 Did you know? Only about 10% of energy passes to the next trophic level (10% law).

☠️Biological Magnification

Non-biodegradable chemicals like some pesticides accumulate and increase in concentration at higher trophic levels.

Example: Humans at the top of food chains get the highest concentration.

In real life: Organic farming reduces pesticide use.

💡 Did you know? DDT is a well-known example.

🛡️Ozone Depletion

The ozone (O₃) layer in the stratosphere absorbs harmful UV rays. CFCs used in refrigerants and aerosols damage it.

Example: UV rays can cause skin cancer.

In real life: CFC-free fridges are now common.

💡 Did you know? The Montreal Protocol (1987) controls ozone-depleting substances.

♻️Waste Management

Biodegradable waste is broken down by microbes; non-biodegradable waste (plastics, glass) persists. Segregation, reduce, reuse and recycle help.

Example: Composting kitchen waste.

In real life: Swachh Bharat promotes segregation.

💡 Did you know? Disposable plastic cups add greatly to waste.

Q4. Ozone layer protects us from

  1. Sound
  2. UV radiation
