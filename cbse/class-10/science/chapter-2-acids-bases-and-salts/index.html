Class 10 Science Chapter 2: Acids, Bases and Salts

NCERT Science · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter explains properties of acids and bases, indicators and the pH scale, neutralisation and other reactions, and the preparation and uses of salts like baking soda, washing soda, bleaching powder and plaster of Paris.

Key concepts

🍋Acids and Bases

Acids taste sour and turn blue litmus red; bases taste bitter, feel soapy and turn red litmus blue. Acids give H⁺ ions in water; bases give OH⁻ ions.

Example: Lemon juice (citric acid), vinegar (acetic acid); soap and lime water are basic.

In real life: Antacids are mild bases for acidity.

💡 Did you know? Water-soluble bases are called alkalis.

📊pH Scale

pH ranges 0–14: less than 7 acidic, 7 neutral, more than 7 basic. Lower pH means stronger acid.

Example: Pure water pH 7; stomach acid about 1–2; milk of magnesia about 10.

In real life: Soil pH decides which crops grow well.

💡 Did you know? Tooth decay starts when mouth pH falls below about 5.5.

⚖️Neutralisation

Acid + base → salt + water. Metal carbonate + acid → salt + CO₂ + water. Acid + metal → salt + hydrogen gas.

Example: NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O.

In real life: Bee sting (acidic) treated with baking soda.

💡 Did you know? Hydrogen burns with a pop sound; CO₂ turns lime water milky.

🧂Important Salts

Common salt NaCl; baking soda NaHCO₃; washing soda Na₂CO₃·10H₂O; bleaching powder CaOCl₂; plaster of Paris CaSO₄·½H₂O.

Example: Plaster of Paris sets into gypsum when mixed with water.

In real life: Doctors use plaster of Paris for fractures.

💡 Did you know? Chlor-alkali process makes NaOH, Cl₂ and H₂ from brine.

💧Water of Crystallisation

Some salts contain fixed water molecules in their crystals. Blue copper sulphate (CuSO₄·5H₂O) turns white on heating.

Example: Adding water turns it blue again.

In real life: Used in lab tests for water.

💡 Did you know? Washing soda has 10 water molecules.

  • Baking soda is NaHCO₃.
  • Plaster of Paris is CaSO₄·½H₂O.
