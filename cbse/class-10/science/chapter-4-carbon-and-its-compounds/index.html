Class 10 Science Chapter 4: Carbon and its Compounds

NCERT Science · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter explains covalent bonding, catenation and allotropes of carbon, homologous series and functional groups, key reactions of carbon compounds, ethanol and ethanoic acid, and how soaps and detergents clean.

Key concepts

🔗Covalent Bonding

Carbon has 4 valence electrons and forms covalent bonds by sharing electrons. Its compounds usually have low melting points and do not conduct electricity.

Example: Methane CH₄: carbon shares 4 pairs with 4 hydrogens.

In real life: Fuels, plastics and medicines are carbon compounds.

💡 Did you know? Carbon is tetravalent.

♾️Catenation and Allotropes

Carbon can link with other carbon atoms to form long chains and rings (catenation). Diamond, graphite and fullerenes are allotropes of carbon.

Example: Diamond is the hardest natural substance; graphite is soft and conducts.

In real life: Pencils use graphite.

💡 Did you know? Fullerene C₆₀ looks like a football.

🧪Homologous Series

Members differ by –CH₂– and have similar properties. Saturated (alkanes, single bonds) and unsaturated (alkenes double, alkynes triple).

Example: Methane, ethane, propane…; ethene C₂H₄; ethyne C₂H₂.

In real life: LPG is mainly butane.

💡 Did you know? Functional groups: –OH alcohol, –COOH carboxylic acid, –CHO aldehyde, >C=O ketone.

🔥Reactions of Carbon Compounds

Combustion, oxidation, addition (hydrogenation), substitution. Ethanol + ethanoic acid → ester (sweet smell) — esterification.

Example: Vegetable oils are hydrogenated with Ni catalyst to make vanaspati.

In real life: Esters are used in perfumes.

💡 Did you know? Saturated fats are considered less healthy than unsaturated oils.

🧼Soaps and Detergents

Soap molecules have a hydrophilic (ionic) head and hydrophobic (carbon chain) tail, forming micelles that trap oil. Soaps form scum in hard water; detergents do not.

Example: Detergents work in hard water.

In real life: Washing clothes and hands.

💡 Did you know? Saponification: fat + NaOH → soap.
