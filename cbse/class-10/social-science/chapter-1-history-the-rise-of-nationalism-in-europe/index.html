Class 10 Social Science Chapter 1: History: The Rise of Nationalism in Europe

NCERT Social Science · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter traces the growth of nationalism in Europe from the French Revolution through liberalism, conservatism and the 1848 revolutions to the unification of Germany and Italy, the role of culture and allegory, and Balkan tensions.

Key concepts

🇫🇷French Revolution

The French Revolution (1789) transferred sovereignty from the monarch to the people and spread ideas of la patrie (fatherland) and le citoyen (citizen).

Example: A new French flag (tricolour) replaced the royal standard.

In real life: Liberty, equality and fraternity inspire constitutions today.

💡 Did you know? Napoleon's Civil Code of 1804 ended privileges by birth and ensured equality before law.

🏛️Liberalism and Reaction

Liberals wanted individual freedom, equality before law and representative government. After Napoleon's defeat, the Congress of Vienna (1815), hosted by Metternich, restored conservative monarchies.

Example: The German Confederation of 39 states was set up in 1815.

In real life: Trade unions in history pushed political unity.

💡 Did you know? A customs union, the Zollverein (1834), helped economic unity in Germany.

🔥Revolutionaries and Romanticism

Giuseppe Mazzini founded Young Italy and Young Europe. Romantic artists and poets used folk songs, language and history to build national feeling.

Example: Greek independence was recognised by the Treaty of Constantinople (1832).

In real life: Folk culture still unites communities.

💡 Did you know? Metternich called Mazzini 'the most dangerous enemy of our social order'.

🇩🇪Unification of Germany and Italy

Prussian chief minister Otto von Bismarck led German unification through wars; in 1871 Kaiser William I was proclaimed emperor at Versailles. Italy was unified by Cavour, Garibaldi and King Victor Emmanuel II (proclaimed king of Italy in 1861).

Example: Garibaldi's Red Shirts conquered the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies.

In real life: Nation-building often involves both diplomacy and war.

💡 Did you know? The Frankfurt Parliament of 1848 failed to unify Germany.

🎨Visualising the Nation

Nations were shown as female figures (allegories): Marianne for France and Germania for Germany. The Balkans became a region of tension that contributed to the First World War.

Example: Frédéric Sorrieu's 1848 prints imagined a world of democratic republics.

In real life: National symbols build shared identity.

💡 Did you know? Britain's nationhood grew through the Act of Union (1707) with Scotland.
