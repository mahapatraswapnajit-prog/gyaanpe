Class 10 Social Science Chapter 10: Geography: Minerals and Energy Resources

NCERT Social Science · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter covers minerals and their modes of occurrence, important ferrous and non-ferrous minerals, conventional and non-conventional energy sources and the need for conservation.

Key concepts

⛏️Minerals

A mineral is a naturally occurring substance with a definite internal structure. Ores are minerals from which metals are extracted. Minerals occur in veins, lodes, beds and placer deposits.

Example: Iron ore: magnetite (up to about 70% iron) and haematite.

In real life: Steel for buildings starts with iron ore.

💡 Did you know? The Odisha-Jharkhand belt has rich iron ore.

🧱Important Minerals

Manganese for steel; copper for wires (Khetri, Rajasthan; Balaghat, MP); bauxite for aluminium (Odisha is a leading producer); mica for electricals (Koderma, Jharkhand); limestone for cement.

Example: Panchpatmali in Koraput (Odisha) has bauxite deposits.

In real life: Aluminium is used in aircraft and foil.

💡 Did you know? Mica can be split into thin sheets.

🔥Conventional Energy

Coal (anthracite highest quality, bituminous, lignite at Neyveli), petroleum (Mumbai High, Digboi in Assam — oldest), natural gas (Krishna-Godavari basin), electricity (thermal and hydel), nuclear (uranium, thorium).

Example: Damodar valley has Gondwana coal.

In real life: Most of India's electricity still comes from coal.

💡 Did you know? The HVJ (Hazira-Vijaipur-Jagdishpur) pipeline carries gas.

☀️Non-conventional Energy

Solar (Rajasthan, Gujarat), wind (Tamil Nadu from Nagercoil to Madurai), tidal (Gulf of Khambhat, Gulf of Kutch), geothermal (Parvati valley — Manikaran, Himachal; Puga valley, Ladakh), biogas.

Example: Biogas plants use cattle dung.

In real life: Rooftop solar cuts electricity bills.

💡 Did you know? India is building large solar parks.

💡Conservation

Use public transport, save electricity, use energy-efficient appliances and promote renewables.

Example: Switching off lights when not needed.

In real life: Energy saved is energy produced.

💡 Did you know? LED bulbs use much less energy.
