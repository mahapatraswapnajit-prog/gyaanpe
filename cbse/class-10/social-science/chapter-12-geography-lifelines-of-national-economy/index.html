Class 10 Social Science Chapter 12: Geography: Lifelines of National Economy

NCERT Social Science · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter describes India's transport and communication networks — roads, railways, pipelines, waterways, ports and airways — and the role of trade and tourism as lifelines of the economy.

Key concepts

🛣️Roadways

The Golden Quadrilateral links Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata. The North–South corridor links Srinagar to Kanyakumari; the East–West corridor links Silchar to Porbandar.

Example: National Highways are built by the NHAI.

In real life: Roads connect villages to markets.

💡 Did you know? Border Roads Organisation builds roads in border areas.

🚆Railways

Indian Railways is the largest public sector undertaking in India and a major carrier of goods and passengers.

Example: Konkan Railway runs along the west coast.

In real life: Railways unite the country.

💡 Did you know? The first train ran from Bombay to Thane in 1853.

🛢️Pipelines

Pipelines carry crude oil, petroleum products and gas cheaply. HVJ pipeline links Hazira to Jagdishpur.

Example: Pipelines reduce road traffic and transport losses.

In real life: Gas reaches homes through pipelines in cities.

💡 Did you know? Initial cost is high but running cost is low.

⚓Ports and Waterways

Major ports include Kandla (Deendayal) — first port developed after independence, Mumbai — largest natural port, JNPT, Marmagao, Kochi, Tuticorin, Chennai, Visakhapatnam (deepest landlocked), Paradip (Odisha) and Haldia.

Example: National Waterway 1 is on the Ganga between Allahabad and Haldia.

In real life: Ports drive foreign trade.

💡 Did you know? About 95% of India's trade by volume moves through sea.

✈️Airways and Communication

Air travel is fastest and reaches remote areas. Communication includes post, telephone, TV, radio and the internet.

Example: Pawan Hans provides helicopter services.

In real life: Digital India expands internet access.

💡 Did you know? India Post and telecom connect the nation.
