Class 10 Social Science Chapter 13: Civics: Power Sharing

NCERT Social Science · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter compares Belgium's accommodation with Sri Lanka's majoritarianism, explains prudential and moral reasons for power sharing, and its horizontal, vertical and other forms.

Key concepts

🇧🇪Belgium

Belgium's population: about 59% Dutch speakers, 40% French and 1% German. To avoid conflict, Belgium amended its constitution four times between 1970 and 1993, sharing power among communities.

Example: Equal Dutch and French ministers at the central level; Brussels has a separate government.

In real life: Belgium's model is called 'accommodation'.

💡 Did you know? A 'community government' handles cultural, educational and language matters.

🇱🇰Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka's Sinhala majority (about 74%) adopted majoritarian measures: the 1956 Act made Sinhala the only official language. Sri Lankan Tamils felt excluded, leading to conflict and civil war.

Example: Preference to Sinhala speakers in jobs and universities.

In real life: Majoritarianism can damage unity.

💡 Did you know? The civil war ended in 2009.

⚖️Why Share Power?

Prudential reasons: reduces conflict and ensures stability. Moral reasons: it is the very spirit of democracy — people affected should have a say.

Example: Power sharing gives legitimacy to government.

In real life: Coalition governments share power.

💡 Did you know? Both reasons support each other.

🧩Forms of Power Sharing

Horizontal: among legislature, executive and judiciary (checks and balances). Vertical: among central, state and local governments. Among social groups (reserved seats). Among parties and pressure groups.

Example: Courts can check laws made by the legislature.

In real life: Panchayats share power at the local level.

💡 Did you know? Coalitions share power among parties.

📝Key Ideas

Accommodation means recognising and respecting differences; majoritarianism means the majority community rules over others.

Example: Belgium chose accommodation; Sri Lanka chose majoritarianism at first.

In real life: Respecting minorities builds trust.

💡 Did you know? Democracy is not just rule of majority.

Key terms
