Class 10 Social Science Chapter 14: Civics: Federalism

NCERT Social Science · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter explains federalism and its features, the Union, State and Concurrent lists, how federalism works in India through linguistic states and language policy, and decentralisation through the 1992 amendments.

Key concepts

🏛️What is Federalism?

Federalism divides power between a central authority and constituent units, each with its own jurisdiction protected by the constitution. Courts settle disputes.

Example: India, USA, Australia are federations.

In real life: States run police and agriculture.

💡 Did you know? Coming-together (USA) vs holding-together (India) federations.

📜Three Lists

Union List: defence, foreign affairs, banking, currency, communications. State List: police, trade within state, agriculture, irrigation. Concurrent List: education, forests, trade unions, marriage, adoption, succession.

Example: If laws conflict on a concurrent subject, the Union law prevails.

In real life: GST council shows cooperative federalism.

💡 Did you know? Residuary subjects (like computer software) go to the Union.

🗺️Federalism in Practice

Linguistic states were formed (States Reorganisation, 1956). Hindi was identified as the official language but not imposed; 22 languages are in the Eighth Schedule.

Example: Some states like J&K had special status earlier.

In real life: Language-based states strengthened unity.

💡 Did you know? Union Territories are run more directly by the Centre.

🏘️Decentralisation

The 1992 constitutional amendments (73rd and 74th) made local governments stronger: regular elections, reserved seats for SCs, STs, OBCs, at least one-third seats for women, and State Election Commissions.

Example: Gram Panchayat headed by the sarpanch.

In real life: Local problems are solved locally.

💡 Did you know? Zila Parishad is at the district level.

🤝Centre–State Relations

Coalition governments after 1990 increased respect for state autonomy.

Example: Earlier, the Centre sometimes dismissed state governments.

In real life: Inter-state councils resolve issues.

💡 Did you know? Cooperative federalism is encouraged today.

Quick revision

  • Education is in the Concurrent List.
  • Residuary powers lie with the Union.
  • One-third local seats reserved for women.
