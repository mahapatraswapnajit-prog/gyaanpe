Class 10 Social Science Chapter 15: Civics: Gender, Religion and Caste

NCERT Social Science · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter examines how gender, religion and caste shape politics in India — the struggle for gender equality, the dangers of communalism, the meaning of a secular state, and caste in politics.

Key concepts

⚧Gender and Politics

Sexual division of labour assigned housework to women. Feminist movements demanded equality in political, legal, educational and career opportunities. Women's representation in legislatures has been low.

Example: One-third seats in panchayats and municipalities are reserved for women.

In real life: Equal pay laws exist, but gaps remain.

💡 Did you know? The Women's Reservation Act (2023) provides for one-third seats for women in Lok Sabha and state assemblies, to be applied after delimitation.

🕌Religion and Communalism

Religion in politics is not wrong in itself, but communalism treats one religion as superior and others as enemies. India is a secular state: no official religion, freedom of religion and equality.

Example: Communalism can take forms from prejudice to violence.

In real life: Interfaith harmony strengthens democracy.

💡 Did you know? The Constitution prohibits discrimination on grounds of religion.

🧱Caste and Politics

Caste inequalities still exist despite laws. In politics, caste affects candidate selection and voting, but no constituency has only one caste, and voters consider many factors.

Example: Reserved seats for SCs and STs aim at fair representation.

In real life: Social reformers like Phule, Ambedkar and Periyar fought caste discrimination.

💡 Did you know? Untouchability is abolished by Article 17.

⚖️Secular State

The Indian state keeps equal distance from all religions but can intervene to ensure equality (e.g., banning untouchability).

Example: No official religion of India.

In real life: Courts uphold secular values.

💡 Did you know? Secularism protects everyone's freedom.

📊Key Terms

Patriarchy: system valuing men more. Feminist: person believing in equal rights for women and men. Communalism: politics based on religious division. Casteism: discrimination based on caste.

Example: Literacy among women has improved but is still lower than men.

In real life: Awareness reduces discrimination.

💡 Did you know? Sex ratio shows gender balance.
