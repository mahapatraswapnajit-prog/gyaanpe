Class 10 Social Science Chapter 16: Civics: Political Parties

NCERT Social Science · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter explains what political parties are, their functions and necessity, types of party systems, recognition of national and state parties, challenges such as dynastic politics and money power, and reforms like the anti-defection law.

Key concepts

🎯What is a Party?

A political party is a group of people who come together to contest elections and hold power, with shared policies. It has leaders, active members and followers.

Example: Parties put forward candidates and policies.

In real life: Manifestos list party promises.

💡 Did you know? Parties are a necessary condition for democracy.

📋Functions

Contest elections, put forward policies, make laws, form and run governments, act as opposition, shape public opinion and provide access to welfare schemes.

Example: Opposition parties criticise and keep the government in check.

In real life: Parties connect people to government.

💡 Did you know? Party discipline guides how members vote.

🗳️Party Systems

One-party (e.g., China), two-party (USA, UK) and multi-party (India). Alliances or fronts form coalitions.

Example: India's multi-party system reflects its diversity.

In real life: Coalition governments need cooperation.

💡 Did you know? The Election Commission recognises national and state parties and gives them symbols.

⚠️Challenges

Lack of internal democracy, dynastic succession, money and muscle power, and lack of meaningful choice.

Example: Rich candidates may dominate.

In real life: Voter awareness helps.

💡 Did you know? Some leaders treat parties like family property.

🔧Reforms

Anti-defection law (MPs/MLAs lose seat if they change parties), affidavits about property and criminal cases by candidates, and mandatory organisational elections are steps for reform.

Example: The Supreme Court made candidate affidavits mandatory.

In real life: NOTA option is available to voters.

💡 Did you know? Citizens can pressure parties through participation.
