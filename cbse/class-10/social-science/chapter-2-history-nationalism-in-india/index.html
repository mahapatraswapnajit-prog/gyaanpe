Class 10 Social Science Chapter 2: History: Nationalism in India

NCERT Social Science · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter traces Indian nationalism under Gandhi — early satyagrahas, the Rowlatt Act and Jallianwala Bagh, Non-Cooperation and Khilafat, Purna Swaraj and Civil Disobedience — and the symbols that built a sense of collective belonging.

Key concepts

🕊️Gandhi and Satyagraha

Gandhi returned from South Africa in 1915. Satyagraha means the power of truth and non-violent resistance. He led Champaran (1917, indigo farmers), Kheda (1917, peasants) and Ahmedabad (1918, mill workers).

Example: Champaran was in Bihar.

In real life: Peaceful protest remains a democratic tool.

💡 Did you know? Satyagraha was first used by Gandhi in South Africa.

😢Rowlatt Act and Jallianwala Bagh

The Rowlatt Act (1919) allowed detention without trial. On 13 April 1919, General Dyer fired on a peaceful crowd at Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar.

Example: Rabindranath Tagore returned his knighthood in protest.

In real life: A memorial stands at Jallianwala Bagh today.

💡 Did you know? Baisakhi was being celebrated that day.

🤝Non-Cooperation and Khilafat

The Non-Cooperation Movement (1920) joined with the Khilafat Movement led by the Ali brothers. People boycotted foreign goods, schools, courts and titles. It was withdrawn after violence at Chauri Chaura (1922).

Example: Foreign cloth was burnt in bonfires.

In real life: Swadeshi supports local industry.

💡 Did you know? Plantation workers in Assam left gardens believing in 'Gandhi Raj'.

🧂Civil Disobedience

At Lahore (1929) under Nehru, Congress demanded Purna Swaraj; 26 January 1930 was celebrated as Independence Day. Gandhi's Dandi March began on 12 March 1930 to break the salt law. The Gandhi-Irwin Pact came in 1931.

Example: The march covered about 240 miles from Sabarmati to Dandi.

In real life: Salt was a symbol everyone could relate to.

💡 Did you know? The Simon Commission (1928) had no Indian member — 'Simon Go Back'.

🇮🇳Sense of Collective Belonging

Images like Bharat Mata (painted by Abanindranath Tagore), songs like Vande Mataram (Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay) and folklore built national unity. Dr Ambedkar and Gandhi signed the Poona Pact (1932) on reserved seats for Depressed Classes.

Example: A tricolour Swaraj flag with a spinning wheel was designed by 1921.

In real life: Shared symbols still unite India.

💡 Did you know? Natesa Sastri collected Tamil folk tales.
