Class 10 Social Science Chapter 22: Economics: Consumer Rights

NCERT Social Science · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter explains how consumers are exploited, the Consumer Protection Act, consumer rights, three-tier consumer courts, quality marks and the role of the consumer movement.

Key concepts

🛒Consumer Exploitation

Consumers are exploited by underweight, adulteration, false information, high prices, poor after-sales service and unsafe products.

Example: Selling adulterated oil or expired medicines.

In real life: Reading labels protects us.

💡 Did you know? Large companies can manipulate markets.

📜Consumer Protection Act

The Consumer Protection Act (COPRA) was passed in 1986 and replaced by a new act in 2019. Consumer courts work at district, state and national levels.

Example: 15 March is World Consumer Rights Day; 24 December is National Consumers' Day in India.

In real life: Consumer forums settle disputes.

💡 Did you know? Consumers can file complaints without a lawyer.

✅Consumer Rights

Right to safety, right to be informed, right to choose, right to seek redressal, right to represent and right to consumer education.

Example: MRP, date of manufacture and expiry must be printed.

In real life: Ask for a bill for every purchase.

💡 Did you know? The Right to Information Act (2005) helps citizens get government information.

🏷️Quality Marks

ISI (industrial products), Agmark (agricultural products) and Hallmark (gold jewellery) certify quality.

Example: Hallmark is given by the Bureau of Indian Standards.

In real life: Packaged water must carry ISI mark.

💡 Did you know? Look for these marks before buying.

🗣️Consumer Movement

Consumer awareness and organisations (consumer forums) help protect rights. The motto: 'Jago Grahak Jago'.

Example: Filing a complaint about a faulty product.

In real life: Aware consumers keep sellers honest.

💡 Did you know? Consumer International is a global federation.
