Class 10 Social Science Chapter 4: History: The Age of Industrialisation

NCERT Social Science · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter explains proto-industrialisation, the rise of factories in Britain, the lives of workers, industrialisation in colonial India with its gomasthas and jobbers, and how markets were created through advertising.

Key concepts

🏚️Proto-industrialisation

Before factories, merchants supplied raw material to rural households who produced goods at home for international markets.

Example: Spinners and weavers in English villages worked for merchants.

In real life: Home-based work still exists today.

💡 Did you know? It was part of a network of commercial exchange.

⚙️Coming of Factories

Cotton and iron industries grew in Britain. The spinning jenny (James Hargreaves, 1764) and James Watt's improved steam engine increased production.

Example: Spinning jenny let one worker run many spindles.

In real life: Factories changed city life.

💡 Did you know? Many industries still depended on hand labour in early years.

👷Life of Workers

Workers faced uncertain jobs, low wages and poor housing. Some feared machines would take their jobs.

Example: Women attacked spinning jennies in some places.

In real life: Labour rights grew from these struggles.

💡 Did you know? Seasonal work meant months without income.

🇮🇳Industrialisation in India

The East India Company used gomasthas to control weavers. Indian textiles declined as Manchester goods flooded markets. The first cotton mill in Bombay came up in 1854; Indian industrialists included Dwarkanath Tagore and Jamsetjee Jeejeebhoy.

Example: Jobbers recruited workers for mills.

In real life: Swadeshi revived Indian industry.

💡 Did you know? Handloom production still survived and even grew in some areas.

📣Market for Goods

Advertisements, labels and calendars created markets. Indian producers used images of gods and nationalist messages to promote swadeshi goods.

Example: 'Made in Manchester' labels signalled quality.

In real life: Brand advertising is everywhere today.

💡 Did you know? Calendars kept ads in homes all year.

Q1. Production before factories for international markets was

  1. Globalisation
  2. Feudalism
  3. Proto-industrialisation
  4. Urbanisation

Q2. The spinning jenny was invented by

  1. Henry Ford
  2. James Watt
  3. James Hargreaves

Q3. James Watt is known for improving the

  1. Steam engine
  2. Spinning jenny

Q4. Gomasthas were

  1. Factory owners
  2. Bankers
  3. Company agents supervising weavers
