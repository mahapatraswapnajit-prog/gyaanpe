Class 10 Social Science Chapter 6: Geography: Resources and Development

NCERT Social Science · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter classifies resources, explains sustainable development and resource planning, land use and degradation, India's soil types and methods of soil conservation.

Key concepts

🌍Resources

Anything that satisfies needs and is available, technologically usable and economically feasible is a resource. Resources can be classified by origin (biotic/abiotic), exhaustibility (renewable/non-renewable), ownership and status of development.

Example: Solar energy is renewable; coal is non-renewable.

In real life: Recycling stretches resources.

💡 Did you know? Potential resources exist but are not yet used fully, like wind in Rajasthan.

♻️Sustainable Development

Development that meets present needs without harming future generations. The Rio Earth Summit (1992) adopted Agenda 21.

Example: Gandhi said there is enough for everybody's need, not for anybody's greed.

In real life: Rainwater harvesting is sustainable.

💡 Did you know? Resource planning in India is done region-wise.

🗺️Land Use

Land is used for forests, agriculture, pasture, settlements and more. Ideally about 33% area should be under forest (National Forest Policy).

Example: Land degradation is caused by mining, overgrazing and over-irrigation.

In real life: Afforestation fights degradation.

💡 Did you know? Punjab and Haryana have very high net sown area.

🟫Soil Types

Alluvial (most widespread, fertile, northern plains), black/regur (cotton, Deccan, retains moisture), red and yellow (iron), laterite (heavy rain, leached, good for cashew with care), arid and forest soils.

Example: Black soil is ideal for cotton.

In real life: Soil cards help farmers.

💡 Did you know? Bangar is old alluvium; khadar is new alluvium.

🌱Soil Conservation

Erosion by wind and water forms gullies and bad lands (Chambal ravines). Contour ploughing, terrace farming, strip cropping and shelter belts conserve soil.

Example: Shelter belts of trees protect soil in western India.

In real life: Terraces on hills save soil.

💡 Did you know? Ploughing down slopes worsens erosion.
