Class 4 Maths Chapter 1: Shapes Around Us

NCERT Maths Mela · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Shapes can be flat (2D) like triangles and pentagons, or solid (3D) like cubes and cylinders. Flat shapes have sides and corners; solids have faces, edges and corners. Buildings combine shapes, and nets fold into solid shapes.

Key concepts

🔷2D Shapes

Flat shapes have only length and breadth.

Example: Triangle, square, rectangle, circle, pentagon.

In real life: Road signs are flat shapes.

💡 Did you know? A pentagon has 5 sides; a hexagon has 6.

📦3D Shapes

Solid shapes have length, breadth and height.

Example: Cube, cuboid, cylinder, cone, sphere.

In real life: Boxes, balls and cans are 3D.

💡 Did you know? A cuboid has 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 corners.

📐Sides, Edges and Corners

Flat shapes have sides and corners; solids have faces, edges and corners.

Example: A triangle has 3 sides and 3 corners.

In real life: Carpenters count edges when making boxes.

💡 Did you know? A sphere has no edges or corners.

🧱Shapes in Buildings

Buildings use many shapes together.

Example: Bricks are cuboids; domes are half spheres.

In real life: Temple shikharas are cone-like.

💡 Did you know? Strong roofs often use triangles.

✂️Nets

A net is a flat shape that folds into a solid.

Example: Six squares can fold into a cube.

In real life: Cardboard boxes start as nets.

💡 Did you know? Not every arrangement of 6 squares folds into a cube.

Key terms

Quick revision

  • Cuboid: 6 faces, 12 edges, 8 corners.
  • Pentagon: 5 sides.
  • Six squares can make a cube net.
