Class 5 Maths Chapter 13: Animal Jumps

NCERT Maths Mela · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Animals jumping equal steps show multiples, and places both animals land are common multiples. Factors divide a number exactly, primes have only two factors, and divisibility rules help check quickly.

Key concepts

🐸Jumping on a Number Line

Animals jumping equal steps show multiples.

Example: A frog jumping 3 steps: 3, 6, 9, 12.

In real life: Hopscotch uses jumps.

💡 Did you know? These are multiples of 3.

🦘Common Multiples

Numbers landed on by two animals are common multiples.

Example: Frog (3) and rabbit (4) both land on 12, 24.

In real life: Two bells ringing together.

💡 Did you know? 12 is the smallest common multiple of 3 and 4.

🔢Factors

Factors of a number divide it exactly.

Example: Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12.

In real life: Arranging 12 chairs in rows.

💡 Did you know? 1 is a factor of every number.

⭐Prime Numbers

A prime has exactly two factors: 1 and itself.

Example: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13.

In real life: Primes are building blocks of numbers.

💡 Did you know? 2 is the only even prime.

✅Divisibility

Quick tests show if a number divides exactly.

Example: Even numbers are divisible by 2; numbers ending in 0 or 5 by 5.

In real life: Sharing equally.

💡 Did you know? Numbers ending in 0 are divisible by 10.
