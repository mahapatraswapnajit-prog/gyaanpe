Class 5 Maths Chapter 5: Far and Near

NCERT Maths Mela · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Maps show places at a smaller size, and the scale tells real distance: measure on the map and multiply by the scale. We estimate distances by steps or time, notice that far things look smaller, and choose suitable units.

Key concepts

🗺️Maps and Scale

A map shows places smaller than real. The scale tells real distance.

Example: Scale 1 cm = 10 km: 5 cm on the map = 50 km.

In real life: Road atlases use scales.

💡 Did you know? Without a scale, map distances can't be used.

📏Measuring on Maps

Measure map distance with a ruler, then multiply by the scale.

Example: 3 cm × 20 km = 60 km.

In real life: Planning a cycle trip.

💡 Did you know? Curvy roads are longer than straight-line distance.

🔭Estimating Distance

We can estimate distance by steps or time.

Example: 1,000 normal steps ≈ about 700–800 m.

In real life: Morning walk distances.

💡 Did you know? Walking speed is about 4–5 km per hour.

🧭Near and Far

Things far away look smaller.

Example: A plane high in the sky looks tiny.

In real life: Artists draw far things smaller.

💡 Did you know? Mountains far away look blue and small.

🔄Units

Use km for long distances, m for short, cm for tiny ones.

Example: School to home: 2 km; classroom length: 8 m.

In real life: Choosing the right unit.

💡 Did you know? 1 km = 1000 m.

  • Real distance = map distance × scale.
  • 1 km = 1000 m.
  • Far things look smaller.

  2. 100 m
  3. 1000 m

  1. Bigger
  2. Same
  3. Smaller
