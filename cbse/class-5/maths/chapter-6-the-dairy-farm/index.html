Class 5 Maths Chapter 6: The Dairy Farm

NCERT Maths Mela · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

At a dairy farm we multiply to find total milk and money, divide to pack milk equally, and handle remainders. Breaking numbers apart makes multiplication easier.

Key concepts

🐄Milk Every Day

Dairy farms collect milk from cows and buffaloes daily.

Example: A cow giving 12 L a day gives 84 L in a week.

In real life: Milk cooperatives like OMFED in Odisha.

💡 Did you know? India is the world's largest milk producer.

✖️Multiplying Big Numbers

Multiply to find totals of equal amounts.

Example: 25 cows × 12 L = 300 L.

In real life: Calculating daily milk.

💡 Did you know? Break numbers: 25 × 12 = 25 × 10 + 25 × 2.

➗Dividing Equally

Divide to share or pack equally.

Example: 300 L into 1 L packets = 300 packets; 300 ÷ 5 = 60 cans of 5 L.

In real life: Packing milk in pouches.

💡 Did you know? Division is the opposite of multiplication.

💰Money from Milk

Multiply litres by price.

Example: 40 L × ₹50 = ₹2,000.

In real life: Dairy accounts.

💡 Did you know? Farmers get paid by quantity and quality.

🧮Remainders

Sometimes division leaves a remainder.

Example: 50 L in 3 L cans: 16 cans, 2 L left.

In real life: Leftover milk becomes curd.

💡 Did you know? Check: 16 × 3 + 2 = 50.

Key terms

TermMeaning
MultiplyFind total of equal groups
DivideShare equally
ProductResult of multiplying
QuotientResult of dividing
RemainderLeftover

Quick revision

  • India is the largest milk producer.
  • 25 × 12 = 300.
  • Check division: quotient × divisor + remainder.
