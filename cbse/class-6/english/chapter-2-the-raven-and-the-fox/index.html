Class 6 English Chapter 2: The Raven and the Fox

NCERT Poorvi · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

In this fable, a fox uses flattery to trick a raven, teaching us to think before trusting sweet words. The chapter practises adjectives and their degrees of comparison.

Key concepts

🦊Theme: Beware of Flattery

In this fable, a clever fox flatters a raven to get its food. Flattery means praising someone falsely to get something.

Example: Believing sweet words without thinking can make us lose.

In real life: Advertisements sometimes flatter buyers.

💡 Did you know? Fables use animals to teach lessons.

🎨Adjectives

Adjectives describe nouns.

Example: a clever fox, a black raven, a sweet voice

In real life: We use adjectives to describe things we buy.

💡 Did you know? Adjectives answer 'what kind?', 'how many?', 'which?'

📊Degrees of Comparison

Adjectives have positive, comparative and superlative forms.

Example: clever → cleverer → cleverest; good → better → best

In real life: Comparing prices and sizes.

💡 Did you know? Long adjectives use more/most: more beautiful.

🗣️Fables

A fable is a short story, often with animals, that teaches a lesson.

Example: Aesop and the Panchatantra have many fables.

In real life: Bedtime stories.

💡 Did you know? The Panchatantra was written in ancient India.

🧠Think Before You Act

Stop and think when someone praises you too much while wanting something.

Example: Ask: why is this person saying this?

In real life: Online scams use flattery.

💡 Did you know? Thinking carefully protects us from tricks.
