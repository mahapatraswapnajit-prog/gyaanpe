Class 6 Maths Chapter 1: Patterns in Mathematics

NCERT Ganita Prakash · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Mathematics studies patterns. Number sequences such as odd, even, square, triangular and cube numbers follow clear rules and can be drawn as shapes. For example, the sum of the first n odd numbers is always n × n. Shape sequences, like polygons, show patterns too.

Key concepts

🔢Number Sequences

A sequence is a list of numbers following a rule.

Example: Odd numbers: 1, 3, 5, 7, ...; even numbers: 2, 4, 6, 8, ...

In real life: Seat numbers in a theatre follow patterns.

💡 Did you know? Mathematics is often called the study of patterns.

🟦Square Numbers

Square numbers come from multiplying a number by itself: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, ...

Example: Dots arranged as a 4 × 4 square make 16.

In real life: Square tiles on a floor show square numbers.

💡 Did you know? 5 × 5 = 25 is the fifth square number.

🔺Triangular Numbers

Triangular numbers can be arranged as triangles: 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, ...

Example: Each new row adds one more dot: 1, 1+2, 1+2+3, ...

In real life: Bowling pins are arranged in a triangle of 10.

💡 Did you know? Two consecutive triangular numbers add up to a square number.

➕Adding Odd Numbers

The sum of the first n odd numbers is n × n.

Example: 1 + 3 + 5 + 7 = 16 = 4 × 4.

In real life: Visual patterns make sums easy to remember.

💡 Did you know? This pattern can be seen by building squares layer by layer.

🧊Cube Numbers and Shapes

Cube numbers come from n × n × n: 1, 8, 27, 64, ...

Example: A 3 × 3 × 3 block of cubes has 27 cubes.

In real life: Rubik's cube shows 27 small cubes.

💡 Did you know? Shapes also form sequences, like regular polygons with 3, 4, 5, ... sides.

Key terms

TermMeaning
SequenceNumbers following a rule
Square numbern × n
Triangular number1 + 2 + ... + n
Cube numbern × n × n

Quick revision

  • Square numbers: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25.
  • Triangular numbers: 1, 3, 6, 10, 15.
  • Sum of first n odd numbers = n × n.
