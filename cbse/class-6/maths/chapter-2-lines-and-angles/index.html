Class 6 Maths Chapter 2: Lines and Angles

NCERT Ganita Prakash · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Points, lines, rays and line segments are the building blocks of geometry. An angle is formed by two rays meeting at a vertex and is measured in degrees with a protractor. Angles are acute (less than 90°), right (90°), obtuse (between 90° and 180°), straight (180°) or reflex (more than 180°).

Key concepts

📍Point, Line, Ray, Segment

A point marks a position. A line segment has two endpoints, a ray has one endpoint, and a line goes on forever both ways.

Example: Segment AB, ray AB starting at A, line AB.

In real life: A torch beam is like a ray.

💡 Did you know? A line has no endpoints.

📐Angles

An angle is formed by two rays with a common endpoint called the vertex.

Example: The hands of a clock form an angle.

In real life: Doors open at different angles.

💡 Did you know? Angles are measured in degrees (°).

🟩Right and Straight Angles

A right angle is 90°; a straight angle is 180°; a full turn is 360°.

Example: The corner of a book is a right angle.

In real life: Wall corners are right angles.

💡 Did you know? Two right angles make a straight angle.

🔻Acute and Obtuse

An acute angle is less than 90°. An obtuse angle is more than 90° but less than 180°.

Example: 60° is acute; 120° is obtuse.

In real life: Scissors opened slightly make an acute angle.

💡 Did you know? An angle more than 180° and less than 360° is a reflex angle.

📏Measuring Angles

A protractor measures angles in degrees.

Example: Place the centre on the vertex and the base along one arm.

In real life: Engineers use angles when designing ramps.

💡 Did you know? A protractor usually shows 0° to 180°.

Key terms

TermMeaning
RayPart of a line with one endpoint
VertexCommon endpoint of an angle's arms
Right angle90°
Acute angleLess than 90°
Obtuse angleBetween 90° and 180°

Quick revision

  • Right angle = 90°; straight angle = 180°.
  • A full turn = 360°.
  • A line segment has two endpoints.
