Class 6 Maths Chapter 7: Fractions

NCERT Ganita Prakash · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

A fraction shows parts of a whole divided into equal parts. Fractions can be placed on a number line, written in equivalent forms like 1/2 = 2/4, and simplified to lowest terms. To compare, add or subtract fractions, make denominators equal first. Mixed fractions combine whole numbers with fractions.

Key concepts

🍰Fractional Units

When a whole is split into equal parts, each part is a fractional unit like 1/2, 1/3, 1/4.

Example: 3/4 means 3 parts of size 1/4.

In real life: Sharing a roti equally uses fractions.

💡 Did you know? The bottom number (denominator) tells how many equal parts.

📍Fractions on a Number Line

Fractions can be placed between whole numbers on a number line.

Example: 1/2 lies halfway between 0 and 1.

In real life: Measuring tapes show halves and quarters.

💡 Did you know? Every fraction has an exact place on the number line.

🔁Equivalent Fractions

Fractions that show the same amount are equivalent.

Example: 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6.

In real life: Half a pizza equals two quarters.

💡 Did you know? Multiply or divide numerator and denominator by the same number.

⚖️Comparing Fractions

With the same denominator, the larger numerator is bigger. With the same numerator, the smaller denominator is bigger.

Example: 3/7 > 2/7; 1/3 > 1/5.

In real life: Comparing shares of a cake uses this.

💡 Did you know? Converting to equal denominators helps compare.

➕Adding and Subtracting

Make denominators equal, then add or subtract numerators.

Example: 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4; 1/2 + 1/3 = 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.

In real life: Recipes add fractions of cups.

💡 Did you know? Brahmagupta described rules for fractions centuries ago.

Key terms

TermMeaning
NumeratorTop number of a fraction
DenominatorNumber of equal parts

Quick revision

  • 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6.
  • Same numerator: smaller denominator is bigger.
  • Equal denominators before adding.
