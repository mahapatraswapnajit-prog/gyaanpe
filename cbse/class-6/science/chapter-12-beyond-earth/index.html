Class 6 Science Chapter 12: Beyond Earth

NCERT Curiosity · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Stars are hot glowing gas balls, and the Sun is our nearest star. Patterns of stars are constellations, like the Saptarishi, which points to the nearly fixed Pole Star. The solar system has the Sun, eight planets, their moons, and an asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter. The Moon is Earth's natural satellite, and ISRO explores space with missions like Chandrayaan-3 and Aditya-L1.

Key concepts

✨Stars

Stars are huge balls of hot gas that make their own light. The Sun is a star.

Example: The Sun looks big because it is the nearest star to Earth.

In real life: Sailors once used stars to find direction.

💡 Did you know? Stars twinkle because their light passes through moving air.

🌌Constellations

Groups of stars forming patterns are constellations.

Example: Saptarishi (Ursa Major) and Orion are well-known constellations.

In real life: Two stars of the Saptarishi point towards the Pole Star.

💡 Did you know? The Pole Star (Dhruva Tara) stays almost fixed in the northern sky.

🪐The Solar System

The Sun and the eight planets with their moons and other bodies form the solar system.

Example: Mercury is closest to the Sun; Jupiter is the largest planet.

In real life: Saturn's rings can be seen with a telescope.

💡 Did you know? An asteroid belt lies between Mars and Jupiter.

🌍Earth and Moon

Earth is the only known planet with life. The Moon is Earth's natural satellite and reflects sunlight.

Example: The Moon has no air or water on its surface to support life.

In real life: Chandrayaan-3 landed near the Moon's south pole in 2023.

💡 Did you know? Astronauts left footprints on the Moon that remain for a long time.

🚀Exploring Space

Telescopes and spacecraft help us study space. ISRO is India's space agency.

Example: Aditya-L1 studies the Sun.

In real life: GPS in phones uses satellites.

💡 Did you know? Artificial satellites help with TV, weather and maps.
