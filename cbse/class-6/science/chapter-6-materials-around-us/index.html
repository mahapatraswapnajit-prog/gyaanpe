Class 6 Science Chapter 6: Materials Around Us

NCERT Curiosity · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Objects are made from materials that have different properties: appearance, hardness, solubility, transparency and whether they float or sink. Grouping materials by these properties helps us choose the right material for each use, such as transparent glass for windows and hard metals for tools.

Key concepts

🧱What are Materials?

Objects are made of materials like wood, glass, plastic, metal and cloth.

Example: A chair can be made of wood, plastic or metal.

In real life: Builders choose materials by their properties.

💡 Did you know? The same material can make many objects.

✨Appearance and Hardness

Some materials are shiny (lustrous), like metals; some are hard, like iron, and some soft, like cotton.

Example: Gold jewellery shines.

In real life: Cutting tools are made of hard materials.

💡 Did you know? Hard materials resist scratching.

💧Soluble or Insoluble

Some materials dissolve in water (soluble), others do not (insoluble).

Example: Sugar and salt dissolve; sand and chalk powder do not.

In real life: Medicines are often dissolved in water.

💡 Did you know? Some liquids mix with water; oil does not.

🪟Transparent, Translucent, Opaque

We can see clearly through transparent materials, partly through translucent, and not at all through opaque materials.

Example: Glass is transparent, butter paper translucent, wood opaque.

In real life: Frosted glass gives privacy.

💡 Did you know? Window glass is chosen because it is transparent.

🛶Floating and Sinking

Some materials float on water while others sink.

Example: Wood and a leaf float; a stone and an iron nail sink.

In real life: Life jackets help people float.

💡 Did you know? Big ships float due to their shape even though made of iron.

Quick revision

  • Sugar is soluble; sand is insoluble.
  • Glass is transparent; wood is opaque.
  • Grouping by properties helps choose materials.
