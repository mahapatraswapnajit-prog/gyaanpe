Class 6 Science Chapter 7: Temperature and its Measurement

NCERT Curiosity · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Our sense of touch cannot tell temperature reliably, so we use thermometers. Clinical thermometers (35–42°C) measure body temperature, which is normally about 37°C. Laboratory thermometers have a wider range for objects. Digital thermometers are safer than mercury ones. Water freezes at 0°C and boils at 100°C.

Key concepts

🌡️Hot and Cold

Touch can be misleading; temperature tells exactly how hot or cold something is.

Example: Water may feel warm to a cold hand and cool to a warm hand.

In real life: Weather reports give temperature in °C.

💡 Did you know? Temperature is measured in degrees Celsius (°C).

🤒Clinical Thermometer

A clinical thermometer measures body temperature, usually from 35°C to 42°C.

Example: Normal human body temperature is about 37°C.

In real life: Doctors check for fever with it.

💡 Did you know? It has a kink so the reading does not fall quickly.

🔬Laboratory Thermometer

A laboratory thermometer has a wider range, often −10°C to 110°C, and is used for other objects, not the body.

Example: It measures the temperature of boiling water (100°C).

In real life: Science labs use it for experiments.

💡 Did you know? It must be kept upright and read while in the liquid.

📟Digital Thermometers

Digital thermometers show the reading on a screen and are safer than mercury ones.

Example: Mercury is poisonous, so many places now use digital thermometers.

In real life: Shops used infrared thermometers during the pandemic.

💡 Did you know? Infrared thermometers measure temperature without touching.

✅Using Thermometers Safely

Clean the thermometer, shake down the reading, and read at eye level.

Example: Don't hold it by the bulb when reading.

In real life: Cooking and medicine both rely on correct temperature.

💡 Did you know? Water freezes at 0°C and boils at 100°C at normal pressure.

Key terms

Quick revision

  • Normal body temperature ≈ 37°C.
  • Water freezes at 0°C, boils at 100°C.
  • Clinical range: 35–42°C.
