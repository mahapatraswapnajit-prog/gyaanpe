Class 6 Social Science Chapter 1: Locating Places on the Earth

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Globes and maps help us locate places. Maps use directions, scale and symbols. Latitudes run east–west, with the Equator at 0°, and longitudes run pole to pole, with the Prime Meridian at 0° through Greenwich. Together they form a grid that pinpoints any place on Earth.

Key concepts

🗺️Maps and Globes

A globe is a small model of the Earth; a map is a flat drawing of the Earth or part of it.

Example: A school atlas has many maps.

In real life: Phones show digital maps.

💡 Did you know? A globe shows true shapes; maps are easier to carry.

🧭Directions

The four main directions are north, south, east and west.

Example: Maps usually have north at the top.

In real life: A compass helps find north.

💡 Did you know? North-east lies between north and east.

📏Scale and Symbols

A scale tells how much real distance a map distance shows; symbols show features like roads and rivers.

Example: 1 cm on a map may stand for 1 km on the ground.

In real life: Tourist maps use simple symbols.

💡 Did you know? Blue is used for water, green for forests.

🌐Latitudes

Imaginary lines running east–west around the globe are latitudes. The Equator is 0°.

Example: The Tropic of Cancer passes through India.

In real life: Places near the Equator are hot.

💡 Did you know? The Equator divides Earth into Northern and Southern Hemispheres.

🕒Longitudes

Imaginary lines from pole to pole are longitudes. The Prime Meridian (0°) passes through Greenwich, near London.

Example: Latitudes and longitudes form a grid to locate any place.

In real life: GPS gives location as latitude and longitude.

💡 Did you know? Indian Standard Time is based on 82½° E longitude.
