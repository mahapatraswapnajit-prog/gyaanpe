Class 6 Social Science Chapter 11: Grassroots Democracy — Part 2: Local Government in Rural Areas

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Villages are governed through Panchayati Raj, with three tiers: Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti and Zila Parishad. The Gram Sabha, made of all adult voters, approves plans and checks spending. The Sarpanch heads the Gram Panchayat, and at least one-third of seats are reserved for women. Panchayats manage local needs like water, roads and sanitation.

Key concepts

🏡Panchayati Raj

Panchayati Raj is the system of local self-government in villages.

Example: It has three tiers: Gram Panchayat (village), Panchayat Samiti (block) and Zila Parishad (district).

In real life: Your village roads and wells are often managed by the panchayat.

💡 Did you know? The 73rd Amendment (1992) gave panchayats constitutional status.

👥Gram Sabha

All adult voters of a village form the Gram Sabha, which discusses and approves panchayat plans.

Example: The Gram Sabha checks how money was spent.

In real life: Meetings happen a few times a year.

💡 Did you know? It is the foundation of grassroots democracy.

🧑‍💼Gram Panchayat and Sarpanch

The Gram Panchayat is elected by villagers and headed by the Sarpanch.

Example: Ward members represent parts of the village.

In real life: Many women sarpanches lead villages today.

💡 Did you know? At least one-third of seats are reserved for women.

🔧Work of Panchayats

Panchayats look after drinking water, roads, sanitation, street lights and local records.

Example: Building a village pond or road.

In real life: Panchayats help run government schemes.

💡 Did you know? They get funds from government and local taxes.

🗣️People's Participation

Villagers can raise problems, suggest plans and hold leaders accountable.

Example: A Gram Sabha may choose where to dig a new well.

In real life: Social audits check panchayat spending.

💡 Did you know? Participation makes decisions fairer.
