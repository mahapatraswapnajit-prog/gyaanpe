Class 6 Social Science Chapter 12: Grassroots Democracy — Part 3: Local Government in Urban Areas

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Urban areas are governed by local bodies: Municipal Corporations for big cities and Municipal Councils for smaller towns. Cities are divided into wards that elect councillors, the Mayor heads the corporation, and a Commissioner manages daily work. They provide water, sanitation, roads and lights, funded partly by taxes, while citizens help by keeping cities clean.

Key concepts

🏙️Urban Local Bodies

Cities and towns are governed by urban local bodies.

Example: Large cities have a Municipal Corporation; smaller towns have a Municipal Council.

In real life: Your city's garbage collection is managed by them.

💡 Did you know? Bhubaneswar has a Municipal Corporation.

🗳️Wards and Councillors

A city is divided into wards, and each ward elects a councillor.

Example: Councillors raise local problems in meetings.

In real life: You can approach your ward councillor with complaints.

💡 Did you know? The 74th Amendment (1992) gave urban bodies constitutional status.

🎖️Mayor and Commissioner

The Mayor heads the Municipal Corporation; the Commissioner, a government officer, manages daily work.

Example: The Mayor chairs council meetings.

In real life: City budgets are discussed in council meetings.

💡 Did you know? Elected members decide; officials implement.

🚰Services

Urban bodies provide water supply, garbage collection, drains, street lights, parks and roads.

Example: Waste segregation into wet and dry helps cities.

In real life: Swachh Bharat Mission promotes clean cities.

💡 Did you know? They collect property tax and other fees.

🙋Citizens' Role

Citizens should pay taxes, keep the city clean and report problems.

Example: Using dustbins and not littering.

In real life: Resident welfare associations help neighbourhoods.

💡 Did you know? Many cities have apps to report issues.
