Class 6 Social Science Chapter 14: Economic Activities Around Us

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Economic activities fall into three sectors: primary (using natural resources, like farming, fishing and mining), secondary (manufacturing goods from raw materials, like making steel or cloth) and tertiary (services such as transport, banking, health and teaching). The sectors depend on each other, and we can see all three around us.

Key concepts

🌾Primary Sector

The primary sector uses natural resources directly: farming, fishing, mining and forestry.

Example: A fisherman in Puri catching fish.

In real life: Odisha's mines supply iron ore.

💡 Did you know? Agriculture employs a large number of Indians.

🏭Secondary Sector

The secondary sector turns raw materials into goods through manufacturing.

Example: Cotton → cloth; iron ore → steel.

In real life: Steel plants in Rourkela belong here.

💡 Did you know? Factories are part of the secondary sector.

🛎️Tertiary Sector

The tertiary sector provides services like transport, banking, teaching, health and trade.

Example: A bus driver, a doctor and a shopkeeper offer services.

In real life: Online services are part of this sector.

💡 Did you know? This sector has grown fast in India.

🔗Interdependence

The three sectors depend on each other.

Example: Farmers need tractors from factories; factories need transport; shops sell the goods.

In real life: A truck strike can raise vegetable prices.

💡 Did you know? A break in one sector affects the others.

🏘️Activities Around Us

Looking around our village or city, we can find all three sectors at work.

Example: Rice farming, a rice mill and a grocery shop in one area.

In real life: Surveying your street teaches economics.

💡 Did you know? Many families work in more than one sector.

Quick revision

  • Farming and fishing: primary.
  • Factories: secondary.
  • Banking and teaching: tertiary.
