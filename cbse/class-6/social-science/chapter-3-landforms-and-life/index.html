Class 6 Social Science Chapter 3: Landforms and Life

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Earth has many landforms: high mountains like the Himalayas, raised flat plateaus rich in minerals, low fertile plains, and dry deserts like the Thar. People adapt their homes, food, clothing and work to these landforms, and most people live in plains where farming and transport are easier.

Key concepts

🏔️Mountains

Mountains are high landforms with steep slopes and peaks.

Example: The Himalayas are young and very high mountains.

In real life: People in mountains grow crops on step-like terraces.

💡 Did you know? Mount Everest is the highest peak in the world.

⛰️Plateaus

Plateaus are raised, mostly flat lands.

Example: The Deccan Plateau is rich in minerals.

In real life: Chota Nagpur Plateau has coal and iron ore.

💡 Did you know? Plateaus often have waterfalls.

🌾Plains

Plains are low, flat lands often formed by rivers, good for farming.

Example: The Ganga plains are very fertile.

In real life: Roads and railways are easy to build on plains.

💡 Did you know? Most people live in plains.

🏜️Deserts

Deserts get very little rain and have few plants.

Example: The Thar Desert in Rajasthan.

In real life: Camels are called 'ships of the desert'.

💡 Did you know? Desert people store water carefully.

🏞️Life Adapts

People adapt their homes, food, clothes and work to landforms and climate.

Example: Houses in hills have sloping roofs; desert homes have thick walls.

In real life: Fishing villages grow along coasts.

💡 Did you know? Rivers have always attracted human settlements.
