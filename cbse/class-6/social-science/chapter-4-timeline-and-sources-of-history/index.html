Class 6 Social Science Chapter 4: Timeline and Sources of History

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Timelines arrange events in order, using BCE for years before the Common Era and CE for after. Historians learn about the past from sources: archaeological remains like tools and buildings, written sources like inscriptions and manuscripts, coins, and oral traditions. Comparing many sources helps build a reliable picture of history.

Key concepts

📅Timelines

A timeline shows events in the order they happened.

Example: Your own timeline: birth, first school day, Class 6.

In real life: Museums display history on timelines.

💡 Did you know? Events are placed at proper distances on a timeline.

🔢BCE and CE

Years before the Common Era are BCE; years after are CE. BCE years count backwards.

Example: 500 BCE is earlier than 100 BCE.

In real life: Calendars help date events.

💡 Did you know? There is no year zero; 1 BCE is followed by 1 CE.

🏺Archaeological Sources

Archaeologists study remains like tools, pots, buildings, bones and seals.

Example: Excavations at Harappa revealed an ancient city.

In real life: Museums show excavated objects.

💡 Did you know? Archaeology means studying the past through material remains.

📜Written Sources

Inscriptions on stone or metal and manuscripts on palm leaves or birch bark are written sources.

Example: Ashoka's inscriptions tell us about his rule.

In real life: Libraries preserve old manuscripts.

💡 Did you know? Odisha has a rich tradition of palm-leaf manuscripts.

🪙Coins and Oral Traditions

Coins tell us about rulers, trade and beliefs; stories and songs passed down also carry history.

Example: Gupta gold coins show kings playing musical instruments.

In real life: Grandparents' stories preserve local history.

💡 Did you know? Historians check many sources before concluding.

Quick revision

  • BCE years count backwards.
  • Archaeologists study material remains.
  • Inscriptions and coins are important sources.
