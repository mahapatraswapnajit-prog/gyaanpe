Class 6 Social Science Chapter 5: India, That Is Bharat

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Our country has many names. The Constitution says 'India, that is Bharat'. Bharata or Bharatavarsha appears in ancient texts, Jambudvipa was another old name, and 'India' came from 'Sindhu', the Indus river, through Persian and Greek. These names reflect a shared identity across many regions.

Key concepts

🇮🇳Names of Our Country

Our Constitution begins: 'India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States.'

Example: Both 'India' and 'Bharat' are official names.

In real life: Our passports say 'Republic of India' and 'Bharat Ganarajya'.

💡 Did you know? The name Bharat is very old and appears in ancient texts.

📜Bharata and Jambudvipa

Ancient texts called the land Bharata or Bharatavarsha; Jambudvipa was another old name.

Example: The Vishnu Purana describes Bharata as the land north of the ocean and south of the snowy mountains.

In real life: Prayers in temples sometimes mention Jambudvipa.

💡 Did you know? Names show how people imagined the whole land.

🏞️India from Sindhu

The name 'India' comes from 'Sindhu', the Indus river. Persians said 'Hindu' and Greeks said 'Indos'.

Example: 'Hindustan' was used in later centuries.

In real life: The Indus flows through Ladakh and Pakistan.

💡 Did you know? Rivers often gave names to regions.

🤝One Land, Many Regions

Many regions with different languages saw themselves as part of a larger land.

Example: Pilgrims travelled from Kashmir to Kanyakumari.

In real life: The Char Dham link four corners of India.

💡 Did you know? Shared stories and festivals connected people.

🗺️Name and Identity

A name reflects a people's history and shared identity.

Example: Both names express pride in our heritage.

In real life: School textbooks use both India and Bharat.

💡 Did you know? Many countries have more than one name.
