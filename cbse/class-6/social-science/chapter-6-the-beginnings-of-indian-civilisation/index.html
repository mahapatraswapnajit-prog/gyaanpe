Class 6 Social Science Chapter 6: The Beginnings of Indian Civilisation

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Around 2600 BCE, the Harappan or Indus-Sarasvati civilisation built planned cities like Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Lothal and Dholavira with straight streets, drains and standard bricks. People farmed, crafted seals, beads and bronze, and traded far away. The cities declined around 1900 BCE, but many traditions continued.

Key concepts

🏙️The Harappan Civilisation

Around 2600 BCE, well-planned cities grew in the Indus and Sarasvati river region. This is called the Harappan or Indus-Sarasvati civilisation.

Example: Harappa and Mohenjo-daro are famous sites.

In real life: Sites are now in India and Pakistan.

💡 Did you know? It was one of the world's earliest urban civilisations.

📐Planned Cities

Cities had straight streets, brick houses and covered drains.

Example: Mohenjo-daro had a Great Bath.

In real life: Modern town planning uses similar ideas.

💡 Did you know? Bricks were of standard size across sites.

⚓Lothal and Dholavira

Lothal in Gujarat had a dockyard; Dholavira had clever water reservoirs.

Example: Dholavira is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

In real life: Ports today follow similar trade routes.

💡 Did you know? Harappans traded with Mesopotamia.

🔏Seals and Crafts

Harappans made seals with animals and script, beads, pottery and bronze objects.

Example: The 'Dancing Girl' is a famous bronze figure.

In real life: Museums display Harappan seals.

💡 Did you know? The Harappan script has not yet been deciphered.

🌾Farming and Decline

Farmers grew wheat, barley and cotton. Around 1900 BCE cities declined, possibly due to climate change and rivers drying.

Example: Cotton cloth was produced very early here.

In real life: Weights and measures show organised trade.

💡 Did you know? People moved to villages but traditions continued.
