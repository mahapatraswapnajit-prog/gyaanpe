Class 6 Social Science Chapter 9: Family and Community

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

A family is a group of related people who care for each other, whether joint or nuclear, and members share roles and responsibilities. Communities are larger groups that help one another and share resources. Values like cooperation, respect and care for elders strengthen them, and the ancient idea 'Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam' sees the whole world as one family.

Key concepts

👨‍👩‍👧Family

A family is a group of people related to each other who care for one another.

Example: A joint family has grandparents, parents and children together; a nuclear family has parents and children.

In real life: Grandparents share stories and traditions.

💡 Did you know? Families teach us values and language.

🧹Roles and Responsibilities

Family members share work and care for each other.

Example: Children can help by keeping their things tidy.

In real life: Families cook and eat together.

💡 Did you know? Sharing household work is fair for everyone.

🏘️Community

A community is a larger group of people living together and helping each other.

Example: Neighbours help during festivals or emergencies.

In real life: Village festivals bring the community together.

💡 Did you know? Communities share wells, schools and temples.

🤲Values

Cooperation, respect, sharing and caring for elders make families and communities strong.

Example: Helping a neighbour carry heavy bags.

In real life: Community kitchens feed many people.

💡 Did you know? Kindness to all, including animals, is valued in Indian tradition.

🌏Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam

The idea 'the world is one family' comes from ancient Indian texts.

Example: It encourages caring for people beyond our own families.

In real life: Volunteering extends our family to society.

💡 Did you know? India used this phrase as the theme of its G20 presidency.

Key terms

Quick revision

  • Joint family: several generations together.
  • Community: people helping each other.
  • Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam: the world is one family.
