Class 7 English Chapter 3: Three Days to See

NCERT Poorvi · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Helen Keller, who was deaf and blind, imagines what she would see with three days of sight, urging us to value our senses. The chapter introduces her life, essay writing and the second conditional for imaginary situations.

Key concepts

👁️Theme: Value of Our Senses

In this essay, Helen Keller, who was deaf and blind, imagines what she would see if she had sight for just three days.

Example: She urges us to use our senses fully and not take them for granted.

In real life: Appreciating small sights around us.

💡 Did you know? Helen Keller became an author and activist.

📚Helen Keller

Helen Keller (1880–1968) lost her sight and hearing as a baby, learnt to read and write with her teacher Anne Sullivan, and graduated from college.

Example: She learnt words through touch, spelling into her hand.

In real life: Braille helps blind people read.

💡 Did you know? She inspired people with disabilities worldwide.

🔮Second Conditional

Used for imaginary situations: If + past, would + verb.

Example: 'If I had three days to see, I would look at my friends' faces.'

In real life: Dream planning.

💡 Did you know? 'If I were' is used for all subjects in imaginary situations.

✍️Essays

An essay presents thoughts on a topic in paragraphs.

Example: Introduction, body, conclusion.

In real life: School essays.

💡 Did you know? Reflective essays share personal feelings.

🌈Gratitude for Senses

Notice colours, sounds and textures every day.

Example: A sunrise, birdsong, a friend's smile.

In real life: Nature walks.

💡 Did you know? Mindful observation builds gratitude.
