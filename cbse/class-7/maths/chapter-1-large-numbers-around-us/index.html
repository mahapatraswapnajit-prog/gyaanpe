Class 7 Maths Chapter 1: Large Numbers Around Us

NCERT Ganita Prakash · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Large numbers in India are read in thousands, lakhs and crores, while the international system uses thousands, millions and billions. Reading, writing and comparing numbers depends on place value. Rounding to the nearest thousand, lakh or crore helps estimate quickly.

Key concepts

🔢Lakhs and Crores

In the Indian system, 1 lakh = 1,00,000 and 1 crore = 1,00,00,000 (100 lakh).

Example: The population of a small city may be about 12,50,000 (twelve lakh fifty thousand).

In real life: Prices of houses and cars in India are often said in lakhs.

💡 Did you know? 10 lakh make 1 million in the international system.

🌍Indian vs International System

The Indian system groups digits as 3, then 2, 2... from the right; the international system groups in threes.

Example: 45,67,890 (Indian) is written 4,567,890 (international).

In real life: News about world population uses millions and billions.

💡 Did you know? 1 crore = 10 million, and 100 crore = 1 billion.

📏Reading Large Numbers

Read a large number group by group, using the place name of each group.

Example: 3,05,00,200 is read as three crore five lakh two hundred.

In real life: Cheques require the amount to be written in words.

💡 Did you know? A zero in a place means that place has nothing in it, but it still keeps the other digits in position.

⚖️Comparing Large Numbers

The number with more digits is larger; if digits are equal, compare from the leftmost digit.

Example: 7,89,000 > 7,87,999 because 9 thousands > 7 thousands in the first place they differ.

In real life: Comparing the populations of states uses this skill.

💡 Did you know? Writing numbers in a column helps compare place by place.

🎯Estimation

Rounding large numbers to the nearest thousand, lakh or crore gives a quick estimate.

Example: 4,78,345 rounded to the nearest lakh is 5,00,000.

In real life: Organisers estimate the crowd at a cricket match.

💡 Did you know? Estimates are useful when exact numbers are not needed or not known.

  • 1 lakh = 100 thousand.
  • Rounding helps make quick estimates.
