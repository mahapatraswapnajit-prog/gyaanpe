Class 7 Maths Chapter 12: Another Peek Beyond the Point

NCERT Ganita Prakash · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Multiplying decimals by 10, 100 or 1000 moves the decimal point to the right, and dividing moves it left. When multiplying decimals, count the total decimal places. To divide by a decimal, shift the decimal points to make the divisor a whole number. Decimals help convert and compare measurements.

Key concepts

✖️Multiplying by 10, 100, 1000

Multiplying a decimal by 10, 100 or 1000 moves the decimal point 1, 2 or 3 places to the right.

Example: 3.45 × 100 = 345.

In real life: Converting metres to centimetres multiplies by 100.

💡 Did you know? Dividing moves the point to the left: 3.45 ÷ 10 = 0.345.

🔢Decimal × Whole Number

Multiply as whole numbers, then put the same number of decimal places as in the decimal.

Example: 1.25 × 4 = 5.00 = 5.

In real life: Price of 4 pens at ₹12.50 each is ₹50.

💡 Did you know? Estimating first helps place the decimal point.

🧮Decimal × Decimal

Multiply as whole numbers; decimal places in the answer = total decimal places in both numbers.

Example: 0.3 × 0.2 = 0.06.

In real life: Area of a 1.5 m × 0.8 m table = 1.2 sq m.

💡 Did you know? Multiplying by a decimal less than 1 makes the number smaller.

➗Dividing Decimals

To divide by a decimal, shift both points the same number of places to make the divisor a whole number.

Example: 1.2 ÷ 0.4 = 12 ÷ 4 = 3.

In real life: How many 0.25 L glasses from 2 L of juice? 2 ÷ 0.25 = 8.

💡 Did you know? Dividing by a decimal less than 1 gives a bigger answer.

📏Decimals and Measurement

Decimals let us convert units and compare measurements precisely.

Example: 2.5 kg = 2500 g; 0.75 m = 75 cm.

In real life: Shopkeepers weigh vegetables in decimals of a kilogram.

💡 Did you know? 1 m = 100 cm and 1 km = 1000 m.
