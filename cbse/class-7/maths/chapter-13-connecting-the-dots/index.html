Class 7 Maths Chapter 13: Connecting the Dots

NCERT Ganita Prakash · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Statistics helps answer questions whose answers vary. The mean is the sum of values divided by their count, and the median is the middle value of ordered data. Outliers can pull the mean far away while the median stays steady. Dot plots help us see data, means and medians at a glance.

Key concepts

📊Statistical Questions

A statistical question expects answers that vary, so we collect data to answer it.

Example: 'How many hours do students in my class sleep?' is statistical.

In real life: Schools collect data to plan lunch menus.

💡 Did you know? 'How old am I?' is not statistical because it has one answer.

⚖️Mean (Average)

Mean = sum of all values ÷ number of values. It is the 'fair share' value.

Example: Mean of 8, 10, 6, 12 and 9 = 45 ÷ 5 = 9.

In real life: A batsman's average uses the mean.

💡 Did you know? The mean always lies between the smallest and largest values.

📍Median

The median is the middle value when data is arranged in order. For an even count, it is the mean of the two middle values.

Example: Median of 3, 5, 5, 7, 8, 9 = (5 + 7) ÷ 2 = 6.

In real life: House prices are often reported using the median.

💡 Did you know? Half the values are below the median and half above.

🚩Outliers

An outlier is a value far away from the rest. It can change the mean a lot but barely changes the median.

Example: In 5, 6, 6, 7, 31, the median is 6 but the mean is 11.

In real life: One very rich person can make an area's average income look high.

💡 Did you know? When outliers exist, the median often describes data better.

🔵Dot Plots

A dot plot shows each data value as a dot above a number line, making patterns easy to see.

Example: Marks of 10 students can be shown as dots above 0 to 10.

In real life: Coaches use them to see how players score.

💡 Did you know? Dot plots quickly show clusters and outliers.

Quick revision

  • Mean = sum ÷ count.
  • Median is the middle value of ordered data.
  • Outliers affect the mean more than the median.
