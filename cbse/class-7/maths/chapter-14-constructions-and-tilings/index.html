Class 7 Maths Chapter 14: Constructions and Tilings

NCERT Ganita Prakash · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Using only a compass and ruler, we can construct perpendicular bisectors and triangles from given sides and angles. Tilings cover a surface with shapes leaving no gaps or overlaps. A regular polygon tiles by itself only if its interior angle divides 360° exactly, which is true for triangles, squares and hexagons.

Key concepts

🧭Compass and Ruler

A compass draws circles and arcs; a ruler draws straight lines. Together they make exact constructions.

Example: An arc of radius 4 cm marks all points 4 cm from the centre.

In real life: Architects began with compass-and-ruler drawings.

💡 Did you know? Constructions do not use a protractor for measuring.

⊥Perpendicular Bisector

The perpendicular bisector of a segment cuts it into two equal halves at 90°.

Example: Arcs from both ends of AB meet at two points; joining them bisects AB.

In real life: Finding the middle of a plank uses this idea.

💡 Did you know? Every point on the perpendicular bisector is equally far from both ends.

🔺Constructing Triangles

A triangle can be drawn from three sides (SSS), two sides and the included angle (SAS), or two angles and a side (ASA).

Example: For sides 5, 6 and 7 cm, draw one side and use arcs for the other two.

In real life: Engineers mark triangular supports this way.

💡 Did you know? Sides must satisfy the triangle inequality, or no triangle forms.

🧩Tilings

A tiling covers a flat surface with shapes without gaps or overlaps.

Example: Squares, equilateral triangles and regular hexagons can tile a floor alone.

In real life: Honeycombs use hexagonal tiling.

💡 Did you know? Angles meeting at a point in a tiling add up to 360°.

⬡Why Some Shapes Tile

A regular polygon tiles alone only if its angle divides 360° exactly.

Example: Hexagon angle 120°: 3 × 120° = 360°, so it tiles.

In real life: Floor and wall tiles are chosen using this rule.

💡 Did you know? A regular pentagon (108°) cannot tile alone.

Quick revision

  • Angles around a point add up to 360°.
  • Squares, equilateral triangles and regular hexagons tile alone.
  • Perpendicular bisector meets the segment at 90°.
