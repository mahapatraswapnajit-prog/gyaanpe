Class 7 Maths Chapter 3: A Peek Beyond the Point

NCERT Ganita Prakash · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Decimals extend place value beyond the point into tenths, hundredths and thousandths. They are used to write measurements in bigger units and money. Decimals are compared place by place from the left, and added or subtracted by lining up the decimal points.

Key concepts

🔍Tenths and Hundredths

When one unit is divided into 10 equal parts, each part is one tenth (0.1). Dividing a tenth into 10 gives a hundredth (0.01).

Example: 0.7 means 7 tenths; 0.07 means 7 hundredths.

In real life: Prices like ₹12.50 use decimals.

💡 Did you know? The decimal point separates the whole part from the fractional part.

📍Place Value with Decimals

Places after the point are tenths, hundredths, thousandths and so on.

Example: In 3.46, 4 is 4 tenths and 6 is 6 hundredths.

In real life: Fuel meters show litres with decimals.

💡 Did you know? Each place is 10 times smaller than the place to its left.

📏Units and Decimals

Smaller units can be written as decimals of bigger units.

Example: 15 mm = 1.5 cm; 250 g = 0.25 kg.

In real life: A doctor writes your height like 1.42 m.

💡 Did you know? 1 cm = 10 mm and 1 kg = 1000 g.

⚖️Comparing Decimals

Compare the whole parts first, then tenths, then hundredths.

Example: 0.5 > 0.45 because 5 tenths > 4 tenths.

In real life: Sports timings like 9.58 s and 9.63 s are compared this way.

💡 Did you know? Adding zeros at the end does not change a decimal: 0.5 = 0.50.

➕Adding and Subtracting Decimals

Line up the decimal points, then add or subtract like whole numbers.

Example: 2.75 + 1.5 = 4.25.

In real life: Adding bills in rupees and paise uses decimal addition.

💡 Did you know? Writing 1.5 as 1.50 helps keep places lined up.

Key terms

TermMeaning
TenthOne part out of 10 equal parts (0.1)
HundredthOne part out of 100 equal parts (0.01)
Decimal pointSeparates whole part from fractional part
Decimal placePosition of a digit after the point
ThousandthOne part out of 1000 (0.001)

Quick revision

  • 0.5 = 0.50 = 1/2.
  • 1 cm = 10 mm, so 1 mm = 0.1 cm.
  • Line up decimal points while adding.
