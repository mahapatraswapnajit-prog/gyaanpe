Class 7 Maths Chapter 8: Working with Fractions

NCERT Ganita Prakash · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Fractions are multiplied by multiplying numerators and denominators. Finding a fraction of a quantity means multiplying. To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal. Multiplying by a fraction less than one makes a number smaller, while dividing by it makes the number bigger.

Key concepts

✖️Multiplying Fractions

Multiply numerators together and denominators together.

Example: 2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12 = 1/2.

In real life: Finding half of three-quarters of a pizza uses this.

💡 Did you know? Simplify before multiplying to make numbers smaller.

🍰Fraction of a Quantity

To find a fraction of a number, multiply the number by the fraction.

Example: 3/4 of 20 = 15.

In real life: 3/4 of ₹200 pocket money is ₹150.

💡 Did you know? 'Of' in fractions means multiply.

🔄Reciprocal

The reciprocal of a/b is b/a. A number times its reciprocal is 1.

Example: Reciprocal of 3/5 is 5/3; 3/5 × 5/3 = 1.

In real life: Reciprocals make division easy.

💡 Did you know? Zero has no reciprocal.

➗Dividing Fractions

To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal.

Example: 1/2 ÷ 1/4 = 1/2 × 4 = 2.

In real life: How many 1/4 kg packets from 2 kg of sugar? 2 ÷ 1/4 = 8.

💡 Did you know? Dividing by a fraction less than 1 gives a bigger answer.

🧮Product Size

Multiplying by a fraction less than 1 makes a number smaller; by more than 1 makes it bigger.

Example: 12 × 1/3 = 4, but 12 × 4/3 = 16.

In real life: Discounts work by multiplying prices by a fraction.

💡 Did you know? This helps check if an answer makes sense.

Key terms

TermMeaning
NumeratorTop number of a fraction
DenominatorBottom number of a fraction
ReciprocalFraction turned upside down
ProductResult of multiplication
Proper fractionNumerator smaller than denominator

Quick revision

  • a/b × c/d = (a × c)/(b × d).
  • a/b ÷ c/d = a/b × d/c.
  • A number × its reciprocal = 1.
