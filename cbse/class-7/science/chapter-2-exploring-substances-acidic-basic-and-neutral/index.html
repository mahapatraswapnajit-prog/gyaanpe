Class 7 Science Chapter 2: Exploring Substances: Acidic, Basic, and Neutral

NCERT Curiosity · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Acids taste sour and bases taste bitter and soapy, while neutral substances are neither. Indicators like litmus, turmeric and China rose show whether a substance is acidic or basic by changing colour. When an acid and a base react, they neutralise each other to form salt and water, releasing heat.

Key concepts

🍋Acids

Acids taste sour. They are found in lemon, vinegar, curd and tamarind.

Example: Lemon has citric acid; vinegar has acetic acid; curd has lactic acid.

In real life: Ant stings contain formic acid, which causes the burning feeling.

💡 Did you know? Never taste unknown substances in a lab.

🧼Bases

Bases taste bitter and feel soapy.

Example: Baking soda, soap and lime water are bases.

In real life: Soap is a base that helps remove grease.

💡 Did you know? Milk of magnesia is a mild base used as an antacid.

🧪Indicators

Indicators change colour in acids or bases.

Example: Acid turns blue litmus red; base turns red litmus blue.

In real life: China rose petals turn magenta in acid and green in base.

💡 Did you know? Turmeric turns red in a base and stays yellow in an acid.

⚖️Neutral Substances

Neutral substances are neither acidic nor basic; they do not change litmus colour.

Example: Pure water and sugar solution are neutral.

In real life: Drinking water should be close to neutral.

💡 Did you know? Salt solution in water is usually neutral.

🤝Neutralisation

An acid and a base react to form salt and water, and heat is released.

Example: Acid + base → salt + water + heat.

In real life: Farmers add lime (a base) to acidic soil.

💡 Did you know? An antacid neutralises extra stomach acid.

Key terms

TermMeaning
AcidSour substance that turns blue litmus red
BaseBitter, soapy substance that turns red litmus blue
IndicatorSubstance that changes colour in acid or base
NeutralNeither acidic nor basic
NeutralisationAcid + base → salt + water

Quick revision

  • Acid: blue litmus → red.
  • Base: red litmus → blue; turmeric → red.
  • Neutralisation gives salt, water and heat.
