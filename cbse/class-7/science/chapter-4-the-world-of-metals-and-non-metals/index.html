Class 7 Science Chapter 4: The World of Metals and Non-metals

NCERT Curiosity · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Metals are lustrous, malleable, ductile, sonorous and good conductors, while non-metals are usually dull, brittle and poor conductors, with a few exceptions like graphite. Metals form basic oxides and non-metals form acidic oxides. Iron rusts in the presence of air and water, which can be prevented by painting or galvanising.

Key concepts

✨Properties of Metals

Metals are usually shiny (lustrous), hard, and good conductors of heat and electricity.

Example: Iron, copper, aluminium, gold and silver are metals.

In real life: Cooking pots are made of metals because they conduct heat.

💡 Did you know? Mercury is the only metal that is liquid at room temperature.

🔨Malleable and Ductile

Metals can be beaten into sheets (malleable) and drawn into wires (ductile).

Example: Aluminium foil and copper wires show these properties.

In real life: Sweets are decorated with thin silver foil (varq).

💡 Did you know? Gold is so malleable it can be beaten into very thin leaves.

🔔Sonorous

Metals make a ringing sound when struck.

Example: School bells and temple bells are made of metal.

In real life: Musical instruments like cymbals use metals.

💡 Did you know? Non-metals usually make a dull sound.

🪨Non-metals

Non-metals are usually dull, brittle and poor conductors.

Example: Sulfur, carbon, oxygen and nitrogen are non-metals.

In real life: Oxygen is a non-metal we need to breathe.

💡 Did you know? Graphite, a form of carbon, conducts electricity. Bromine is a liquid non-metal.

🟤Reactions and Rusting

Metals react with oxygen to form basic oxides; non-metals form acidic oxides. Iron rusts in the presence of both air and water.

Example: Rust is iron oxide; painting or galvanising iron prevents it.

In real life: Iron gates are painted to stop rusting.

💡 Did you know? Galvanising means coating iron with zinc.

Key terms

TermMeaning
LustreShine of a metal
MalleableCan be beaten into sheets
DuctileCan be drawn into wires
SonorousMakes a ringing sound
RustingIron reacting with air and water

Quick revision

  • Mercury is a liquid metal; bromine is a liquid non-metal.
  • Metal oxides are basic; non-metal oxides are acidic.
  • Rusting needs both air and water.

Practice questions

Q1. Which metal is liquid at room temperature?

  1. Sodium
  2. Copper
  3. Mercury
  4. Iron
Show answerC) Mercury

Q2. The property of being drawn into wires is

  1. Ductility
  2. Malleability
  3. Lustre
  4. Sonority
Show answerA) Ductility

Q3. Aluminium foil shows that aluminium is

  1. Ductile
  2. Malleable
  3. Sonorous
  4. Brittle
Show answerB) Malleable

Q4. Which non-metal conducts electricity?

  1. Graphite
  2. Oxygen
  3. Sulfur
  4. Nitrogen
Show answerA) Graphite

Q5. Rusting of iron needs

  1. Only sunlight
  2. Only air
  3. Both air and water
  4. Only water
Show answerC) Both air and water
