Class 7 Science Chapter 5: Changes Around Us: Physical and Chemical

NCERT Curiosity · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Changes around us are physical when no new substance is formed, like melting or dissolving, and chemical when new substances form, like burning, rusting or curdling. Gas, colour change, heat or light can signal a chemical change. Crystallisation is a physical change, and rusting can be prevented by painting or galvanising.

Key concepts

🧊Physical Changes

In a physical change, no new substance is formed; often only shape, size or state changes.

Example: Melting ice, cutting paper and dissolving salt in water.

In real life: Folding clothes changes their shape but not what they are made of.

💡 Did you know? Many physical changes can be reversed.

🔥Chemical Changes

In a chemical change, one or more new substances are formed.

Example: Burning paper, rusting iron and curdling of milk.

In real life: Cooking food is a chemical change.

💡 Did you know? Most chemical changes cannot be easily reversed.

🔎Signs of a Chemical Change

Gas bubbles, colour change, heat or light, and new smell can show a chemical change.

Example: Baking soda + vinegar gives bubbles of carbon dioxide.

In real life: A cut apple turning brown is a chemical change.

💡 Did you know? Not every colour change is chemical, so check for a new substance.

💎Crystallisation

Getting pure, large crystals of a substance from its solution is called crystallisation.

Example: Sugar crystals form when a hot sugar solution cools slowly.

In real life: Salt is made from sea water by evaporation and crystallisation.

💡 Did you know? Crystallisation is a physical change.

🛡️Preventing Unwanted Changes

Some chemical changes like rusting can be slowed down.

Example: Painting iron or coating it with zinc (galvanising) prevents rust.

In real life: Cooking utensils of stainless steel do not rust easily.

💡 Did you know? Stainless steel is made by mixing iron with chromium and nickel.

Key terms

Physical changeNo new substance formed
Chemical changeNew substance formed

Quick revision

  • Melting and dissolving are physical changes.
  • Burning and rusting are chemical changes.
  • Baking soda + vinegar gives carbon dioxide.

Practice questions

Q1. Which is a physical change?

  1. Melting ice
  2. Cooking rice
  3. Rusting iron
  4. Burning wood
Show answerA) Melting ice

Q2. Which is a chemical change?

  1. Burning paper
  2. Dissolving sugar
  3. Cutting paper
  4. Breaking glass
Show answerA) Burning paper

Q3. Baking soda and vinegar produce bubbles of

  1. Hydrogen
  2. Carbon dioxide
  3. Oxygen
  4. Nitrogen
Show answerB) Carbon dioxide

Q4. Curdling of milk is a

  1. Reversible change
  2. No change
  3. Chemical change
  4. Physical change
Show answerC) Chemical change

Q5. In a physical change

  1. A new substance forms
  2. Light is always produced
  3. No new substance forms
  4. Gas is always released
Show answerC) No new substance forms
