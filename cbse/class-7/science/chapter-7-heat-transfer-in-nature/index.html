Class 7 Science Chapter 7: Heat Transfer in Nature

NCERT Curiosity · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Heat moves by conduction through solids, by convection through liquids and gases, and by radiation without any medium, as from the Sun. Uneven heating of land and sea causes sea breezes during the day and land breezes at night. The Sun's heat also drives the water cycle through evaporation and condensation.

Key concepts

🥄Conduction

In conduction, heat moves through a solid from the hotter part to the colder part.

Example: A steel spoon in hot tea becomes hot at the handle.

In real life: Cooking pan handles are made of plastic or wood.

💡 Did you know? Metals are good conductors of heat; wood and plastic are poor conductors.

🌀Convection

In liquids and gases, heated particles rise and cooler ones sink, carrying heat. This is convection.

Example: Water in a pot heats from the bottom up by convection.

In real life: Room heaters work by convection.

💡 Did you know? Warm air rises, which is why hot-air balloons fly.

☀️Radiation

Radiation transfers heat without any medium, even through empty space.

Example: Heat from the Sun reaches the Earth by radiation.

In real life: We wear light-coloured clothes in summer.

💡 Did you know? Dark surfaces absorb more heat than light-coloured ones.

🌊Sea and Land Breeze

During the day land heats faster, so cool air from the sea blows to land (sea breeze). At night, land cools faster, and air blows from land to sea (land breeze).

Example: Coastal towns like Puri feel a cool sea breeze in the afternoon.

In real life: Fishermen use the land breeze to sail out early morning.

💡 Did you know? These breezes are caused by convection.

💧Water Cycle

Heat from the Sun evaporates water; vapour cools and condenses into clouds, and falls as rain.

Example: Puddles dry up after rain because of evaporation.

In real life: Rain fills rivers, lakes and wells.

💡 Did you know? Water keeps moving between land, oceans and air.

Quick revision

  • Sun's heat reaches us by radiation.
  • Day: sea breeze; night: land breeze.
  • Metals conduct heat well.
