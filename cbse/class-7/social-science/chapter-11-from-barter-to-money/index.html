Class 7 Social Science Chapter 11: From Barter to Money

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Trade began with barter, which needed a double coincidence of wants. People then used commodity money like cowrie shells, then metal coins such as punch-marked coins, followed by paper currency and today's digital money like UPI. Money acts as a medium of exchange, a measure of value and a store of value. In India, the RBI issues currency.

Key concepts

🔁Barter System

In barter, people exchange goods directly without money.

Example: A farmer trades rice for a potter's pots.

In real life: Some villages still exchange goods during festivals.

💡 Did you know? Barter needs a 'double coincidence of wants': both must want what the other has.

🐚Commodity Money

People began using commonly accepted items like cowrie shells, salt or grain as money.

Example: Cowrie shells were used as money in many parts of India.

In real life: Museums display cowrie shells used as currency.

💡 Did you know? The word 'salary' is linked to salt used as payment in Rome.

🪙Coins

Metal coins with fixed value made trade easier. India's earliest coins were punch-marked silver coins.

Example: Kushana and Gupta kings issued gold coins.

In real life: Coin collecting (numismatics) teaches history.

💡 Did you know? Coins carried symbols or images of rulers.

💵Paper and Digital Money

Paper currency and now digital money like UPI, cards and net banking are widely used.

Example: In India, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) issues currency notes.

In real life: Shops in villages now accept QR code payments.

💡 Did you know? UPI lets people pay instantly using a mobile phone.

🎯Functions of Money

Money works as a medium of exchange, a measure of value and a store of value.

Example: Prices tell us the value of goods in money.

In real life: Saving money in a bank keeps it safe.

💡 Did you know? Money can be saved and used later, unlike perishable goods.

Key terms

TermMeaning
BarterExchange of goods without money
Double coincidence of wantsBoth traders want each other's goods
Commodity moneyGoods like shells used as money
CurrencyMoney in use, like coins and notes
UPISystem for instant mobile payments

Quick revision

  • Barter needs a double coincidence of wants.
  • RBI issues currency notes in India.
  • Money is a medium of exchange and store of value.
