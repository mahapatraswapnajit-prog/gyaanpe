Class 7 Social Science Chapter 13: The Story of Indian Farming

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Farming in India has a long history, from early grain farming to modern methods. Kharif crops grow in the monsoon and rabi crops in winter. Irrigation, and later the Green Revolution of the 1960s, greatly increased food production. Today hardy millets and sustainable practices like crop rotation are being encouraged.

Key concepts

🌾Farming Through Time

Farming in India began thousands of years ago and changed how people lived.

Example: Early farmers grew wheat, barley, rice and millets.

In real life: Farming still supports a large share of Indians.

💡 Did you know? Mehrgarh has evidence of very early farming in the subcontinent.

🗓️Kharif and Rabi

Kharif crops are sown with the monsoon (June–July); rabi crops are sown in winter (October–November).

Example: Rice, maize and cotton are kharif; wheat, mustard and gram are rabi.

In real life: Farmers plan crops by season.

💡 Did you know? Zaid crops like watermelon grow in the short summer season.

💧Irrigation

Irrigation supplies water to crops through canals, wells, tanks and drip systems.

Example: Drip irrigation saves water by watering plant roots directly.

In real life: Many farmers use borewells today.

💡 Did you know? Ancient India built tanks and canals for irrigation.

🚜Green Revolution

In the 1960s, high-yielding seeds, fertilisers and irrigation greatly raised food production.

Example: Punjab and Haryana saw big increases in wheat output.

In real life: Overuse of fertilisers later harmed some soils.

💡 Did you know? India moved from food shortage to food self-sufficiency.

🌱Millets and Sustainable Farming

Millets like ragi, jowar and bajra are nutritious and need less water.

Example: 2023 was celebrated as the International Year of Millets.

In real life: Odisha has a special mission to promote millets.

💡 Did you know? Crop rotation and organic manure keep soil healthy.
