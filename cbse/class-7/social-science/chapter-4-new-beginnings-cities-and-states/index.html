Class 7 Social Science Chapter 4: New Beginnings: Cities and States

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

After the Harappan cities declined, new cities arose in the Ganga plains in a second urbanisation. People settled in janapadas, which grew into sixteen mahajanapadas such as Magadha and Kosala. Some states, like the Vajji confederacy, were run by assemblies. Trade expanded and punch-marked coins came into use.

Key concepts

🏙️A Second Urbanisation

After the decline of Harappan cities, new cities grew in the Ganga plains around the 6th century BCE.

Example: Rajgir, Vaishali, Kashi and Ujjain became important towns.

In real life: Many of these places are still important cities today.

💡 Did you know? Historians call this the 'second urbanisation' of India.

👑Janapadas

Janapada means 'the land where a people settled'. Early groups set up such territories.

Example: Kuru and Panchala were early janapadas.

In real life: Names of regions often recall these ancient people.

💡 Did you know? The word 'jana' means people.

🗺️Mahajanapadas

By the 6th century BCE, there were sixteen large states called mahajanapadas.

Example: Magadha, Kosala, Vatsa and Avanti were among them.

In real life: Buddhist and Jain texts list the sixteen mahajanapadas.

💡 Did you know? Magadha later grew into the most powerful one.

🤝Gaṇas and Saṅghas

Some states were ruled by assemblies of people rather than a single king. These are called gaṇas or saṅghas.

Example: The Vajji confederacy with its capital at Vaishali was a gaṇa.

In real life: They are early examples of group decision-making.

💡 Did you know? These assemblies discussed matters before deciding.

🪙Trade and Coins

Trade grew along rivers and roads, and the earliest Indian coins, called punch-marked coins, came into use.

Example: Punch-marked coins were made of silver with symbols stamped on them.

In real life: Museums display these ancient coins.

💡 Did you know? Coins made trade easier than barter.

Quick revision

  • There were 16 mahajanapadas.
  • Vaishali was the capital of the Vajji gaṇa.
  • Punch-marked coins were the earliest Indian coins.
