Class 7 Social Science Chapter 7: The Gupta Era: An Age of Tireless Creativity

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The Gupta era, from the 4th century CE, saw strong rulers like Samudragupta and Chandragupta II and remarkable creativity. Aryabhata advanced astronomy and mathematics, Kalidasa wrote great Sanskrit works, metalworkers made the rust-resistant Iron Pillar, and centres like Nalanda drew learners. Travellers like Faxian recorded life in this period.

Key concepts

👑The Gupta Empire

The Gupta Empire began in the 4th century CE. Chandragupta I, Samudragupta and Chandragupta II were great rulers.

Example: Samudragupta's victories are described in the Prayag (Allahabad) pillar inscription by Harisena.

In real life: Prayagraj still has this pillar inside the fort.

💡 Did you know? Chandragupta II is also called Vikramaditya.

🔢Science and Mathematics

Aryabhata wrote the Aryabhatiya, explaining that the Earth rotates on its axis.

Example: Indian mathematicians used the place-value system with zero.

In real life: Our number system came from these ideas.

💡 Did you know? India's first satellite was named Aryabhata in his honour.

📚Literature

Kalidasa wrote famous works like Abhijnana Shakuntalam and Meghaduta.

Example: Meghaduta is about a cloud carrying a message.

In real life: His plays are still performed today.

💡 Did you know? Kalidasa is considered one of the greatest Sanskrit poets.

⚙️Metal Work

The Iron Pillar in Delhi (Mehrauli) has stood for over 1,600 years with very little rust.

Example: It shows the high skill of ancient Indian metal workers.

In real life: Visitors see it at the Qutub complex.

💡 Did you know? Scientists still study why it does not rust easily.

🎓Learning and Travellers

Learning flourished; the Chinese traveller Faxian (Fa-Hien) visited India during Chandragupta II's reign.

Example: Nalanda began as a centre of learning in the Gupta period.

In real life: Nalanda's ruins in Bihar are a World Heritage Site.

💡 Did you know? Students came to Nalanda from many countries.
