Class 7 Social Science Chapter 9: From the Rulers to the Ruled: Types of Governments

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Governments make and enforce decisions for a country. In a monarchy a king or queen rules; in a dictatorship one person rules without free elections; in a theocracy religious leaders rule; and in a democracy people choose their government. India is a representative democracy where every adult aged 18 or above can vote.

Key concepts

🏛️What is Government?

A government makes decisions and rules for a country and keeps order.

Example: Building roads, running schools and keeping peace are government jobs.

In real life: The police and courts are part of how a state works.

💡 Did you know? Every country has some form of government.

👑Monarchy

In a monarchy, a king or queen rules, and power usually passes within a family.

Example: The United Kingdom has a monarch, but elected leaders run the government.

In real life: Old Indian kingdoms were mostly monarchies.

💡 Did you know? In an absolute monarchy, the ruler has full power.

🗳️Democracy

In a democracy, people choose their government through elections.

Example: India is the world's largest democracy.

In real life: Voting is the right and duty of citizens.

💡 Did you know? Abraham Lincoln described democracy as government of the people, by the people, for the people.

🚫Dictatorship and Others

In a dictatorship, one person or a small group holds power without free elections. In a theocracy, religious leaders rule; in an oligarchy, a few people rule.

Example: Citizens in a dictatorship often cannot criticise the ruler.

In real life: Free newspapers are a sign of democracy.

💡 Did you know? Democracy protects freedom of speech.

🙋Direct and Representative Democracy

In direct democracy, people decide matters themselves; in representative democracy, they elect representatives.

Example: Gram Sabha meetings are an example of direct participation.

In real life: Members of Parliament represent voters.

💡 Did you know? India gives every adult citizen aged 18 or above the right to vote.

Key terms

  • Voting age in India is 18.

Q1. In a democracy, the government is chosen by

  1. The king
  2. The people through elections
  3. A religious leader
  4. The army
Show answerB) The people through elections

Q2. India is the world's largest

  1. Dictatorship
  2. Democracy
  3. Monarchy
  4. Theocracy
Show answerB) Democracy

Q3. Rule by a king or queen is called

  1. Oligarchy
  2. Democracy
  3. Republic
  4. Monarchy
Show answerD) Monarchy

Q4. The minimum voting age in India is

  1. 25
  2. 21
  3. 18
  4. 16
Show answerC) 18

Q5. When one person rules without free elections, it is a

  1. Dictatorship
  2. Federation
  3. Republic
  4. Democracy
