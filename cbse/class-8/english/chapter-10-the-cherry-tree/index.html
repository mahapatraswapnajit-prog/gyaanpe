Class 8 English Chapter 10: The Cherry Tree

NCERT Poorvi · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Rakesh plants a cherry seed and patiently cares for it as it survives setbacks and finally blossoms. Ruskin Bond's story celebrates patience, growth and our bond with nature, with practice in phrasal verbs and sequencing.

Key concepts

🍒The Story

Rakesh plants a cherry seed in his grandfather's garden and watches it grow, through many setbacks, into a tree that blossoms.

Example: The young tree survives being cut, attacked by insects and harsh weather.

In real life: Planting a tree and caring for it teaches patience.

💡 Did you know? The story is by Ruskin Bond, who has written many stories set in the hills of Mussoorie.

⏳Patience and Care

Growth takes time. Rakesh's care for the plant mirrors his own growing up.

Example: Watering, protecting and waiting season after season.

In real life: Gardening clubs help children learn patience.

💡 Did you know? Ruskin Bond received the Sahitya Akademi Award for his writing.

🌳Bond with Nature

The story shows the joy of nurturing life and feeling connected to nature.

Example: Rakesh feels the tree is special because he grew it himself.

In real life: Van Mahotsav in July celebrates tree planting.

💡 Did you know? Trees give fruit, shade, oxygen and homes for birds and insects.

🔤Phrasal Verbs

A phrasal verb is a verb plus a small word that together give a new meaning.

Example: 'grow up' = become an adult; 'look after' = take care of; 'give up' = stop trying.

In real life: 'Don't give up' is a common phrase of encouragement.

💡 Did you know? Phrasal verbs are very common in spoken English.

🗓️Sequencing Events

Stories often follow time order. Words like first, then, later and finally show sequence.

Example: First the seed, then a shoot, later a small tree, finally blossoms.

In real life: Recipes and instructions also use sequence words.

💡 Did you know? Sequencing helps in writing summaries.
