Class 8 English Chapter 2: A Concrete Example

NCERT Poorvi · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

This poem invites careful reading of how a title, images and comparisons carry meaning. Alongside it, students learn about the two meanings of 'concrete', similes, metaphors, imagery, symbols and theme.

Key concepts

📜Reading a Poem

A poem uses carefully chosen words, rhythm and images to share a feeling or idea in few words.

Example: Read a poem aloud twice: once for sound, once for meaning.

In real life: Song lyrics are poems set to music.

💡 Did you know? The title of a poem often gives a clue or a symbol to its meaning.

🧱'Concrete' — Two Meanings

'Concrete' can mean a building material, or something real and specific rather than vague.

Example: 'Give me a concrete example' = give a real, specific example.

In real life: Teachers ask for 'concrete examples' to make ideas clear.

💡 Did you know? Many English words have more than one meaning (polysemy).

🔤Simile and Metaphor

A simile compares using 'like' or 'as'; a metaphor says one thing IS another.

Example: Simile: 'as hard as stone'. Metaphor: 'His heart is stone.'

In real life: Advertisements often use metaphors.

💡 Did you know? Both help readers picture an idea quickly.

🖼️Imagery

Imagery is language that creates pictures, sounds or feelings in the reader's mind.

Example: 'The grey city hummed with traffic' appeals to sight and sound.

In real life: Travel writers use imagery to make places come alive.

💡 Did you know? Good imagery uses specific, concrete nouns and strong verbs.

💭Theme

The theme is the central idea a poem explores.

Example: A poem may use a simple object to talk about a big idea like change or hope.

In real life: Discussing themes builds critical thinking.

💡 Did you know? Asking 'What is the poet really saying?' helps find the theme.

Key terms

Quick revision

  • A simile uses 'like' or 'as'.
  • 'Concrete' can mean real and specific.
  • A poem's title can be a clue to its meaning.

Practice questions

Q1. A simile compares two things using

  1. Question marks
  2. 'And' or 'but'
  3. 'Like' or 'as'
  4. Only numbers
Show answerC) 'Like' or 'as'

Q2. 'His heart is stone' is an example of a

  1. Metaphor
  2. Question
  3. Simile
  4. Rhyme
Show answerA) Metaphor

Q3. 'Give me a concrete example' means give an example that is

  1. Very long
  2. Made of cement
  3. Imaginary
  4. Real and specific
Show answerD) Real and specific

Q4. Words that create pictures in the reader's mind are called

  1. Punctuation
  2. Imagery
  3. Grammar
  4. Spelling
Show answerB) Imagery

Q5. 'As brave as a lion' is a

  1. Metaphor
  2. Proverb
  3. Idiom only
  4. Simile
Show answerD) Simile
