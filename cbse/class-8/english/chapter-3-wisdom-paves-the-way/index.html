Class 8 English Chapter 3: Wisdom Paves the Way

NCERT Poorvi · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

In this play, four young men use sharp observation and logical reasoning to answer a merchant's complaint before a king and become royal advisers. It highlights wisdom, deduction and the format of a play, with practice in modal verbs.

Key concepts

🎭A Play

This chapter is a play in which four young men use careful observation and reasoning to answer a merchant's complaint in a king's court, and win places as royal advisers.

Example: Plays are written mostly as dialogue between characters.

In real life: School annual-day dramas follow the same format.

💡 Did you know? Stage directions, usually in brackets, tell actors how to move or speak.

🔎Observation and Deduction

Deduction means reaching a conclusion from clues you observe.

Example: Seeing wet footprints and an umbrella by the door, you deduce it is raining.

In real life: Doctors deduce illnesses from symptoms.

💡 Did you know? Detectives and scientists both depend on careful observation.

⚖️Wisdom vs Knowledge

Knowledge is knowing facts; wisdom is using knowledge well to make good judgements.

Example: Knowing traffic rules is knowledge; crossing safely is wisdom.

In real life: Wise choices come from thinking before acting.

💡 Did you know? Many folk tales reward wisdom over strength or wealth.

📝Modal Verbs

Modals like can, could, may, might, must, should and would show ability, permission, possibility or duty.

Example: 'You must tell the truth.' 'It might rain today.'

In real life: Polite requests use 'could' and 'would'.

💡 Did you know? A modal is followed by the base form of the verb: 'can go', not 'can goes'.

👑Moral

Clear thinking and honesty can solve problems that power and wealth cannot.

Example: The king rewards the young men's reasoning.

In real life: Good teams value people who think carefully.

💡 Did you know? Advisers have played important roles in many royal courts.
