Class 8 English Chapter 4: A Tale of Valour: Major Somnath Sharma and the Battle of Badgam

NCERT Poorvi · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

This true account honours Major Somnath Sharma, who led his men bravely at Badgam in 1947 and became the first recipient of the Param Vir Chakra. It explores courage, duty and sacrifice, with practice in the past perfect tense and words of valour.

Key concepts

🎖️A True Hero

Major Somnath Sharma led his company of the Kumaon Regiment in the Battle of Badgam in Kashmir on 3 November 1947, defending the area against a much larger force.

Example: He fought on despite having his arm in a plaster cast.

In real life: His bravery helped protect the Srinagar airfield.

💡 Did you know? He was the first recipient of the Param Vir Chakra, India's highest wartime gallantry award (awarded posthumously).

🏅The Param Vir Chakra

The Param Vir Chakra is India's highest military honour for bravery in the face of the enemy.

Example: 'Posthumously' means awarded after the person's death.

In real life: Republic Day honours gallantry award winners.

💡 Did you know? The medal's design shows the vajra, symbolising sacrifice.

💪Values of Courage

Courage, duty, leadership and sacrifice are the values the story celebrates.

Example: A leader who stands with his team in danger earns their trust.

In real life: Firefighters and doctors also show everyday courage.

💡 Did you know? Courage is not the absence of fear but acting rightly despite it.

🕰️Past Perfect Tense

The past perfect (had + past participle) shows an action that happened before another past action.

Example: 'The soldiers had reached the post before the attack began.'

In real life: It is common in history writing and news reports.

💡 Did you know? Use it to make the order of past events clear.

📝Vocabulary of Valour

Words like valour, gallantry, sacrifice, resolve and duty describe heroic actions.

Example: 'Valour' means great courage in danger.

In real life: War memorials list soldiers who showed valour.

💡 Did you know? Many such words come from Latin and French.
