Class 8 English Chapter 7: The Case of the Fifth Word

NCERT Poorvi · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Chief Brown solves Tim Nolan's case by decoding a hidden message with an encyclopedia's help. The chapter explores how mysteries work — clues, codes and logical solutions — and practises reported speech and inference.

Key concepts

🕵️A Mystery Story

In this detective story, Chief Brown investigates Tim Nolan's case and cracks a coded message with the help of an encyclopedia.

Example: Detectives look for clues that others overlook.

In real life: Crossword and code puzzles train the same skills.

💡 Did you know? A good mystery gives readers enough clues to try solving it themselves.

🔐Codes and Clues

A code hides a message using a rule. Breaking the code means finding the rule.

Example: A message where every fifth word matters hides meaning in plain sight.

In real life: Passwords and secret codes protect information today.

💡 Did you know? Reference books like dictionaries and encyclopedias can help decode clues.

🧩Parts of a Mystery

A mystery has a crime or puzzle, clues, suspects, a detective and a solution.

Example: Red herrings are false clues that mislead the reader.

In real life: Many TV series follow this pattern.

💡 Did you know? The solution should be logical, not magic.

💬Reported Speech

Reported (indirect) speech tells what someone said without quoting their exact words; tenses often shift back.

Example: Direct: He said, 'I am tired.' Reported: He said that he was tired.

In real life: News writers use reported speech a lot.

💡 Did you know? Pronouns and time words change too: 'today' → 'that day'.

📖Reading Skills

Inference means understanding what is suggested but not stated directly.

Example: 'His shoes were muddy' lets us infer he walked outside.

In real life: Inference helps in comprehension tests.

💡 Did you know? Re-reading clues often reveals what was missed.

Key terms

TermMeaning

Quick revision

  • Chief Brown is the detective in the story.
  • An encyclopedia helps crack the message.
  • Reported speech usually shifts the tense back.
