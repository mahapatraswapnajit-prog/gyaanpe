Class 8 Maths Chapter 1: A Square and a Cube

NCERT Ganita Prakash · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Square numbers are n × n and cube numbers are n × n × n. The chapter explores their patterns, finds square and cube roots using prime factors, and meets Ramanujan's famous number 1729.

Key concepts

🟦Square Numbers

A square number is a number multiplied by itself: n × n = n². The first few are 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36…

Example: 12² = 12 × 12 = 144.

In real life: Square floor tiles: a 9 × 9 floor needs 81 tiles.

💡 Did you know? A perfect square never ends in 2, 3, 7 or 8.

➕Squares and Odd Numbers

Adding the first n odd numbers always gives n².

Example: 1 + 3 + 5 + 7 = 16 = 4².

In real life: You can check if a number is a square by subtracting 1, 3, 5, 7… until you reach 0.

💡 Did you know? Each new square of dots grows by an L-shaped layer of an odd number of dots.

√Square Roots

The square root of a number is the number that, multiplied by itself, gives it. Pair up equal prime factors to find it.

Example: 324 = 2×2×3×3×3×3, so √324 = 2×3×3 = 18.

In real life: From the area of a square plot you can find the length of its side.

💡 Did you know? In a perfect square every prime factor appears an even number of times.

🧊Cube Numbers

A cube number is n × n × n = n³. The first few are 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216…

Example: 6³ = 6 × 6 × 6 = 216.

In real life: A 3 × 3 × 3 puzzle cube is built from 27 small cubes.

💡 Did you know? The cube of an odd number is odd; the cube of an even number is even.

🚕Cube Roots and 1729

The cube root of a number is the number that, used three times in a product, gives it. Group equal prime factors in threes.

Example: 1728 = 12³, so ∛1728 = 12.

In real life: The story goes that Hardy called his taxi number 1729 dull, and Ramanujan said it was very interesting.

💡 Did you know? 1729 = 1³ + 12³ = 9³ + 10³. It is the smallest number that is a sum of two cubes in two different ways (Ramanujan's taxicab number).

Key terms

TermMeaning
Perfect squareA number that is some whole number times itself
Square rootThe number whose square gives the given number
Perfect cubeA number that is some whole number used three times in a product
Cube rootThe number whose cube gives the given number

Quick revision

  • A perfect square never ends in 2, 3, 7 or 8.
  • The sum of the first n odd numbers is n².
  • 1729 = 1³ + 12³ = 9³ + 10³.
