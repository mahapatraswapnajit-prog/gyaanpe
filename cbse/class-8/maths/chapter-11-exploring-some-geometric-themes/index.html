Class 8 Maths Chapter 11: Exploring Some Geometric Themes

NCERT Ganita Prakash · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter explores fractals such as the Sierpinski gasket and carpet and the Koch snowflake, and then visualises solids: making them from nets and seeing their front, top and side views.

Key concepts

❄️Fractals

A fractal is a pattern in which small parts look like the whole shape. This is called self-similarity.

Example: A fern leaf is made of smaller leaflets shaped like the whole leaf.

In real life: Computer graphics use fractals to draw mountains and trees.

💡 Did you know? Broccoli, lightning and river networks show fractal patterns.

🔺Sierpinski Gasket and Carpet

Gasket: remove the middle triangle from a triangle, then repeat on the 3 that remain. Carpet: split a square into 9, remove the centre, repeat on the 8 that remain.

Example: Gasket: 3 triangles after step 1, 9 after step 2.

In real life: These patterns appear in rangoli and tile designs.

💡 Did you know? Each step of the carpet keeps 8 of every 9 squares.

🌨️Koch Snowflake

Replace the middle third of each side with two sides of a small equilateral triangle. Each side becomes 4 sides, each one-third as long.

Example: A triangle (3 sides) becomes 12 sides, then 48.

In real life: Snowflake designs in art use this idea.

💡 Did you know? The perimeter keeps growing by 4/3 each step, but the area stays limited.

🧊Making Solids

Solids have faces, edges and vertices. For many solids, Faces + Vertices − Edges = 2.

Example: Cube: 6 + 8 − 12 = 2.

In real life: Boxes are made by folding nets of cardboard.

💡 Did you know? A net is a flat shape that folds into a solid.

👁️Projections of Solids

A solid looks different from the front, the top and the side. These views are its projections.

Example: An upright cylinder looks like a rectangle from the front and a circle from the top.

In real life: Architects draw front, top and side views of buildings.

💡 Did you know? Shadows are a kind of projection.

Quick revision

  • Sierpinski gasket: each step keeps 3 smaller triangles for each one.
  • Koch curve: each segment becomes 4 segments of one-third length.
  • Cube: 6 faces, 8 vertices, 12 edges.
