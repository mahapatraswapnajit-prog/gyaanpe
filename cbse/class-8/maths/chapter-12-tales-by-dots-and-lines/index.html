Class 8 Maths Chapter 12: Tales by Dots and Lines

NCERT Ganita Prakash · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter studies two ways to describe the centre of data: the mean, a balance point, and the median, the middle value. It shows how each changes, why the median resists outliers, and how to find unknown values and means with frequencies.

Key concepts

⚖️Mean as a Balance Point

Mean = sum of values ÷ number of values. It is the point where the data balances.

Example: Mean of 4, 6, 8, 10 = 28 ÷ 4 = 7.

In real life: Average marks of a class are a mean.

💡 Did you know? The distances above the mean and below it cancel out exactly.

📊How the Mean Changes

Adding the same number to every value raises the mean by that number. One very large value pulls the mean up.

Example: Mean of 2, 4, 6 is 4; add 10 to each and the mean becomes 14.

In real life: A single star batter can lift a team's average score.

💡 Did you know? Removing a value equal to the mean does not change the mean.

🔢Median

The median is the middle value after arranging data in order. For an even count, take the mean of the two middle values.

Example: 3, 9, 4, 7, 5 → 3, 4, 5, 7, 9 → median 5. For 2, 4, 6, 8 → median 5.

In real life: House prices are often reported as a median.

💡 Did you know? Half the values lie below the median and half above.

🚨Mean vs Median

The median hardly changes when one value is extreme, but the mean can change a lot.

Example: Salaries 10, 11, 12, 13, 100 (thousand ₹): mean 29.2, median 12.

In real life: Median gives a fairer picture of typical income.

💡 Did you know? An extreme value is called an outlier.

🧩Finding Unknowns

Mean × count = total, so a missing value can be found. With frequencies, mean = sum of (value × frequency) ÷ total frequency.

Example: Mean of 4 numbers is 10, three are 8, 9, 12 → fourth = 40 − 29 = 11.

In real life: Cricket averages are found from total runs and innings.

💡 Did you know? Frequency means how many times a value occurs.

Key terms

Quick revision

  • Mean = sum of values ÷ number of values.
  • The median is the middle value of ordered data.
  • Mean × number of values = total.
