Class 8 Maths Chapter 6: We Distribute, Yet Things Multiply

NCERT Ganita Prakash · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The distributive property connects addition and multiplication. Using it, the chapter builds the identities (a + b)², (a − b)² and (a + b)(a − b), uses them for fast multiplication, and shows how to catch common algebra mistakes.

Key concepts

🔁Properties of Multiplication

Multiplication is commutative (ab = ba), associative ((ab)c = a(bc)) and distributes over addition: a(b + c) = ab + ac.

Example: 7 × 105 = 7 × 100 + 7 × 5 = 735.

In real life: Breaking numbers apart makes mental maths easy at a shop.

💡 Did you know? Distribution also works with subtraction: a(b − c) = ab − ac.

🧮Square of a Sum

(a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b².

Example: 102² = 100² + 2 × 100 × 2 + 2² = 10,404.

In real life: Squaring numbers near 100 becomes quick.

💡 Did you know? Draw a square of side a + b: it splits into a², b² and two rectangles of ab.

➖Square of a Difference

(a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b².

Example: 98² = 100² − 2 × 100 × 2 + 2² = 9,604.

In real life: Useful for numbers just below a round number.

💡 Did you know? It is the same as (a + b)² with b replaced by −b.

✳️Sum times Difference

(a + b)(a − b) = a² − b².

Example: 53 × 47 = 50² − 3² = 2500 − 9 = 2491.

In real life: Mental-maths contests often use this trick.

💡 Did you know? Two numbers equally far from a round number multiply very fast with this.

🐞Spotting Mistakes

(a + b)² is NOT a² + b²; the 2ab part is often forgotten. (a + b)(c + d) = ac + ad + bc + bd.

Example: (3 + 4)² = 49, but 3² + 4² = 25.

In real life: Checking with small numbers catches algebra mistakes.

💡 Did you know? 12 × 13 = (10 + 2)(10 + 3) = 100 + 30 + 20 + 6 = 156.

  • (a + b)(a − b) = a² − b².
