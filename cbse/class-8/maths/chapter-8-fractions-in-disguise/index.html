Class 8 Maths Chapter 8: Fractions in Disguise

NCERT Ganita Prakash · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Percentages are fractions in disguise, out of 100. The chapter converts between fractions, decimals and percentages, finds percentages of quantities, works with increases, decreases and discounts, and turns ratios into percentages.

Key concepts

💯Percent means 'per hundred'

A percentage is a fraction with denominator 100.

Example: 25% = 25/100 = 1/4.

In real life: Marks, discounts and interest rates are given in percent.

💡 Did you know? The word comes from the Latin 'per centum', meaning per hundred.

🔄Converting

Fraction or decimal → percent: multiply by 100. Percent → fraction: divide by 100 and simplify.

Example: 3/20 = 15%; 0.35 = 35%; 120% = 1.2.

In real life: Cricket strike rates are runs per 100 balls.

💡 Did you know? A percentage can be more than 100%, like a price that has doubled (200%).

🧮Percentage of a Quantity

p% of a quantity = quantity × p ÷ 100.

Example: 20% of 450 = 90; 15% of ₹800 = ₹120.

In real life: Calculating tips and discounts uses this.

💡 Did you know? 10% of a number is just the number ÷ 10.

📈Increase and Decrease

Increase by p%: new = old × (100 + p) ÷ 100. Decrease by p%: new = old × (100 − p) ÷ 100.

Example: ₹500 up by 10% → ₹550; then down by 10% → ₹495.

In real life: Sale prices and salary hikes use these.

💡 Did you know? A rise and an equal percentage fall do not cancel out.

🔍Percentages from Ratios

A ratio can be turned into percentages of the whole.

Example: Boys : girls = 2 : 3 → girls are 3/5 = 60% of the class.

In real life: Survey results are usually shown in percentages.

💡 Did you know? Percentages make groups of different sizes easy to compare.

Quick revision

  • x% = x/100.
  • p% of Q = Q × p ÷ 100.
  • A 10% rise followed by a 10% fall gives less than the original.
