Class 8 Maths Chapter 9: The Baudhayana–Pythagoras Theorem

NCERT Ganita Prakash · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter explores squares built on the sides of right triangles and arrives at a² + b² = c², known in India through Baudhayana's Sulba Sutra. It covers doubling squares, Pythagorean triples, testing for right angles and real-life uses.

Key concepts

📐The Theorem

In a right triangle with legs a, b and hypotenuse c: a² + b² = c².

Example: Legs 6 and 8 → c² = 36 + 64 = 100, so c = 10.

In real life: Carpenters use it to check that corners are square.

💡 Did you know? Baudhayana's Sulba Sutra in ancient India described this result for building altars.

🟦Doubling a Square

The square drawn on the diagonal of a square has twice the area of the original square.

Example: An isosceles right triangle with legs 1 has hypotenuse √2.

In real life: This was used to double the size of square altars.

💡 Did you know? Legs 5 and 5 give hypotenuse² = 25 + 25 = 50.

🔢Pythagorean Triples

Whole numbers a, b, c with a² + b² = c² form a triple.

Example: (3, 4, 5), (5, 12, 13), (8, 15, 17); multiples like (6, 8, 10) also work.

In real life: Masons use a rope with 3-4-5 knots to mark a right angle.

💡 Did you know? There are infinitely many Pythagorean triples.

✅Testing for a Right Angle

If the squares of the two shorter sides add up to the square of the longest side, the triangle is right-angled.

Example: 7, 24, 25: 49 + 576 = 625 = 25², so it is right-angled.

In real life: Builders check walls this way.

💡 Did you know? The hypotenuse is always the longest side.

🪜Applications

Use the theorem to find a missing side in real problems.

Example: A 13 m ladder with its foot 5 m from a wall reaches 12 m up (169 − 25 = 144).

In real life: Finding the shortest path across a field.

💡 Did you know? TV sizes are measured along the diagonal.

Quick revision

  • In a right triangle, a² + b² = c².
  • (3, 4, 5) is the smallest Pythagorean triple.
  • The hypotenuse is the longest side.
