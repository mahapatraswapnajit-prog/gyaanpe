Class 8 Science Chapter 1: Exploring the Investigative World of Science

NCERT Curiosity · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Science is a way of finding out: observe, ask a question, make a hypothesis, test it fairly, record data and draw conclusions. The chapter also previews the big themes of the book — microbes, forces, matter, light, ecosystems and climate change.

Key concepts

🔍Science Begins with Questions

Scientists start by observing carefully and asking questions about what they notice.

Example: Why does a bicycle tyre feel harder on a hot afternoon?

In real life: You do science every time you test an idea in the kitchen.

💡 Did you know? Many big discoveries started with a simple 'why?'.

💡Hypothesis

A hypothesis is a testable explanation or prediction that can be checked by an experiment.

Example: 'Seeds sprout faster in warm places' can be tested with two pots.

In real life: Doctors form a hypothesis about an illness before running tests.

💡 Did you know? A good hypothesis can be proved wrong by evidence.

🧪Fair Test

In a fair test only one factor is changed; all other factors are kept the same (controlled).

Example: To test sunlight on plants, keep water, soil and pot size the same.

In real life: Companies test new products with fair comparisons.

💡 Did you know? Repeating an experiment checks that results are reliable.

📊Data and Conclusions

Record observations in tables, look for patterns and draw a conclusion. If results do not support the hypothesis, revise it and test again.

Example: A table of temperature vs time shows how fast water cools.

In real life: Weather forecasts improve as more data is collected.

💡 Did you know? Science changes when new evidence appears — that is its strength.

🌍Science All Around Us

Science helps us understand microbes, forces, matter, light, living communities and big problems like climate change.

Example: Burning coal and petrol adds carbon dioxide, which warms the Earth.

In real life: Choosing to walk or cycle short distances reduces pollution.

💡 Did you know? Scientists across the world share results so others can check them.

Key terms

TermMeaning
ObservationNoticing something carefully using the senses or tools
HypothesisA testable explanation
Fair testChanging only one factor at a time
Controlled variableA factor kept the same
ConclusionWhat the evidence shows

Quick revision

  • Change only one factor in a fair test.
  • Repeat experiments to check results.
  • Scientific ideas can change with new evidence.

Practice questions

Q1. A scientific investigation usually starts with

  1. Guessing without looking
  2. Observing and asking a question
  3. Writing the conclusion
  4. Publishing results
Show answerB) Observing and asking a question

Q2. A testable explanation is called a

  1. Table
  2. Hypothesis
  3. Observation
  4. Conclusion
Show answerB) Hypothesis

Q3. In a fair test you change

  1. Two factors together
  2. Only one factor at a time
  3. No factors
  4. All the factors
Show answerB) Only one factor at a time

Q4. A factor kept the same in an experiment is a

  1. Hypothesis
  2. Result
  3. Controlled variable
  4. Changed variable
Show answerC) Controlled variable

Q5. Why do scientists repeat an experiment?

  1. It is never needed
  2. To waste time
  3. To check the results are reliable
  4. To always change the hypothesis
Show answerC) To check the results are reliable
