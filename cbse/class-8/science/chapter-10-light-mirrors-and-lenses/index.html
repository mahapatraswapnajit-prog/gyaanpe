Class 8 Science Chapter 10: Light: Mirrors and Lenses

NCERT Curiosity · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter covers the laws of reflection, images in plane mirrors, the different images formed by concave and convex mirrors and their uses, and how convex and concave lenses bend light in magnifiers, spectacles and cameras.

Key concepts

🪞Laws of Reflection

The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection. The incident ray, reflected ray and normal lie in the same plane.

Example: A ray hitting a mirror at 30° to the normal reflects at 30°.

In real life: Periscopes use two mirrors to see over walls.

💡 Did you know? The normal is a line perpendicular to the mirror at the point where light hits it.

🔁Plane Mirror Images

A plane mirror forms an image that is virtual, erect, the same size, as far behind the mirror as the object is in front, and laterally inverted.

Example: Your right hand looks like the left hand in a mirror.

In real life: Dressing mirrors are plane mirrors.

💡 Did you know? 'AMBULANCE' is written reversed on vehicles so drivers read it correctly in mirrors.

🥄Concave Mirrors

A concave mirror curves inward and brings light rays together. Close up it gives an enlarged, erect image; far away, an inverted image.

Example: The inside of a shiny spoon acts like a concave mirror.

In real life: Torch and headlight reflectors are concave.

💡 Did you know? Dentists use concave mirrors to see enlarged images of teeth.

🚗Convex Mirrors

A convex mirror bulges outward and spreads rays apart. It always gives an erect, smaller image and a wide field of view.

Example: Rear-view mirrors of vehicles are convex.

In real life: Shops use them to watch large areas.

💡 Did you know? Convex mirrors at sharp turns help drivers see oncoming traffic.

🔍Lenses

A lens bends (refracts) light. A convex lens is thicker in the middle and brings rays together; a concave lens is thinner in the middle and spreads them out.

Example: A magnifying glass is a convex lens.

In real life: Cameras, microscopes and our own eyes use lenses.

💡 Did you know? Spectacles for short-sightedness use concave lenses.

Key terms

TermMeaning
NormalLine perpendicular to the mirror
Lateral inversionLeft-right swap in a mirror image
Concave mirrorCurves inward; converges light
Convex mirrorBulges outward; diverges light
Convex lensThicker in the middle; converges light

Quick revision

  • Angle of incidence = angle of reflection.
  • Vehicle rear-view mirrors are convex.
  • A magnifying glass is a convex lens.
