Class 8 Science Chapter 12: How Nature Works in Harmony

NCERT Curiosity · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Nature works as connected ecosystems of living and non-living parts. The chapter explains producers, consumers and decomposers, food chains and webs, kinds of interactions such as mutualism and parasitism, and how balance is kept in nature.

Key concepts

🌳Ecosystems

An ecosystem includes living things (plants, animals, microbes) and non-living things (sunlight, air, water, soil) interacting together.

Example: A pond has fish, frogs, plants, water, mud and sunlight.

In real life: A school garden is a small ecosystem.

💡 Did you know? Living things that live together in an area form a community.

🍃Producers, Consumers, Decomposers

Green plants are producers. Animals are consumers: herbivores, carnivores or omnivores. Fungi and bacteria are decomposers that break down dead matter.

Example: Grass (producer) → deer (herbivore) → tiger (carnivore).

In real life: Compost pits use decomposers to make manure.

💡 Did you know? Without decomposers, dead leaves and bodies would pile up.

🔗Food Chains and Webs

A food chain shows who eats whom. Many connected food chains form a food web.

Example: Grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → eagle.

In real life: Using pesticides can harm animals higher up the chain.

💡 Did you know? Energy passes along the chain, starting from the Sun.

🤝Interactions

Organisms interact in many ways: predation, competition, mutualism (both benefit) and parasitism (one benefits, one is harmed).

Example: Bees get nectar and flowers get pollinated (mutualism); Cuscuta (amarbel) lives on other plants (parasite).

In real life: Farmers keep bees to improve crop yields.

💡 Did you know? A lichen is a partnership of a fungus and an alga.

⚖️Balance in Nature

Every organism matters. A change in one population causes changes in others.

Example: If tigers disappear, deer may increase and overgraze the forest.

In real life: Protecting forests and wetlands keeps ecosystems balanced.

💡 Did you know? Nutrients are recycled again and again through decomposers.

Quick revision

  • Green plants are producers.
  • Many food chains together make a food web.
  • Removing one species can affect many others.

Practice questions

Q1. Producers in an ecosystem are

  1. Lions
  2. Green plants
  3. Humans
  4. Mushrooms
Show answerB) Green plants

Q2. Decomposers include

  1. Eagles
  2. Fungi and bacteria
  3. Grass
  4. Deer
Show answerB) Fungi and bacteria

Q3. Which is a correct food chain?

  1. Snake → Frog → Grass → Grasshopper
  2. Frog → Grass → Snake → Grasshopper
  3. Grasshopper → Grass → Frog → Snake
  4. Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake
Show answerD) Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake

Q4. Which is a non-living part of an ecosystem?

  1. Bacteria
  2. Grass
  3. Frog
  4. Sunlight
Show answerD) Sunlight

Q5. Bees and flowers both benefit from each other. This is

  1. Mutualism
  2. Parasitism
  3. Competition
  4. Predation
Show answerA) Mutualism
