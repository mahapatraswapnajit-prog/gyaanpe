Class 8 Science Chapter 2: The Invisible Living World: Beyond our Naked Eye

NCERT Curiosity · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

All living things are made of cells, which come in many shapes and build up tissues, organs and systems. The chapter also explores microorganisms, how they help in making curd, bread and medicines, and how some cause disease or spoil food.

Key concepts

🔬The Cell

The cell is the basic unit of life. Every living thing is made of one or more cells.

Example: Robert Hooke saw tiny box-like cells in a thin slice of cork in 1665.

In real life: Blood tests look at cells under a microscope.

💡 Did you know? An ostrich egg yolk is a single large cell.

🌿Plant and Animal Cells

Both have a cell membrane, cytoplasm and nucleus. Plant cells also have a cell wall, chloroplasts and a large vacuole.

Example: Chloroplasts are green and make food by photosynthesis.

In real life: Onion peel and cheek cells are classic school microscope samples.

💡 Did you know? Animal cells have no cell wall, so their shapes can vary.

🧩Shapes and Levels

Cells have shapes that suit their jobs. Cells form tissues, tissues form organs, organs form organ systems, and these make an organism.

Example: Nerve cells are long to carry messages; red blood cells are disc-shaped.

In real life: Your heart is an organ in the circulatory system.

💡 Did you know? Muscle cells together form muscle tissue.

🦠Microorganisms

Microbes are living things too small to see without a microscope: bacteria, fungi, protozoa and algae. Viruses multiply only inside living cells.

Example: Amoeba is a protozoan; yeast is a fungus.

In real life: A drop of pond water contains thousands of microbes.

💡 Did you know? Some bacteria can double their numbers in about 20 minutes in good conditions.

🍞Microbes and Us

Many microbes help us; some cause disease or spoil food.

Example: Lactobacillus turns milk into curd; yeast makes dough rise by releasing carbon dioxide.

In real life: Rhizobium bacteria in pulse roots add nitrogen to the soil.

💡 Did you know? Alexander Fleming discovered the antibiotic penicillin from a mould in 1928.

Quick revision

  • Cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism.
  • Plant cells have a cell wall; animal cells do not.
  • Viruses multiply only inside living cells.

Practice questions

Q1. The basic unit of life is the

  1. Cell
  2. Atom
  3. Tissue
  4. Organ
Show answerA) Cell

Q2. Which part is found in plant cells but not in animal cells?

  1. Cytoplasm
  2. Cell membrane
  3. Nucleus
  4. Cell wall
Show answerD) Cell wall

Q3. The correct order of organisation is

  1. Organ → cell → tissue → organ system
  2. Cell → tissue → organ → organ system
  3. Tissue → cell → organ → organ system
  4. Cell → organ → tissue → organ system
Show answerB) Cell → tissue → organ → organ system

Q4. Curd is made from milk by

  1. Lactobacillus bacteria
  2. Yeast
  3. A virus
  4. Algae
Show answerA) Lactobacillus bacteria

Q5. Bread dough rises because yeast releases

  1. Nitrogen
  2. Carbon dioxide
  3. Salt
  4. Oxygen
Show answerB) Carbon dioxide
