Class 8 Science Chapter 4: Electricity: Magnetic and Heating Effects

NCERT Curiosity · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Electric current has a magnetic effect, used in electromagnets, bells and motors, and a heating effect, used in heaters and irons and controlled by fuses and MCBs. The chapter also shows how a cell turns chemical energy into electricity.

Key concepts

🧭Magnetic Effect of Current

A wire carrying electric current behaves like a magnet and deflects a nearby compass needle.

Example: Switch the current on and the compass needle turns; switch off and it returns.

In real life: Every electric motor depends on this effect.

💡 Did you know? Hans Christian Oersted noticed this in 1820.

🧲Electromagnets

Wind insulated wire around an iron nail and pass current: it becomes a magnet that works only while current flows.

Example: More turns of wire or more current make a stronger electromagnet.

In real life: Electric bells, speakers and motors use electromagnets.

💡 Did you know? Huge electromagnets lift scrap iron in junkyards.

🔥Heating Effect of Current

When current flows through a wire, the wire gets hot. Wires with high resistance heat more.

Example: Heaters, irons and toasters use nichrome coils.

In real life: This is why an iron gets hot when switched on.

💡 Did you know? Old bulbs glowed because their tungsten filament became white-hot.

🛡️Safety: Fuse and MCB

A fuse wire melts and breaks the circuit when too much current flows. An MCB switches off automatically.

Example: Overloading a socket with many appliances can melt a fuse.

In real life: MCB boxes protect modern homes.

💡 Did you know? Never use a thick copper wire in place of fuse wire.

🔋How a Cell Makes Electricity

A cell changes chemical energy into electrical energy. A battery is two or more cells joined together.

Example: A lemon with a copper strip and a zinc strip makes a small cell.

In real life: Mobile phones run on rechargeable lithium-ion batteries.

💡 Did you know? Alessandro Volta built one of the first batteries around 1800.

Quick revision

  • A current-carrying wire deflects a compass needle.
  • An electromagnet works only while current flows.
  • A fuse melts when too much current flows.

Practice questions

Q1. A compass needle moves near a current-carrying wire. This shows the

  1. Sound effect of current
  2. Chemical effect of current
  3. Heating effect of current
  4. Magnetic effect of current
Show answerD) Magnetic effect of current

Q2. Who discovered this effect in 1820?

  1. Michael Faraday
  2. Hans Christian Oersted
  3. Alessandro Volta
  4. Thomas Edison
Show answerB) Hans Christian Oersted

Q3. An electromagnet is made by

  1. Cooling a copper wire
  2. Winding wire around iron and passing current
  3. Heating a nail
  4. Rubbing iron with a magnet
Show answerB) Winding wire around iron and passing current

Q4. An electromagnet becomes stronger if you

  1. Use a plastic core
  2. Reduce the current
  3. Increase the turns of wire
  4. Remove the iron core
Show answerC) Increase the turns of wire

Q5. The heating coil of an electric iron is usually made of

  1. Copper
  2. Nichrome
  3. Plastic
  4. Glass
Show answerB) Nichrome
