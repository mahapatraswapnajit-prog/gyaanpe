Class 8 Science Chapter 7: Particulate Nature of Matter

NCERT Curiosity · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Matter is made of tiny moving particles with spaces between them. How tightly the particles are packed and how they move decide whether matter is a solid, liquid or gas, and explain diffusion, compression and the effect of heating.

Key concepts

⚛️Matter is Made of Particles

All matter is made of extremely tiny particles with spaces between them. The particles are always moving and attract one another.

Example: A crystal of potassium permanganate colours a whole beaker of water.

In real life: Smells reach you because particles travel through air.

💡 Did you know? Particles are far too small to see even with an ordinary microscope.

🧱Solids

In solids, particles are tightly packed and vibrate in fixed positions. Solids have a fixed shape and fixed volume.

Example: A stone keeps its shape in any container.

In real life: Bridges and buildings rely on strong solids.

💡 Did you know? Strong forces of attraction hold solid particles together.

💧Liquids

In liquids, particles are close but can slide past one another. Liquids have a fixed volume but take the shape of their container.

Example: Milk takes the shape of the glass it is poured into.

In real life: Hydraulic brakes use the fact that liquids hardly compress.

💡 Did you know? Liquids are very hard to compress.

💨Gases

In gases, particles are far apart and move freely and fast. Gases have no fixed shape or volume and are easily compressed.

Example: Cooking gas is compressed into cylinders.

In real life: Tyres are filled with compressed air.

💡 Did you know? Interparticle spaces are largest in gases.

🌫️Diffusion and Heat

Particles mixing on their own is diffusion; it is fastest in gases. Heating makes particles move faster, which can change the state.

Example: The smell of incense spreads across a room.

In real life: Hot tea dissolves sugar faster than cold tea.

💡 Did you know? Sugar dissolves into the spaces between water particles.

Key terms

TermMeaning
ParticleTiny unit that matter is made of
Interparticle spaceGap between particles
DiffusionParticles mixing on their own
CompressibleCan be squeezed into less space
States of matterSolid, liquid and gas

Quick revision

  • Solids: fixed shape and volume.
  • Liquids: fixed volume, no fixed shape.
  • Gases: no fixed shape or volume; diffusion is fastest.
