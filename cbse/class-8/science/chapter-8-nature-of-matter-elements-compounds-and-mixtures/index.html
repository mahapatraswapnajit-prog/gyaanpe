Class 8 Science Chapter 8: Nature of Matter: Elements, Compounds and Mixtures

NCERT Curiosity · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Matter can be classified as mixtures or pure substances, and pure substances as elements or compounds. The chapter explains how they differ, how mixtures are separated, their everyday uses, and minerals and ores found in the Earth.

Key concepts

🥗Mixtures

A mixture has two or more substances mixed in any proportion; each keeps its own properties and can be separated by physical methods.

Example: Salt water, air, and sand with iron filings are mixtures.

In real life: Filters in water purifiers separate solid impurities.

💡 Did you know? Homogeneous mixtures look uniform (salt water); heterogeneous ones do not (sand in water).

💎Pure Substances

A pure substance has a fixed composition and the same properties throughout. Elements and compounds are pure substances.

Example: Distilled water and pure gold are pure substances.

In real life: Medicines need very pure substances.

💡 Did you know? Pure water boils at 100 °C at sea level.

🧪Elements

An element is made of only one kind of atom and cannot be broken into simpler substances by chemical means. About 118 elements are known.

Example: Oxygen (O), iron (Fe), gold (Au) and carbon (C) are elements.

In real life: Copper is used in electric wires.

💡 Did you know? Elements are grouped as metals, non-metals and metalloids.

🔗Compounds

A compound forms when elements combine chemically in a fixed ratio. Its properties differ from those of its elements.

Example: Water (H₂O) is made of hydrogen and oxygen; common salt is sodium chloride (NaCl).

In real life: Baking soda is a compound used in cooking.

💡 Did you know? Hydrogen burns and oxygen helps burning, yet water puts out fire.

⛏️Minerals

Minerals are naturally occurring solids with a definite composition. Ores are minerals from which metals are extracted.

Example: Bauxite is an ore of aluminium; haematite is an ore of iron.

In real life: Aluminium from bauxite is used in utensils and aircraft.

💡 Did you know? Odisha is rich in iron ore, bauxite and chromite.

Key terms

TermMeaning
ElementMade of only one kind of atom
CompoundElements combined chemically in a fixed ratio
MixtureSubstances mixed in any ratio
Pure substanceFixed composition throughout
OreMineral from which a metal is extracted

Quick revision

  • Water (H₂O) is a compound; air is a mixture.
  • Mixtures can be separated by physical methods.
  • Bauxite is an ore of aluminium.
