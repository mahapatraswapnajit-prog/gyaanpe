Class 8 Science Chapter 9: The Amazing World of Solutes, Solvents and Solutions

NCERT Curiosity · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Solutions form when solutes dissolve in solvents. The chapter explores saturated solutions, how temperature and pressure change solubility of solids and gases, and how density decides whether an object floats or sinks.

Key concepts

🥤Solute, Solvent, Solution

The substance that dissolves is the solute; the one it dissolves in is the solvent; together they make a solution.

Example: In sugar water, sugar is the solute and water is the solvent.

In real life: Nimbu-pani is a solution with more than one solute.

💡 Did you know? Water dissolves so many substances that it is called the universal solvent.

🧂Saturated Solutions

A solution that cannot dissolve any more solute at a given temperature is saturated.

Example: Keep adding salt to water; at some point it settles at the bottom.

In real life: Pickles are made in strong salt solutions.

💡 Did you know? Solubility is how much solute dissolves in a fixed amount of solvent.

🌡️Temperature and Solubility

Most solids dissolve more in hot water. Gases dissolve less in warm water.

Example: More sugar dissolves in hot milk than in cold milk.

In real life: A cold soda stays fizzy longer than a warm one.

💡 Did you know? Fish can struggle in warm water because it holds less oxygen.

🍾Gases and Pressure

More gas dissolves in a liquid under higher pressure.

Example: Opening a soda bottle lowers pressure, so carbon dioxide bubbles out.

In real life: Soft drinks are bottled under pressure.

💡 Did you know? Divers must rise slowly because dissolved gases come out as pressure drops.

⚖️Density, Floating and Sinking

Density = mass ÷ volume. Water's density is about 1 g/cm³. Objects less dense than a liquid float in it.

Example: Ice (about 0.92 g/cm³) floats on water.

In real life: Oil floats on water because it is less dense.

💡 Did you know? Salty sea water is denser, so it is easier to float in the sea.
