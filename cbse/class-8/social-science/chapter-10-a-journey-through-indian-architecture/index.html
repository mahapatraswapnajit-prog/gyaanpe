Class 8 Social Science Chapter 10: A Journey Through Indian Architecture

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Indian architecture spans planned Harappan cities, Buddhist stupas and rock-cut caves, Nagara, Dravida and Kalinga temples, Indo-Islamic domes and minarets, and stepwells and forts — a long tradition of skill and design.

Key concepts

🧱Early Cities

Harappan cities had planned streets, brick houses and covered drains.

Example: Mohenjo-daro's Great Bath is a famous Harappan structure.

In real life: Town planning today still uses grid streets and drains.

💡 Did you know? Harappans used baked bricks of standard sizes.

☸️Stupas and Caves

Buddhist stupas are dome-shaped mounds; caves were carved from rock as monasteries and halls.

Example: The Sanchi Stupa; the Ajanta and Ellora caves.

In real life: Udayagiri and Khandagiri caves are in Bhubaneswar.

💡 Did you know? Ellora's Kailasa temple was carved from a single rock from the top down.

🛕Temple Styles

North Indian temples follow the Nagara style with curved towers (shikhara); South Indian temples follow the Dravida style with tall gateways (gopurams).

Example: The Brihadeeswara Temple, Thanjavur, is a great Dravida temple.

In real life: Konark Sun Temple is built like a giant chariot with stone wheels.

💡 Did you know? Odisha's temples, like the Lingaraj Temple, form a distinct Kalinga style.

🕌Indo-Islamic Architecture

From the 13th century, arches, domes and minarets blended with Indian craftsmanship.

Example: Qutub Minar, Gol Gumbaz and the Taj Mahal.

In real life: Many railway stations and buildings copied these domes.

💡 Did you know? The Taj Mahal was built by Shah Jahan in memory of Mumtaz Mahal.

💧Stepwells, Forts and More

Indians built stepwells to store water, and strong forts and palaces.

Example: Rani ki Vav in Gujarat is a beautifully carved stepwell.

In real life: Visiting heritage sites helps protect them.

💡 Did you know? Many of these are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
