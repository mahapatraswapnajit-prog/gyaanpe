Class 8 Social Science Chapter 11: The Role of the Judiciary in Our Society

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Courts settle disputes, interpret laws and protect rights. The chapter explains India's three-level court system, why judicial independence matters, tools like PIL, legal aid and Lok Adalats, and the challenge of long delays.

Key concepts

⚖️Why Courts?

The judiciary settles disputes, interprets laws and protects the rights of citizens.

Example: A court decides a land dispute between two neighbours.

In real life: Rule of law means no one is above the law.

💡 Did you know? Courts make sure everyone, including the government, follows the law.

🏛️Structure of Courts

India has an integrated judiciary: the Supreme Court at the top, High Courts in states, and district and subordinate courts below.

Example: The Orissa High Court is in Cuttack.

In real life: Appeals can move from lower courts to higher courts.

💡 Did you know? The Supreme Court is in New Delhi and is headed by the Chief Justice of India.

🛡️Independent Judiciary

Judges must be free from pressure by the government or powerful people so they can decide fairly.

Example: Judges cannot be removed easily; their salaries are protected.

In real life: Independence of courts protects ordinary citizens.

💡 Did you know? The Supreme Court can strike down laws that violate the Constitution (judicial review).

📝Access to Justice

Courts can be approached through Public Interest Litigation (PIL) on issues affecting many people. Free legal aid helps those who cannot pay.

Example: PILs have been used for clean air and children's rights.

In real life: Legal aid clinics help poor families.

💡 Did you know? Lok Adalats settle cases quickly through compromise.

⏳Challenges

Many cases are pending for years. Faster justice needs more judges, technology and simpler procedures.

Example: E-courts allow online filing and video hearings.

In real life: Lok Adalats and mediation reduce delays.

💡 Did you know? 'Justice delayed is justice denied' is a well-known saying.
