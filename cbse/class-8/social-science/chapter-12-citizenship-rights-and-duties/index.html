Class 8 Social Science Chapter 12: Citizenship: Rights and Duties

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Citizenship brings rights and duties. The chapter explains the six Fundamental Rights, the Fundamental Duties added in 1976, why rights and duties go together, and how active citizens strengthen democracy.

Key concepts

🪪Who is a Citizen?

A citizen is a full member of a country with rights and duties under its Constitution.

Example: Citizenship can be gained by birth, descent, registration or naturalisation.

In real life: Passports are issued to citizens.

💡 Did you know? India has single citizenship for the whole country.

📘Fundamental Rights

The Constitution guarantees six Fundamental Rights: Equality, Freedom, Against Exploitation, Freedom of Religion, Cultural and Educational Rights, and Constitutional Remedies.

Example: The Right to Education gives free education to children aged 6–14.

In real life: If rights are violated, citizens can go to court.

💡 Did you know? Dr B. R. Ambedkar called the Right to Constitutional Remedies the 'heart and soul' of the Constitution.

🤲Fundamental Duties

Citizens also have Fundamental Duties, such as respecting the Constitution, the National Flag and Anthem, protecting the environment and public property.

Example: Not littering and saving water are duties we can practise daily.

In real life: Cleanliness drives reflect our duty to the nation.

💡 Did you know? Fundamental Duties were added to the Constitution in 1976.

🔗Rights and Duties Together

Rights and duties go hand in hand: enjoying freedom means respecting others' freedom.

Example: Freedom of speech does not allow spreading hatred.

In real life: Reporting child labour helps protect rights.

💡 Did you know? Child labour is banned under the Right against Exploitation.

🌟Active Citizenship

Good citizens vote, follow laws, help the community and take part in public life.

Example: Volunteering in a cleanliness or tree-planting drive.

In real life: Students can join eco-clubs and NSS.

💡 Did you know? Gram Sabhas let citizens discuss village issues directly.
