Class 8 Social Science Chapter 4: The Colonial Era in India

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

European traders came to India and the British East India Company turned trade into rule after Plassey and Buxar. The chapter explains colonialism's economic harm, early resistance such as the Paika Rebellion, and the Revolt of 1857 that ended Company rule.

Key concepts

⛵Europeans Arrive

Portuguese, Dutch, French and British traders came to India for spices and textiles.

Example: Vasco da Gama reached Calicut in 1498 by sea.

In real life: Old Dutch and Portuguese buildings still stand in Kochi and Goa.

💡 Did you know? The English East India Company was founded in 1600.

🏴From Trade to Rule

The East India Company used wars and alliances to gain political power.

Example: The Battle of Plassey (1757) and the Battle of Buxar (1764) gave it control of Bengal.

In real life: Bengal, Bihar and Odisha came under Company control after Buxar.

💡 Did you know? Colonialism means one country controlling another for its own benefit.

💸Economic Impact

British rule drained wealth from India through heavy taxes and unfair trade. Indian handloom industries declined as British machine-made cloth flooded markets.

Example: Weavers of Bengal lost their livelihoods.

In real life: Khadi became a symbol of self-reliance in the freedom movement.

💡 Did you know? Repeated famines killed millions during colonial rule.

✊Early Resistance

Many peasants, tribals and soldiers rose against colonial rule.

Example: The Paika Rebellion of Odisha (1817) and the Santhal Hul (1855) were major uprisings.

In real life: Odisha celebrates the Paika heroes every year.

💡 Did you know? Bakshi Jagabandhu led the Paika Rebellion.

🔥The Revolt of 1857

In 1857 soldiers and many common people rose against the Company. Leaders included Mangal Pandey, Rani Lakshmibai, Tantia Tope and Bahadur Shah Zafar.

Example: After the revolt, the British Crown took direct control of India in 1858.

In real life: Jhansi fort recalls Rani Lakshmibai's bravery.

💡 Did you know? It is often called India's First War of Independence.
