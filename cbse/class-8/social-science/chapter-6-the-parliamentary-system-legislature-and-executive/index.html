Class 8 Social Science Chapter 6: The Parliamentary System: Legislature and Executive

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

India follows a parliamentary system. The chapter explains Parliament's structure, how bills become laws, how Parliament keeps the executive accountable through questions, debates and the budget, and how Union and State governments are organised.

Key concepts

🏛️India's Parliament

Parliament has the President and two Houses: the Lok Sabha (House of the People) and the Rajya Sabha (Council of States).

Example: Members of the Lok Sabha are elected directly by voters.

In real life: The new Parliament building was opened in New Delhi in 2023.

💡 Did you know? The Rajya Sabha is a permanent House; one-third of its members retire every two years.

📜How a Law is Made

A bill is discussed and passed by both Houses and then gets the President's assent to become an Act.

Example: A Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha.

In real life: Laws on education, roads and safety come through this process.

💡 Did you know? Committees study bills in detail before voting.

❓Holding the Government Accountable

Parliament questions ministers, debates policies and can pass a no-confidence motion.

Example: Question Hour is when members ask ministers questions.

In real life: News channels report debates from Parliament.

💡 Did you know? The government must keep the support of the Lok Sabha majority to stay in power.

💰Control over Money

The government cannot spend public money without Parliament's approval. The Union Budget is presented every year.

Example: The Finance Minister presents the Budget, usually on 1 February.

In real life: The Budget decides money for schools, hospitals and roads.

💡 Did you know? Taxes too need Parliament's approval.

👥Union and State Governments

The President is the head of state; the Prime Minister leads the government with the Council of Ministers. In states, the Governor is head and the Chief Minister leads the government with the Legislative Assembly.

Example: Odisha's Legislative Assembly sits in Bhubaneswar.

In real life: Your MLA represents you in the state assembly.

💡 Did you know? Ministers are collectively responsible to the elected House.

Quick revision

  • Parliament = President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha.
  • A bill becomes law after both Houses pass it and the President assents.
  • A Money Bill starts only in the Lok Sabha.
