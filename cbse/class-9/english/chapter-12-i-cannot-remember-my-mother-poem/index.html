Class 9 English Chapter 12: I Cannot Remember My Mother (Poem)

NCERT Kaveri · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

Tagore's poem shows a speaker who cannot recall his mother's face but senses her presence through a tune, a flower's fragrance and the sky, using sensory imagery and repetition.

Key concepts

💭About the Poem

In this poem, Rabindranath Tagore's speaker cannot recall his mother's face, as she died when he was very young, yet he feels her presence through a tune, a fragrance and the sky.

Example: Her memory returns in a song heard while playing, in the scent of flowers on autumn mornings, and in the quiet sky.

In real life: Many people keep memories alive through smells and songs.

💡 Did you know? Tagore wrote in Bengali and translated many poems himself.

👃Memory through the Senses

Sensory imagery connects feelings to sound, smell and sight. Memories often return through these senses.

Example: The scent of shiuli (night jasmine) flowers in autumn.

In real life: A familiar song can bring back childhood.

💡 Did you know? Smell is strongly linked to memory in the brain.

🕊️Tone and Mood

The tone is tender and longing; the mood is gentle and reflective, not loudly sad.

Example: A soft, quiet poem about love and absence.

In real life: Music sets mood in films.

💡 Did you know? Tone is the poet's attitude; mood is the reader's feeling.

📜About Tagore

Tagore (1861–1941) won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1913 for Gitanjali — the first non-European to win it. He wrote India's national anthem and founded Visva-Bharati at Santiniketan.

Example: 'Jana Gana Mana' and Bangladesh's 'Amar Shonar Bangla' are his.

In real life: Santiniketan is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

💡 Did you know? He was called 'Gurudev'.

🔁Repetition

Repeating a key line (like the title's idea) stresses the speaker's longing.

Example: The idea 'I cannot remember my mother' returns in each part.

In real life: Speeches repeat key phrases for effect.

💡 Did you know? Repetition can show deep emotion.
