Class 9 English Chapter 3: Winds of Change

NCERT Kaveri · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The lesson traces the traditional Indian hand fan as craft and heritage, how it adapts to modern times, and practises idioms and contrast connectors.

Key concepts

🪭About the Lesson

The lesson looks at the traditional Indian hand fan (pankha) — its craft, materials and place in daily life — and how it is changing with time.

Example: Hand fans are made of palm leaves, bamboo, cloth or peacock feathers.

In real life: Handicraft fairs still sell decorated pankhas.

💡 Did you know? Large ceiling 'punkahs' were once pulled by ropes.

🌿Heritage and Craft

Craftspeople weave, paint and embroider fans with regional designs, keeping heritage alive.

Example: A fan woven from palm leaves in a coastal village.

In real life: GI tags protect regional crafts.

💡 Did you know? Traditional crafts often use local, eco-friendly materials.

🔄Change and Adaptability

Electric fans replaced many hand fans, but crafts survive by adapting — as décor, gifts and cultural symbols.

Example: Designers turn traditional fans into wall art.

In real life: Startups bring rural crafts online.

💡 Did you know? 'Winds of change' is an idiom for big changes happening.

💬Idioms

An idiom has a meaning different from its words. 'Winds of change' = forces bringing change; 'stand the test of time' = last long; 'keep alive' = preserve.

Example: This craft has stood the test of time.

In real life: News headlines love idioms.

💡 Did you know? Idioms make writing lively but must be used correctly.

🔗Connectors of Contrast

Although, however, but, yet, whereas and despite show contrast.

Example: Although electric fans are common, hand fans are still made.

In real life: Debates use contrast connectors.

💡 Did you know? 'Despite' is followed by a noun or -ing form.
