Class 9 Maths Chapter 10: How Quantities Combine: Understanding Data

NCERT Ganita Manjari · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter explains how quantities combine using weighted means — for marks, mixtures and prices — and how stacked and 100% stacked bar charts display parts of a whole.

Key concepts

⚖️Weighted Mean

A weighted mean gives more importance to some values: (sum of weight × value) ÷ (sum of weights).

Example: Test 60 (weight 1), exam 80 (weight 3): (60 + 240) ÷ 4 = 75.

In real life: Average price of mixed purchases is a weighted mean.

💡 Did you know? Report cards often weigh exams more than class tests.

🧪Mixtures

When mixtures combine, the strength of the result is a weighted mean of the strengths.

Example: 1 L of 10% juice + 3 L of 30% juice → (10 + 90) ÷ 4 = 25%.

In real life: Mixing paints, tea or fertiliser uses this idea.

💡 Did you know? Equal amounts give the simple average: 10% and 30% → 20%.

🛒Average Price

Average cost = total cost ÷ total quantity, not the average of prices.

Example: 2 kg at ₹40 and 3 kg at ₹50: (80 + 150) ÷ 5 = ₹46 per kg.

In real life: Shopkeepers set prices for mixed goods this way.

💡 Did you know? The larger quantity pulls the average towards its price.

📊Stacked Bar Charts

A stacked bar shows parts of a total stacked on top of each other in one bar.

Example: Each bar = total students, split into boys and girls.

In real life: Election results by region use stacked bars.

💡 Did you know? Stacked bars show both totals and their parts.

💯100% Stacked Bars

In a 100% stacked bar, every bar has the same height (100%) and shows the percentage share of each part.

Example: Compare the share of girls across classes of different sizes.

In real life: Budget shares are often shown this way.

💡 Did you know? They show proportions, not actual totals.

Key terms

TermMeaning
Weighted meanMean giving different importance to values
WeightImportance or quantity of a value
MixtureCombination of parts with different strengths

Quick revision

  • Weighted mean = Σ(weight × value) ÷ Σ(weights).
  • Equal amounts → simple average.
  • Each bar in a 100% stacked chart totals 100%.

  2. 75
  3. 65
  4. 80
Show answerB) 75

  1. 40%

  3. 25%

Show answerD) 20%

  1. 30%
  2. 40%
