Class 9 Maths Chapter 11: The World of Algorithms

NCERT Ganita Manjari · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter introduces algorithmic thinking: clear step-by-step procedures to find divisors, the greatest common divisor, and Euclid's subtraction algorithm, linking GCD and LCM.

Key concepts

🤖What is an Algorithm?

An algorithm is a finite, step-by-step set of clear instructions to solve a problem.

Example: A recipe or the steps for long division are algorithms.

In real life: Every app on your phone runs algorithms.

💡 Did you know? The word comes from the name of the mathematician al-Khwarizmi.

🔢Finding Divisors

To list divisors of n, test numbers from 1 up to the square root of n; each divisor found gives a pair.

Example: Divisors of 36: 1×36, 2×18, 3×12, 4×9, 6×6.

In real life: Arranging chairs in equal rows needs divisors.

💡 Did you know? A number with exactly two divisors is prime.

🤝GCD (HCF)

The greatest common divisor is the largest number dividing both numbers.

Example: GCD(12, 18) = 6.

In real life: Cutting two ribbons into equal longest pieces.

💡 Did you know? If GCD is 1, the numbers are coprime, like 17 and 5.

➖Euclid's Subtraction Algorithm

Replace the larger number by the difference of the two; repeat until both are equal. That number is the GCD.

Example: 48, 18 → 30, 18 → 12, 18 → 12, 6 → 6, 6. GCD = 6.

In real life: Computers use the faster division version.

💡 Did you know? Euclid described this method in his 'Elements' over 2,000 years ago.

🔗GCD and LCM

For two numbers, GCD × LCM = product of the numbers.

Example: 12 × 18 = 216 = 6 × 36, so LCM(12, 18) = 36.

In real life: Bus timetables use LCM to find when buses meet.

💡 Did you know? An algorithm must always stop after a finite number of steps.

Quick revision

  • Euclid's algorithm repeatedly subtracts the smaller from the larger.
  • GCD × LCM = product of two numbers.
  • An algorithm must end in finitely many steps.
