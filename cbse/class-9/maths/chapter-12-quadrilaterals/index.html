Class 9 Maths Chapter 12: Quadrilaterals

NCERT Ganita Manjari · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter proves properties of parallelograms, conditions for a quadrilateral to be a parallelogram, the midpoint theorem and its converse, and properties of rectangles, rhombuses and squares.

Key concepts

▱Parallelogram Properties

In a parallelogram, opposite sides are equal, opposite angles are equal, adjacent angles add to 180°, and diagonals bisect each other.

Example: One angle 75° → adjacent angle 105°.

In real life: Folding gates keep these properties as they move.

💡 Did you know? A diagonal divides a parallelogram into two congruent triangles.

🔍Proving a Parallelogram

A quadrilateral is a parallelogram if: both pairs of opposite sides are equal, or diagonals bisect each other, or one pair of opposite sides is equal and parallel.

Example: Two equal and parallel sticks joined at the ends form a parallelogram.

In real life: Carpenters check frames using diagonals.

💡 Did you know? Any one of these conditions is enough.

➗Midpoint Theorem

The segment joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and half its length.

Example: Third side 10 cm → midsegment 5 cm.

In real life: Used in proofs and in dividing land fairly.

💡 Did you know? Its converse: a line through the midpoint of one side, parallel to another, bisects the third side.

⬛Special Parallelograms

A parallelogram with equal diagonals is a rectangle; with perpendicular diagonals is a rhombus; with both, a square.

Example: Square: diagonals equal and perpendicular.

In real life: Tiles and window frames use these shapes.

💡 Did you know? Every square is a rhombus and a rectangle.

🔶Varignon's Result

Joining the midpoints of the sides of any quadrilateral, in order, always gives a parallelogram.

Example: Draw any four-sided shape and join its side midpoints — you get a parallelogram.

In real life: A neat surprise for geometry art.

💡 Did you know? This follows from the midpoint theorem.

Quick revision

  • Diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.
  • Midsegment = half of the third side.
  • Joining midpoints of any quadrilateral gives a parallelogram.

Practice questions

Q1. The segment joining midpoints of two sides of a triangle is

  1. Double the third side
  2. Parallel to the third side and half of it
  3. Equal to the third side
  4. Perpendicular to the third side
Show answerB) Parallel to the third side and half of it

Q2. If the third side is 10 cm, the midsegment is

  1. 10 cm
  2. 2.5 cm
  3. 20 cm
  4. 5 cm
Show answerD) 5 cm

Q3. The diagonals of a parallelogram

  1. Are always perpendicular
  2. Bisect each other
  3. Never meet
  4. Are always equal
Show answerB) Bisect each other

Q4. A parallelogram with equal diagonals is a

  1. Rectangle
  2. Kite
  3. Rhombus that is not a square
  4. Trapezium
Show answerA) Rectangle

Q5. A parallelogram with perpendicular diagonals is a

  1. Trapezium
  2. Kite only
  3. Rhombus
  4. Rectangle that is not a square
Show answerC) Rhombus
