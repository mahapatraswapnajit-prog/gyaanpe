Class 9 Maths Chapter 14: Math of Space: Surface Area and Volume

NCERT Ganita Manjari · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter finds surface areas and volumes of cuboids, cubes, cylinders, cones, spheres and hemispheres, and applies them to real problems of covering and filling.

Key concepts

📦Cuboid and Cube

Cuboid: volume = l × b × h; total surface area = 2(lb + bh + hl). Cube: volume = a³; surface area = 6a².

Example: Cuboid 5 × 4 × 3: volume 60. Cube of side 5: surface area 150.

In real life: Cartons and rooms are cuboids.

💡 Did you know? 1 litre = 1000 cm³.

🥫Cylinder

Volume = πr²h; curved surface area = 2πrh; total = 2πr(r + h).

Example: r = 7, h = 10 (π = 22/7): volume 1540, curved surface 440.

In real life: Water tanks and cans are cylinders.

💡 Did you know? A cylinder is like a stack of circles.

🍦Cone

Volume = (1/3)πr²h; slant height l = square root of (r² + h²); curved surface area = πrl.

Example: r = 3, h = 4 → l = 5.

In real life: Ice-cream cones and tent tops.

💡 Did you know? A cone holds exactly one-third of the cylinder with the same base and height.

⚽Sphere and Hemisphere

Sphere: surface area = 4πr², volume = (4/3)πr³. Hemisphere: curved surface 2πr², volume (2/3)πr³.

Example: Sphere r = 7 (π = 22/7): surface area 616.

In real life: Balls, domes and planets.

💡 Did you know? Archimedes was proud of finding the sphere–cylinder volume ratio 2 : 3.

🏗️Real-World Use

Surface area tells how much material covers a shape; volume tells how much it holds.

Example: Paint needed = surface area; water stored = volume.

In real life: Packaging design balances surface area and volume.

💡 Did you know? Units: area in square units, volume in cubic units.

Key terms
