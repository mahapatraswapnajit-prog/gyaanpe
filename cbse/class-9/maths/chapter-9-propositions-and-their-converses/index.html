Class 9 Maths Chapter 9: Propositions and their Converses

NCERT Ganita Manjari · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter develops mathematical reasoning: propositions, their converses, counterexamples to disprove claims, and general proofs — including proof by contradiction — to establish truth.

Key concepts

📜Propositions

A proposition is a statement that is either true or false, not both.

Example: '7 is a prime number' is a true proposition. 'Is 7 big?' is not a proposition.

In real life: Clear statements avoid confusion in rules and laws.

💡 Did you know? Mathematics builds on clearly stated propositions.

🔁Converse

The converse of 'If P, then Q' is 'If Q, then P'. A true statement can have a false converse.

Example: 'If a number is divisible by 4, it is even' is true. Converse 'If even, then divisible by 4' is false (6).

In real life: 'If it rains, the ground is wet' — the converse need not hold.

💡 Did you know? When both a statement and its converse are true, we say 'P if and only if Q'.

❌Counterexamples

One counterexample is enough to show a general statement is false.

Example: 'All primes are odd' is false because 2 is prime and even.

In real life: Testing a rule with unusual cases.

💡 Did you know? Many examples cannot prove a general statement, but one counterexample can disprove it.

✅Proof

A proof shows a statement is true in every case using logical steps from accepted facts.

Example: Sum of two even numbers: 2m + 2n = 2(m + n), always even.

In real life: Engineers prove designs are safe, not just test a few.

💡 Did you know? Checking 100 cases is not a proof; a general argument is.

🔍Proof by Contradiction

Assume the statement is false and show this leads to something impossible.

Example: Used to prove the square root of 2 is irrational.

In real life: Ruling out an option by showing it leads to nonsense.

💡 Did you know? Also called 'reductio ad absurdum'.

Key terms

TermMeaning
PropositionStatement that is true or false
ConverseSwaps 'if' and 'then' parts
CounterexampleOne case that disproves a claim
ProofGeneral logical argument
If and only ifBoth a statement and its converse are true

Quick revision

  • The converse of 'If P then Q' is 'If Q then P'.
  • One counterexample disproves a general claim.
  • Examples alone do not prove a general statement.
