Class 9 Science Chapter 10: Sound Waves: Characteristics and Applications

NCERT Exploration · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter explains sound as a longitudinal wave, its frequency, wavelength, amplitude and speed, echoes and reverberation, the audible range, ultrasound and SONAR.

Key concepts

🔊Sound is a Wave

Sound is produced by vibrations and travels as a longitudinal wave of compressions and rarefactions through a medium.

Example: A plucked guitar string vibrates the air.

In real life: Astronauts use radios in space.

💡 Did you know? Sound cannot travel through a vacuum.

📏Wave Properties

Wavelength (λ) is the distance between two compressions; frequency (f) is vibrations per second, in hertz; amplitude decides loudness; frequency decides pitch. Speed v = f × λ.

Example: f = 500 Hz, λ = 0.68 m → v = 340 m/s.

In real life: Music apps show frequencies.

💡 Did you know? A whistle has high pitch; a drum low pitch.

🏃Speed of Sound

Sound travels faster in solids than liquids, and faster in liquids than gases. In air it is about 340 m/s, and it increases with temperature.

Example: Thunder is heard after lightning is seen.

In real life: Railway workers once listened to tracks for trains.

💡 Did you know? Sound is about 4 times faster in water than in air.

🗣️Echo and Reverberation

An echo is a reflected sound heard separately. It needs the reflector at least about 17 m away. Repeated reflections causing persistence of sound is reverberation.

Example: Clap near a large hill or well.

In real life: Stethoscopes guide sound by reflection.

💡 Did you know? Auditoriums use soft panels to reduce reverberation.

🦇Ultrasound and SONAR

Humans hear 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz. Above 20 kHz is ultrasound. SONAR uses ultrasound echoes to find depth: distance = speed × time ÷ 2.

Example: Echo returns in 2 s at 1500 m/s → depth 1500 m.

In real life: Ultrasound scans check organs safely.

💡 Did you know? Bats and dolphins use ultrasound to navigate.

Quick revision

  • Audible range: 20 Hz – 20 kHz.
  • Speed of sound in air ≈ 340 m/s.
  • Sound cannot travel in a vacuum.
