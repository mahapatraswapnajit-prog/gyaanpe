Class 9 Science Chapter 12: Patterns in Life: Diversity and Classification

NCERT Exploration · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter shows why living things are classified, explains binomial nomenclature, the hierarchy of classification, Whittaker's five kingdoms and the three domains, and major groups of plants and vertebrates.

Key concepts

🗂️Why Classify?

Classification groups organisms by shared features, making the huge variety of life easier to study and showing how groups are related.

Example: Animals with backbones are grouped as vertebrates.

In real life: Libraries classify books the same way.

💡 Did you know? Scientists have named nearly 2 million species so far.

🏷️Binomial Nomenclature

Carl Linnaeus gave each species a two-part scientific name: genus (capital) and species (small), written in italics.

Example: Humans: Homo sapiens; mango: Mangifera indica.

In real life: Doctors and vets use scientific names to avoid confusion.

💡 Did you know? Scientific names are the same in every language.

🪜Hierarchy

Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species. Species is the basic unit.

Example: Lion and tiger share the genus Panthera.

In real life: Family trees work like this hierarchy.

💡 Did you know? Members of one species can interbreed.

🌍Five Kingdoms

Whittaker's five kingdoms: Monera (bacteria), Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia. Scientists today also use three domains: Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya.

Example: Mushrooms are fungi, not plants.

In real life: Yeast (fungus) is used in baking.

💡 Did you know? Fungi absorb food; they cannot make it.

🌳Plants and Animals

Plants: thallophytes, bryophytes, pteridophytes, gymnosperms, angiosperms. Vertebrates: fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, mammals.

Example: Ferns are pteridophytes; pine is a gymnosperm.

In real life: Bats are mammals, not birds.

💡 Did you know? Mosses are called the amphibians of the plant kingdom.
