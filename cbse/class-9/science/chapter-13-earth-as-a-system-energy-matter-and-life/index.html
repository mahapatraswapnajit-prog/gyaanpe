Class 9 Science Chapter 13: Earth as a System: Energy, Matter, and Life

NCERT Exploration · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter views Earth as linked spheres, traces energy flow through food chains, explains the water, carbon and nitrogen cycles, and describes the greenhouse effect and the ozone layer.

Key concepts

🌎Four Spheres

Earth's systems: atmosphere (air), hydrosphere (water), lithosphere (rock and soil) and biosphere (life). They constantly interact.

Example: Rain (hydrosphere) erodes rock (lithosphere).

In real life: Floods show how spheres affect each other.

💡 Did you know? About 71% of Earth's surface is covered by water.

☀️Energy Flow

The Sun is the main source of energy. Producers capture it; consumers eat producers; decomposers recycle dead matter. Only about 10% of energy passes to the next level.

Example: Grass → deer → tiger.

In real life: Eating lower on the food chain needs less land.

💡 Did you know? Energy flows one way; it is not recycled.

💧Water Cycle

Evaporation, condensation, precipitation and collection cycle water between land, sea and air.

Example: Plants release water vapour by transpiration.

In real life: Monsoon rains are part of this cycle.

💡 Did you know? Most fresh water is locked in ice.

🌿Carbon and Nitrogen Cycles

Photosynthesis takes in CO₂; respiration and burning release it. Nitrogen-fixing bacteria in legume root nodules turn air nitrogen into usable compounds.

Example: Farmers grow pulses to enrich soil with nitrogen.

In real life: Burning fossil fuels adds extra CO₂.

💡 Did you know? Nitrogen makes up about 78% of air.

🛡️Greenhouse Effect and Ozone

Greenhouse gases like CO₂ and methane trap heat and keep Earth warm; too much causes global warming. The ozone layer absorbs harmful UV rays; CFCs damage it.

Example: Without any greenhouse effect Earth would be freezing.

In real life: Sunscreen protects skin from UV.

💡 Did you know? The Montreal Protocol reduced CFC use.
