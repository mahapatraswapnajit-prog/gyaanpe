Class 9 Science Chapter 7: Work, Energy, and Simple Machines

NCERT Exploration · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter defines work, kinetic and potential energy, conservation of energy and power, and explains how simple machines such as levers, pulleys and inclined planes give mechanical advantage.

Key concepts

🏋️Work

Work is done when a force moves an object in its direction: W = F × s. Unit: joule (J).

Example: A 10 N force moving a box 5 m does 50 J of work.

In real life: Lifting goods onto a truck needs work.

💡 Did you know? Holding a heavy bag still does no work in physics.

🏃Kinetic and Potential Energy

Kinetic energy KE = ½mv² is energy of motion. Potential energy PE = mgh is energy due to height.

Example: 2 kg at 3 m/s has KE = 9 J. 2 kg at 5 m height has PE = 2 × 10 × 5 = 100 J (g = 10 m/s²).

In real life: Dams store water's potential energy.

💡 Did you know? Doubling speed makes KE four times larger.

🔄Conservation of Energy

Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only changed from one form to another.

Example: A falling ball changes PE into KE.

In real life: Hydropower converts PE of water into electricity.

💡 Did you know? Some energy always turns into heat due to friction.

⚡Power

Power = work ÷ time; unit watt (W). 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J is the commercial unit of energy.

Example: 1000 J in 10 s → 100 W.

In real life: Electricity bills count 'units' = kWh.

💡 Did you know? 1 horsepower ≈ 746 W.

🔧Simple Machines

Levers, pulleys and inclined planes make work easier. Mechanical advantage = load ÷ effort. A lever balances when effort × effort arm = load × load arm.

Example: Load 200 N lifted with effort 50 N → MA = 4.

In real life: Ramps help wheelchairs go up steps.

💡 Did you know? A single fixed pulley only changes the direction of force.
