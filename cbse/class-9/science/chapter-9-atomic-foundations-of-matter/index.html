Class 9 Science Chapter 9: Atomic Foundations of Matter

NCERT Exploration · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter covers the laws of chemical combination, Dalton's atomic theory, symbols and formulae, atomic and molecular masses, ions and the mole concept.

Key concepts

⚖️Laws of Chemical Combination

Law of conservation of mass: mass is neither created nor destroyed in a reaction. Law of constant proportions: a compound always has elements in a fixed mass ratio.

Example: Water always has hydrogen : oxygen = 1 : 8 by mass.

In real life: Recipes for medicines rely on fixed ratios.

💡 Did you know? Lavoisier and Proust gave these laws.

⚛️Dalton's Atomic Theory

Dalton proposed that matter is made of tiny indivisible atoms; atoms of one element are identical; atoms combine in small whole-number ratios.

Example: H₂O: 2 hydrogen atoms + 1 oxygen atom.

In real life: Later discoveries showed atoms are divisible.

💡 Did you know? Kanad, an ancient Indian thinker, also spoke of tiny particles called 'parmanu'.

🔤Symbols and Formulae

Each element has a symbol (O, Na, Fe). A formula shows the atoms in a molecule or compound, written by crossing valencies.

Example: Al³⁺ and O²⁻ give Al₂O₃.

In real life: Chemical names are common across the world.

💡 Did you know? Na comes from Latin 'natrium'.

🧮Atomic and Molecular Mass

Masses are measured in atomic mass units (u). Molecular mass = sum of atomic masses.

Example: H₂O = 2 × 1 + 16 = 18 u; CO₂ = 12 + 2 × 16 = 44 u.

In real life: Chemists weigh reactants using molar masses.

💡 Did you know? 1 u is 1/12 the mass of a carbon-12 atom.

🥚The Mole

One mole contains 6.022 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro number). Molar mass in grams equals the molecular mass in u.

Example: 18 g of water = 1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ molecules.

In real life: Pharmacies dose some medicines by moles.

💡 Did you know? A mole is like a 'dozen' for tiny particles.
