Class 9 Social Science Chapter 2: Shaping of the Earth's Surface

NCERT Understanding Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter explains the Earth's interior, plate movement, internal forces that cause earthquakes, volcanoes and mountains, and how rivers, glaciers, wind and waves shape landforms.

Key concepts

🌍Inside the Earth

The Earth has three main layers: the thin crust, the thick mantle and the very hot core (outer liquid, inner solid).

Example: The crust under oceans is thinner than under continents.

In real life: Earthquake waves help scientists study the interior.

💡 Did you know? The core is roughly as hot as the Sun's surface.

🧩Plate Tectonics

The crust and upper mantle are broken into large plates that move slowly. Where they collide, mountains form; where they move apart, new crust forms.

Example: The Himalayas rose when the Indian plate pushed into the Eurasian plate.

In real life: Plate edges have more earthquakes.

💡 Did you know? The Himalayas are still rising by a few millimetres a year.

🌋Endogenic Forces

Forces from inside the Earth cause folding, faulting, earthquakes and volcanoes. Sudden ones cause disasters; slow ones build mountains.

Example: Barren Island in the Andaman Sea is India's only active volcano.

In real life: Earthquake-safe buildings save lives.

💡 Did you know? The point on the surface above an earthquake's origin is the epicentre.

🌊Rivers at Work

Exogenic (surface) forces like rivers wear down and build land. Rivers cut V-shaped valleys, make waterfalls and meanders, form oxbow lakes, and build deltas at the sea.

Example: The Ganga–Brahmaputra delta is among the largest in the world.

In real life: Fertile plains are made by river deposits.

💡 Did you know? Weathering breaks rock; erosion carries it away.

🏜️Ice, Wind and Waves

Glaciers carve U-shaped valleys; wind forms sand dunes and mushroom rocks; sea waves make cliffs, caves, arches and beaches.

Example: Thar Desert has many sand dunes.

In real life: Beach tourism depends on wave-built beaches.

💡 Did you know? Glacier-made deposits are called moraines.

Key terms

TermMeaning
CrustOutermost thin layer of the Earth
PlateLarge moving piece of the Earth's surface

ErosionWearing away and carrying of material

Quick revision

  • The Himalayas formed from the collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates.
  • Rivers make V-shaped valleys; glaciers make U-shaped valleys.
  • Barren Island is India's only active volcano.

  2. Crust
  3. Core
