Class 9 Social Science Chapter 6: Democracy

NCERT Understanding Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter explains what democracy is, its key features, direct and representative forms, the making of India's democracy and the arguments for democracy along with its challenges.

Key concepts

🗳️What is Democracy?

Democracy is a form of government in which rulers are chosen by the people. Abraham Lincoln described it as government of the people, by the people, for the people.

Example: India, the USA and Japan are democracies.

In real life: Your class monitor election is a small democracy.

💡 Did you know? The word comes from Greek 'demos' (people) and 'kratos' (rule).

📋Key Features

Major decisions by elected representatives; free and fair elections; one person, one vote, one value; rule of law; respect for rights.

Example: Every adult citizen aged 18+ can vote in India.

In real life: Courts protect citizens' rights.

💡 Did you know? Regular elections let people change rulers peacefully.

🏛️Direct and Representative

In direct democracy all citizens decide together; in representative democracy people elect representatives to decide for them. Large countries use representative democracy.

Example: Gram Sabha meetings are close to direct democracy.

In real life: MLAs and MPs are our representatives.

💡 Did you know? Ancient Athens had a direct assembly.

📜India's Democracy

India adopted its Constitution on 26 November 1949; it came into force on 26 January 1950. India is the world's largest democracy.

Example: 26 January is Republic Day.

In real life: Constitution Day is celebrated on 26 November.

💡 Did you know? Universal adult franchise was given from the very beginning.

⚖️Why Democracy?

Democracy improves decision-making through discussion, protects dignity and equality, allows mistakes to be corrected and gives a peaceful way to settle conflicts. It also faces challenges like inequality and misuse of power.

Example: Free press questions the government.

In real life: Peaceful protests are a democratic right.

💡 Did you know? Democracy works best with informed, active citizens.
