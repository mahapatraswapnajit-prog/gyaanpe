Class 9 Social Science Chapter 7: Elections

NCERT Understanding Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter explains why elections matter, the role of the Election Commission, the first-past-the-post and proportional representation systems, and lessons from the Emergency of 1975–77.

Key concepts

🗳️Why Elections?

Elections let people choose representatives, form governments and remove them peacefully. They must be regular, free and fair.

Example: Lok Sabha elections are held at least every 5 years.

In real life: Higher turnout makes results more representative.

💡 Did you know? A secret ballot protects voters from pressure.

🏛️Election Commission

The Election Commission of India is an independent constitutional body that conducts elections to Parliament, state legislatures and the offices of President and Vice-President. It enforces the Model Code of Conduct.

Example: EVMs with VVPAT slips are used for voting.

In real life: Indelible ink on the finger prevents double voting.

💡 Did you know? NOTA ('None of the Above') has been on ballots since 2013.

🥇First Past the Post

In FPTP, the country is divided into constituencies; the candidate with the most votes in each wins, even without a majority. India uses FPTP for Lok Sabha and Vidhan Sabha elections.

Example: Lok Sabha has 543 elected seats.

In real life: A party can win many seats with under half the votes.

💡 Did you know? Some seats are reserved for SC and ST candidates.

📊Proportional Representation

In PR, parties get seats in proportion to their share of votes. India uses a form of PR (single transferable vote) to elect the President and Rajya Sabha members.

Example: A party with 30% votes gets about 30% seats.

In real life: PR gives small parties a voice.

💡 Did you know? Many European countries use PR.

⚠️The Emergency (1975–77)

A national Emergency was declared in June 1975. Elections were postponed, civil liberties were curtailed and the press was censored. Elections in 1977 brought a change of government, and later amendments added safeguards.

Example: The 44th Amendment made declaring an Emergency harder.

In real life: Citizens' vigilance protects democracy.

💡 Did you know? It reminds us why free elections and rights matter.
