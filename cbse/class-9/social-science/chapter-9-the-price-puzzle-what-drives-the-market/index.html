Class 9 Social Science Chapter 9: The Price Puzzle: What Drives the Market

NCERT Understanding Society: India and Beyond · Simple notes, key terms and practice questions

The chapter explains markets, the laws of demand and supply, how equilibrium price is reached, surplus and shortage, and how governments influence prices through MSP and the public distribution system.

Key concepts

🛒What is a Market?

A market is any arrangement where buyers and sellers exchange goods or services — a haat, a mall or an online app.

Example: Online shopping is also a market.

In real life: Weekly haats are common in Odisha villages.

💡 Did you know? Prices carry information between buyers and sellers.

📉Law of Demand

Other things remaining the same, when price rises, quantity demanded falls; when price falls, quantity demanded rises.

Example: Cheaper mangoes in season → people buy more.

In real life: Sales and discounts use the law of demand.

💡 Did you know? Demand also depends on income, tastes and prices of substitutes.

📈Law of Supply

Other things remaining the same, when price rises, sellers supply more; when price falls, they supply less.

Example: High onion prices encourage farmers to grow more onions.

In real life: A bad monsoon can reduce supply of crops.

💡 Did you know? Supply depends on costs, technology and weather.

⚖️Equilibrium Price

The market price settles where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. If price is too high there is a surplus; if too low, a shortage.

Example: Tomato prices crash when harvest floods the market (surplus).

In real life: Festival-time flower prices rise due to high demand.

💡 Did you know? Prices keep adjusting towards equilibrium.

🏛️Government and Prices

Governments sometimes step in: Minimum Support Price (MSP) protects farmers; price ceilings and the public distribution system help consumers.

Example: Ration shops sell grain at lower prices.

In real life: Fuel taxes affect prices everyone pays.

💡 Did you know? Very high general price rise is called inflation.

Key terms

Quick revision

  • Price up → demand down.
  • Price up → supply up.
  • At equilibrium, demand equals supply.
